Cockpit glossary

The words you meet in the lessons, in plain English. In the trainer, any word with a dotted underline shows its meaning when you point at it or tap it.

A/T B737
Autothrottle. The computer moves the thrust levers to hold a speed or a power setting.
A/THR A320
Autothrust. The computer moves the engine thrust to hold the speed.
ADIRS A320
Air data and inertial reference system. Three units that measure speed, altitude, attitude and position. They must align on the ground before flight.
Alternator C172, PA-28
An engine-driven generator in a light aircraft. It charges the battery and powers the electrics once the engine runs.
APU A320, B737
Auxiliary power unit. A small jet engine in the tail that gives electrical power and compressed air on the ground, without the main engines.
ATC
Air traffic control. The people on the radio who give clearances and keep aircraft apart.
ATIS
Automatic terminal information service. A recorded radio message with the runway in use, wind, weather and QNH. Pilots listen to it before calling ATC.
Autobrake also AUTO BRK, AUTO BRAKE
Brakes automatically after touchdown, or at full power on a rejected take-off.
AVAIL
Available. Shown when the APU or ground power is ready to use.
Avionics
The aircraft's radios, navigation and display electronics.
Base also base leg C172
The circuit leg flown across the approach path, before the last turn onto final.
Best glide also best glide speed C172, PA-28
The speed that gives the longest glide with no engine power. About 68 knots in the light aircraft here.
Bleed air
Hot compressed air taken from an engine or the APU. It is used for air conditioning, anti-ice and starting the engines.
Bus also AC bus, AC buses
An electrical bus is a main power line that many items connect to. Generators and batteries feed the buses.
CDU B737
Control display unit. The keyboard and screen used to talk to the FMC.
Circuit C172
A rectangle flown around the runway: take-off, crosswind, downwind, base, final and landing. Used to practise take-offs and landings.
Clearance
Permission from ATC, for example to fly a route or climb to an altitude. You read it back so ATC can check you heard it right.
Cold and dark
An aircraft with everything switched off: no power, no lights, no engines. The start of a power up lesson.
Cost index
A number in the flight computer that balances fuel against time. Low saves fuel, high saves time.
Crossfeed also X FEED
A valve that lets one fuel side feed both engines, used to balance the fuel.
Crosswind also crosswind leg C172
In the circuit, the leg flown at right angles to the runway after take-off. (A crosswind is also wind blowing across the runway.)
CWS B737
Control wheel steering. On the B737, autopilot on with no mode chosen: it holds the attitude and keeps the wings level.
DME
Distance measuring equipment. Shows how many nautical miles you are from a radio station.
Downwind also downwind leg C172
The circuit leg parallel to the runway, flown the opposite way to the landing.
ECAM A320
Electronic centralised aircraft monitor. The two centre screens on the A320 that show engines, system pages, warnings and what to do about them.
EFIS
Electronic flight instrument system. The PFD and ND, and the small panel that controls them (baro, range, map mode).
EGT
Exhaust gas temperature. How hot the engine's exhaust is. It rises when the fuel lights during a start and must stay below a limit.
FCU A320
Flight control unit. The A320's autopilot panel on the glareshield: speed, heading, altitude and vertical speed.
Final also final approach C172
The last part of the approach, lined up with the runway.
Flare
Raising the nose just above the runway to stop the descent, so the aircraft touches down gently.
Flight director also FD, F/D
Bars on the PFD that show where to point the aircraft to follow the selected autopilot modes, even when flying by hand.
Flight level also FL
Altitude in hundreds of feet on the standard pressure setting (1013 hPa). FL350 is 35,000 feet.
FLX also FLEX A320
Reduced take-off thrust, used when the runway is long enough. It saves wear on the engines.
FMA
Flight mode annunciator. The row at the top of the PFD that shows what the autopilot and autothrust are doing right now.
FMC B737
Flight management computer. Holds the route and performance data, and guides the autopilot along the route.
Glide slope also G/S
The part of an ILS that shows the descent path down to the runway, usually 3 degrees.
Ground spoilers A320
Panels on top of the wing that pop up at touchdown to kill lift and help braking.
Holding point
Yellow lines across a taxiway before a runway. Stop there until cleared onto the runway.
HSI C172, PA-28
Horizontal situation indicator. The compass at the bottom of a glass PFD, with the course needle for a VOR or ILS.
IAS
Indicated airspeed. The speed shown on the speed tape, in knots.
Ident also identify
A radio station's short Morse code name. Pilots listen to it to check they tuned the right station.
Igniters
Spark plugs in a jet engine that light the fuel during a start.
ILS
Instrument landing system. Radio beams that guide an aircraft to the runway: the localizer for left and right, the glide slope for up and down.
IRS B737
Inertial reference system. Gyros and accelerometers that work out position and attitude. They must align before the aircraft moves.
Knots also kt
Nautical miles per hour, the unit for aircraft speed. 100 knots is about 185 km/h.
LNAV B737
Lateral navigation. The autopilot follows the FMC route left and right, waypoint by waypoint.
Localizer also LOC
The part of an ILS that shows the runway centreline, left or right.
LVL CHG B737
Level change. A B737 mode that climbs at climb power or descends at idle, holding the speed with pitch.
Magneto also magnetos C172, PA-28
A small engine-driven generator that makes the sparks for a piston engine. There are two, so one can fail safely.
MCDU A320
Multipurpose control and display unit. The A320's keyboard and screen for the flight management computer.
MCP B737
Mode control panel. The B737's autopilot panel on the glareshield.
MFD C172, PA-28
Multi-function display. In a light aircraft glass cockpit, the screen with the engine readings and the map.
Mixture C172, PA-28
The control that sets how much fuel goes with the air into a piston engine. Fully out (idle cut-off) stops the engine.
N1
Speed of a jet engine's fan, in percent. The main thrust reading.
N2
Speed of a jet engine's core (high-pressure spool), in percent. Watched closely during engine start.
ND
Navigation display. The map screen: route, waypoints, heading, and VOR or ILS information.
NM also nautical miles, nautical mile
Nautical mile, about 1.85 km. Used for distances in aviation.
Pack also packs, PACKS
An air conditioning pack. It cools and pressurises the cabin using bleed air.
PAPI
Precision approach path indicator. Four lights beside the runway: two white and two red means you are on the right slope. More white is high, more red is low.
PFD
Primary flight display. The main flying screen: speed, attitude, altitude, vertical speed and heading.
Pitot
A small forward-facing tube that measures airspeed. It is heated so ice cannot block it.
Primer PA-28
A hand pump that sprays a little fuel straight into the cylinders for a cold start.
PTU A320
Power transfer unit. Moves hydraulic power between the A320's green and yellow systems. It makes a barking sound.
QNH
The local air pressure setting. With QNH set, the altimeter shows height above sea level. ATC and the ATIS give it.
Radial also radials
A line going out from a VOR station, named by its direction. On the 090 radial you are east of the station.
Read-back also read back, readback
Repeating ATC's important words (route, altitude, squawk) so they can check you heard right.
RPM
Revolutions per minute. How fast a piston engine (and its propeller) turns.
Run-up C172, PA-28
Engine checks done on the ground before take-off in a piston aircraft, including the magneto check.
Squawk
The four-digit code you set in the transponder so ATC radar can identify you. Digits 0 to 7 only.
Stall also stall horn
When the wing meets the air at too steep an angle and stops lifting properly. The stall horn warns a few knots before. Lower the nose to recover.
Starvation also starved
Fuel starvation: fuel is on board, but none reaches the engine, for example because the selected tank is empty.
STD
Standard pressure, 1013 hPa. Set above the transition altitude so every aircraft reads the same flight levels.
T.O CONFIG A320
A test button that checks the A320 is set up for take-off and warns if it is not.
TA/RA B737
A transponder mode that also gives traffic advisories (TA) and resolution advisories (RA) to avoid other aircraft.
Threshold
The start of the usable runway for landing, marked with white stripes.
THS A320
Trimmable horizontal stabiliser. The A320's pitch trim, set for take-off from the PERF page.
TOGA A320
Take-off / go-around power. The highest thrust setting.
Transition altitude
The altitude where pilots change the altimeter from QNH to standard (1013) in the climb.
Transponder also XPDR
A radio box that answers ATC radar with your squawk code and altitude.
Trim
A small control that takes the pressure off the yoke or stick, so the aircraft holds its attitude by itself.
V/S
Vertical speed. How fast the aircraft climbs or descends, in feet per minute. Also an autopilot mode.
V1
Take-off decision speed. Below it you can still stop on the runway, above it you continue the take-off.
V2
Take-off safety speed. The speed to climb at if an engine fails after V1.
VNAV B737
Vertical navigation. The FMC controls the climb or descent and the speed.
VOR
VHF omnidirectional range. A ground radio station. The needle shows which radial you are on and the course to or from the station.
VR
Rotation speed. The speed to pull back and lift the nose for take-off.
Waypoint also waypoints
A named point on a route. In Runway Zero, RZ101, RZ102 and RZ103 are made-up training waypoints.
Yoke C172, PA-28
The control wheel. Pull back for nose up, push for nose down, turn to bank.
ZFW
Zero fuel weight. The aircraft with passengers and cargo but no fuel, in tonnes.

These are simplified explanations written for Runway Zero, for learning only. They are not taken from any aircraft manual. Spotted a mistake or a missing word? Tell us here.
