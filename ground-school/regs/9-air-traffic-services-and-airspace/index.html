What air traffic services are for and who gives them: area, approach and aerodrome control, flight information service, ATIS and alerting with its three phases. Then the airspace: classes A to G and how India uses them, control zones and control areas, FIRs and location indicators, route and point names, and special use airspace.

What ATS is for

  • Air traffic services are in Annex 11. The detailed procedures are in PANS-ATM, Doc 4444. In India ATS is given by the Airports Authority of India.
  • Objectives: prevent collisions between aircraft, prevent collisions on the manoeuvring area with obstructions, expedite and keep an orderly flow of traffic, give advice and information for safe flights, and alert the right people when an aircraft needs search and rescue.

Airspace classes

Classes A to E are controlled airspace. F and G are uncontrolled. The 250 kt limit below FL 100 applies to all flights in D, E, F and G, and to VFR in C.
ClassFlights allowedSeparationNotes
AIFR onlyAll flights separatedClearance and two-way radio
BIFR and VFRAll flights separated from each otherClearance and two-way radio
CIFR and VFRIFR from IFR and from VFR. VFR from IFRVFR gets traffic information on other VFR. VFR 250 kt below FL 100
DIFR and VFRIFR from IFR onlyIFR gets traffic information on VFR. VFR gets it on all. 250 kt below FL 100
EIFR and VFRIFR from IFRVFR needs no clearance or radio. Not used for control zones
FIFR and VFRIFR from IFR as far as practicalAdvisory service for IFR, FIS for all on request
GIFR and VFRNoneFlight information service on request
  • Where two classes meet one above the other, a flight at the common level follows the less restrictive class. B is less restrictive than A, C less than B, and so on.
  • In India, Classes A and B are not in use at present.
  • Class C: around busy airports with radar and a runway capacity of more than 20 movements an hour, from 4,000 ft to FL 285. The airspace within 30 nm of these airports below that is Class D. VFR flights need a pre-departure clearance to enter Class C.
  • Class D: other control zones, terminal areas and control areas. Class E: ATS routes outside controlled airspace. Class F: advisory routes and areas. Class G: the rest.

Memory trick

Read down the table and the service gets thinner: A and B separate everyone, C separates IFR from everyone, D and E only IFR from IFR, F only advises, G only informs.

Control zones, control areas and traffic zones

AirspaceWhat it isLimits

Control area (CTA)Controlled airspace from a stated limit above the surfaceLower limit at least 700 ft (200 m) above ground or water
Terminal control area (TMA)A control area where several ATS routes meet, near one or more major aerodromesSet by the AIP
Airway (AWY)A control area in the form of a corridorTakes the class of the airspace around it
Aerodrome traffic zone (ATZ)Airspace around an aerodrome to protect aerodrome trafficSet by the AIP
  • An aerodrome where ATC is given to aerodrome traffic is a controlled aerodrome. It need not be inside a control zone.
  • Aircraft holding near an aerodrome count as arriving aircraft.

FIRs, location indicators and names

  • A Flight Information Region (FIR) is airspace in which flight information and alerting services are given. India has the Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai FIRs, with Guwahati as a sub-FIR.
  • A location indicator has four letters. The first is the region (V for India and nearby States). The second is the FIR or State. The last two are the place.
  • In India the second letter is I for Delhi, A for Mumbai, E for Kolkata and Guwahati, and O for Chennai. So VIDP is Delhi, VABB Mumbai, VECC Kolkata, VOMM Chennai.
  • An FIR's own code ends in F: VIDF, VABF, VECF, VOMF.

Flight information service and ATIS

  • FIS goes to all aircraft that may be affected and that are under ATC or otherwise known to ATS.
  • It includes SIGMET information, volcanic ash, release of radioactive or toxic material, changes in navaid serviceability, changes in aerodrome conditions, unmanned free balloons, weather at departure, destination and alternates, and collision hazards in Classes C to G. AIRMET is not issued in India.
  • FIS does not separate traffic. The pilot stays responsible.
  • ATIS (Automatic Terminal Information Service) is a continuous recorded broadcast of current aerodrome information at busy aerodromes, to cut radio load.
  • Each ATIS has a letter (Information Alpha, Bravo...) that changes with each update. Pilots confirm the letter on first contact.
  • ATIS goes out on a separate VHF frequency or the voice channel of a VOR, never on an ILS. A broadcast should not be longer than 30 seconds where possible.
  • ATIS is updated at once when there is a significant change, and otherwise with each routine weather report.

Alerting service

PhaseCode wordWhen
UncertaintyINCERFANo contact for 30 minutes after a report was due or after the first failed attempt to call, or the aircraft has not arrived within 30 minutes of its ETA
AlertALERFACalls during the uncertainty phase fail, or an aircraft cleared to land does not land within 5 minutes and cannot be called, or there are reports that its operating efficiency is impaired, or it is under unlawful interference
DistressDETRESFAFurther calls fail and it is likely the aircraft is in distress, its fuel is thought to be exhausted, or it is reported to be about to make, or has made, a forced landing

The ATS unit tells the Rescue Coordination Centre (RCC), and also tells the operator where it can. In India the RCCs are at Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai, run by the AAI.

Memory trick

INCERFA, ALERFA, DETRESFA: uncertain, alarmed, in distress. 30 minutes starts it, 5 minutes after a landing clearance jumps straight to alert.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • ATS: Annex 11, PANS-ATM Doc 4444. In India, the AAI.
  • ATC: area (ACC), approach, aerodrome (tower). Plus FIS, alerting, advisory.
  • Class A: IFR only. B: all separated. C: IFR separated from all. D: IFR from IFR. E: VFR needs no clearance. F: advisory. G: information.
  • 250 kt below FL 100 in C (VFR), D, E, F, G.
  • India: A and B not used. C from 4,000 ft to FL 285 at busy radar airports, D within 30 nm.
  • CTR: from the surface, at least 5 nm out. CTA: from at least 700 ft.
  • Class E is never used for a control zone.
  • Location indicators: V region. Delhi I, Mumbai A, Kolkata E, Chennai O. FIR codes end in F.
  • NDB 2 or 3 letters, VOR 3, ILS 4 starting I, points 5 letters.
  • TSA: no transit. TRA: transit with clearance.
  • ATIS: VHF or VOR, never ILS. About 30 seconds. New letter each update.
  • INCERFA 30 min. ALERFA: not landed 5 min after landing clearance. DETRESFA: fuel gone, forced landing.

Sample questions

5 of the 33 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The ICAO Annex for Air Traffic Services is:

    1. Annex 2
    2. Annex 10
    3. Annex 11
    4. Annex 15
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Annex 11. Annex 10 is telecommunications, Annex 15 is AIS.

  2. Q2. In Class C airspace, VFR flights are separated from:

    1. Other VFR flights
    2. IFR flights
    3. All flights
    4. No one
    Show answer

    Answer: B. VFR is separated from IFR and gets traffic information on other VFR.

  3. Q3. The lateral limit of a control zone must extend at least how far from the aerodrome in the approach directions?

    1. 2 nm
    2. 5 nm (9.3 km)
    3. 10 nm
    4. 20 nm
    Show answer

    Answer: B. 5 nm, which is 9.3 km.

  4. Q4. An ILS identifier has:

    1. 2 letters
    2. 3 letters
    3. 4 letters starting with I
    4. 5 letters
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Four letters starting with I, such as IGHT.

  5. Q5. Flight information service:

    1. Separates traffic
    2. Is given only in Class A
    3. Is given only to IFR flights
    4. Gives advice and information useful for safe flight, but does not separate traffic
    Show answer

    Answer: D. FIS informs. It does not control.
