Nationality and registration marks (Annex 7)

  • Every aircraft carries a nationality mark (which country) followed by a registration mark (which aircraft). India: VT- then three letters, such as VT-RZA.
  • The nationality mark is chosen from the radio call sign series that the ITU (International Telecommunication Union) gives to each State.
  • The registration mark is given by the State of Registry (or by a common mark registering authority). It may be letters, numbers or both, 1 to 5 characters.
  • Common marks: a group of States that runs aircraft jointly (such as an international operating agency) can use a common mark, taken from the series the ITU gives to ICAO (4YA to 4YZ).
  • Letters must be Roman capitals without ornament. Numbers are Arabic numerals.

Combinations not allowed as registration marks, because they could be mistaken for signals: five letter groups of the International Code of Signals, three letter groups starting with Q (the Q code), and SOS, PAN, XXX and TTT or other distress, urgency and safety signals.

All letters and numbers in one group are the same height. Marks are painted on, or fixed just as permanently, and kept clean and visible.

Identification plate: every aircraft carries a plate with at least its nationality and registration marks, made of fireproof metal or other fireproof material, fixed in a prominent place near the main entrance. In India the plate also carries the owner's name and address.

Registration in India

Old exam answer

Rule numbers below are from the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026, in force from 1 January 2027, with the old Aircraft Rules, 1937 number in brackets. Older question banks use the old numbers. The new rules keep the two registration categories, and add IFSC leasing companies to Category A.

  • The ITU series for India is AT to AW, but India still uses its pre-independence mark VT for civil aircraft. VU is used for Indian Air Force aircraft.
  • The Central Government (DGCA) registers aircraft and grants the certificate of registration.
  • Category A (Rule 39 (4), old Rule 30): wholly or partly owned by Indian citizens, an Indian company with its main business in India, a company registered with the International Financial Services Centre (IFSC) to lease aircraft, the Central or a State Government or their companies, or a foreign company that has leased it to one of these. Category B: owned by foreigners resident or doing business in India, or a foreign company doing business in India. Only Category A aircraft may be used for public transport or aerial work (Rule 186).
  • A registration mark can be reserved with the DGCA for 1 year, and the reservation can be extended a year at a time.
  • The certificate of registration shows the type, constructor's number, year of manufacture, the marks, the owner's name, nationality and address, where the aircraft is usually kept, and the date of registration. For a leased aircraft it also names the lessor and lessee and the period of the lease.
  • A registration is valid until it is cancelled by the DGCA. A change of ownership must be reported to the DGCA at once, and the new owner applies for a new certificate (Rule 42, old 33).
  • The DGCA keeps a register of aircraft, open to the public and on its website.
The DGCA may cancel a registration if
It was not made according to the rules, or was obtained by false information
It would be better registered in another country
The aircraft is destroyed or permanently withdrawn from use
Keeping it registered in India is not in the public interest
The lease has ended (expired, ended by agreement, or ended under its terms)
The certificate of airworthiness has been expired for 5 years or more
  • State marks (Rule 48, old 37A): no advertising on the outside except as allowed by the DGCA. The aircraft's name and the owner's name and emblem may be shown if they cannot be confused with the marks. A civil aircraft may not carry marks meant for State (military) aircraft. The national flag may be shown if it cannot be confused with military markings.
  • Leasing companies in GIFT City (Gandhinagar), registered with the IFSC Authority, may lease aircraft to Indian operators.

Airworthiness (Annex 8)

Airworthy means the aircraft, engine, propeller or part conforms to its approved design and is in a condition for safe operation. Annex 8 looks at airworthiness from the engineering side. Annex 6 (operations) looks at it from the operating side, and the two are written to fit together.

Certificate or taskWhoWhat it means
Type CertificateState of DesignThe design of the aircraft type (or engine or propeller) meets the airworthiness requirements
Certificate of Airworthiness (CofA)State of RegistryThis particular aircraft is airworthy. Other ICAO States recognise it if it meets ICAO minimum standards
Continuing airworthinessState of RegistryDecides whether the CofA stays valid or is renewed, even after the aircraft is damaged in another State
  • Annex 8's detailed technical standards for certifying aeroplanes apply to multi-engined aeroplanes over 5,700 kg maximum certificated take-off mass. Annex 8 also has parts for helicopters and for small aeroplanes. Guidance is in the Airworthiness Manual (Doc 9760).
  • If an aircraft is damaged, the State of Registry judges whether it is still airworthy and may let it fly to a repair base.
  • Modern designs must have security features: a strengthened cockpit door, stronger cabin floors and ceilings, and a least-risk bomb location where a suspect device can be placed.

Airworthiness in India

  • No aircraft may fly without a valid CofA (or special CofA) issued by the DGCA, kept on board, with its conditions met (Rule 11, old 15). A special flight permit (Rule 57) can allow a flight that does not meet these. Aircraft registered abroad follow their own State's rules.
  • In India the CofA itself does not lapse by date, but it is only valid with a current Airworthiness Review Certificate (ARC), renewed every year after a review of the aircraft and its records (CAR-M).
  • No modification or repair affecting safety may be made to an aircraft with a valid CofA without the DGCA's prior approval, unless the rules require it. Manufacturer's approved modifications and repair schemes count as approved (Rule 52, old 53).
  • The CofA is deemed suspended if the aircraft is not maintained or modified as required, has major damage, or develops a major defect affecting safety. If the DGCA doubts the safety of the aircraft or its type, it may suspend or cancel the CofA, or order checks and repairs.
  • An aircraft whose CofA is suspended may not fly, except under a special flight permit from the DGCA (for example, a ferry flight to a repair base).
  • Where a flight manual is required, the DGCA endorses the CofA to say so.
  • Every aircraft must carry the instruments and equipment, including radio, needed for the kind of flight it is making.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Nationality mark from the ITU call sign series. Registration mark from the State of Registry. Common marks: series given to ICAO.
  • Registration mark: 1 to 5 characters. Not allowed: 5 letter signal code groups, 3 letter groups starting with Q, SOS, PAN, XXX, TTT.
  • Heights: wings at least 50 cm (lower surface, left half), fuselage and tail at least 30 cm, lighter than air at least 50 cm.
  • Identification plate: fireproof, near the main entrance, with nationality and registration marks.
  • India: ITU series AT to AW, but uses VT (civil). VU for the Air Force. Three letters after the hyphen.
  • Registration valid until cancelled by the DGCA. Cancelled if the CofA has expired for 5 years or more, among other reasons.
  • Category A (Indian owned or leased to Indians) for public transport and aerial work. Category B for foreign owners in India.
  • Type Certificate: State of Design. CofA and continuing airworthiness: State of Registry, even after damage abroad.
  • Annex 8 technical standards: multi-engined aeroplanes over 5,700 kg.
  • India: CofA plus a yearly ARC. Modifications need DGCA approval. Suspended CofA: fly only with a special flight permit.

Sample questions

5 of the 34 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The registration mark of an aircraft is assigned by:

    1. The ITU
    2. ICAO
    3. The State of Registry or a common mark registering authority
    4. The aircraft manufacturer
    Show answer

    Answer: C. The State of Registry (or a common mark registering authority) assigns the registration mark. The nationality mark comes from the ITU call sign series.

  2. Q2. Registration marks must not be confused with combinations of how many letters used in the International Code of Signals?

    1. Three
    2. Four
    3. Five
    4. Six
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Five letter combinations of the International Code of Signals are not allowed.

  3. Q3. The nationality mark used on Indian civil aircraft is:

    1. IN
    2. AT
    3. VT
    4. VU
    Show answer

    Answer: C. VT for civil aircraft. VU is used for the Air Force.

  4. Q4. For a leased aircraft, the certificate of registration also shows:

    1. The ticket prices
    2. The crew list
    3. The names and addresses of the lessor and lessee and the lease period
    4. The insurance company
    Show answer

    Answer: C. For leased aircraft the certificate records the lease details and the parties.

  5. Q5. Annex 8 deals with airworthiness from:

    1. The operating point of view
    2. The airport's point of view
    3. The passenger's point of view
    4. The engineering point of view
    Show answer

    Answer: D. Annex 8 is the engineering side. Annex 6 covers the operating side.
