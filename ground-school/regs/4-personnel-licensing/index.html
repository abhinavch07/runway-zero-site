Annex 1 and India's licensing rules: who grants licences, education and age, medical classes and how long they last, English proficiency, and what it takes to get, use and renew the SPL, PPL, CPL, Instrument Rating and ATPL, with ratings, cross-country rules and recency.

The basics

  • Annex 1 (Personnel Licensing) sets the international standards for licences of pilots, flight engineers, navigators, air traffic controllers, engineers and dispatchers. India's rules follow it, with differences listed in AIP India GEN 1.7.
  • In India the licensing authority is the Central Government (DGCA), under Rule 69 of the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026 (old Rule 38). It may refuse to grant or renew a licence if it thinks fit.
  • From 1 January 2027 the licences and ratings are in the First Schedule of the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026 (old Schedule II of the Aircraft Rules, 1937). Section J is still the CPL (Aeroplanes). Details are in CAR Section 7 (Series B for licences, Series C for medicals).
  • A licence holder must not use the licence when he knows his fitness has dropped below the standard for that licence.
The draft of the 2026 rules had proposed any Class 12 pass for the CPL and ATPL. The final rules keep Physics and Mathematics.

  • A first medical is valid from the date of the examination. A renewal done within the 45 days before the old one expires runs on from the day after the old expiry, so no days are lost. Done earlier than that, it runs from the date of the exam (Rule 79). Licence renewals work the same way, counting from when the application is made.
  • After any illness or injury that stops you from flying for 15 days or more, you may not fly again until a fresh medical finds you fit. You or your employer must tell the DGCA at once. Unfitness of 15 to 60 days needs a special medical with a DGCA panel Class 1 examiner.
  • Any in-flight incapacitation of a crew member must be reported by the operator to the DGCA.
  • Initial Class 1 medicals and certain renewals are done at the Indian Air Force medical centres, such as IAM Bengaluru and AFCME New Delhi. Other renewals can be done by DGCA approved Class 1 examiners (see the links on our home page).

Student Pilot Licence (SPL)

  • Age 16, Class 10, Class 2 medical, police (security) clearance, and oral exams in Air Regulations, Air Navigation, Aviation Meteorology and Aircraft and Engines.
  • Issued by a flying club or flight training organisation authorised by the DGCA.
  • Privileges: fly as PIC within India only, always under the authority and supervision of a flight instructor or approved examiner, VFR only, no passengers, animals or goods, and no flying for hire or reward.
  • No cross-country flight until the student has 10 hours of solo time and has passed the Air Navigation and Aviation Meteorology exams.

Private Pilot Licence (PPL)

  • Age 17, Class 10, Class 2 medical, written exams in Air Regulations, Air Navigation, Meteorology and Aircraft and Engines.
  • Experience: at least 40 hours of flight time, including at least 10 hours solo, 5 hours of cross-country as sole occupant with one flight of at least 150 nm making full stop landings at two different aerodromes, and 5 hours solo in the 12 months before applying.
  • Up to 20 hours of credit in total: half of microlight or glider time (up to 10 hours) and light sport aircraft solo time (up to 20 hours), flown in the last 24 months.
  • Skill test with an examiner within 6 months before applying.
  • Privileges: PIC or co-pilot of aeroplanes in the licence's rating, carrying passengers, but no hire or reward of any kind. Passengers at night need a night rating and 5 take-offs and 5 landings at night as PIC in the last 6 months. IFR needs an Instrument Rating.
  • Renewal: medical plus 5 hours as PIC in the last 12 months, or a flying test in the last 6 months.
  • A cross-country flight means going beyond 50 nm from the departure aerodrome.

Old exam answer

PPL changes from 2027: the 1937 Rules asked for 20 hours solo. The 2026 First Schedule asks for 10 hours solo plus 5 solo hours in the last 12 months. The open rating is not in the 2026 First Schedule. Class and type ratings are now as the DGCA specifies, so the open rating figures above are from the 1937 Rules.

Skill tests, within the 6 months before applying: a general flying test by day and by night, a cross-country test by day of at least 250 nm with at least one full stop landing at another aerodrome, and a cross-country test by night of at least 120 nm, returning to the departure aerodrome without landing elsewhere.

  • Privileges: everything a PPL can do in aeroplanes in the rating. PIC of any aeroplane in operations other than commercial air transport. PIC in commercial air transport of an aeroplane in the rating that is certified for single pilot operation. A complex single pilot aeroplane also needs command upgrade training. Co-pilot of any aeroplane in the rating where a co-pilot is required.
  • To carry passengers at night as PIC: 10 take-offs and 10 landings at night as PIC in the 6 months before the flight.
  • IFR flights, as PIC or co-pilot, need a current Instrument Rating.
  • At 65, the privileges become those of a PPL.
  • Validity: 10 years (since 11 October 2023, before that 5 years).
  • Renewal: a medical, 10 hours as PIC in the last 6 months (half of co-pilot time counts towards it), or instead the general flying tests by day and night, and a current FRTOL.
  • Extension to a new class or type: pass the Aircraft and Engines exam for it, train under supervision (or on a simulator) to the DGCA syllabus, and pass a skill test.
  • A cross-country flight, unless stated otherwise, means going beyond 50 nm from the departure aerodrome.

Old exam answer

Older books and question banks give the CPL a validity of 5 years. Since 11 October 2023 CPLs and ATPLs issued or renewed are valid for 10 years.

Old exam answer

CPL changes from 2027: older question banks say a CPL holder may be PIC up to 5,700 kg, and that cross-country means beyond 100 nm. The 2026 First Schedule drops the 5,700 kg limit and uses 50 nm. The CPL open rating (1,000 hours, 500 as PIC, 4 types) and the 6 month co-pilot proficiency check for aeroplanes over 5,700 kg are no longer in the rules. The DGCA sets them in CARs.

Instrument Rating (IR)

  • Lets the holder fly under the Instrument Flight Rules.
  • Experience: at least 100 hours as PIC, including 50 hours cross-country, and at least 40 hours of instrument time, of which no more than 20 may be instrument ground time (simulator). At least 5 hours of instrument time in the 6 months before applying.
  • Written and oral exams, a current FRTOL, and an instrument flying test within 6 months before applying.
  • Valid 12 months from the test. Renewed by passing the IR test again. If the test is done within the 2 months before expiry, the new 12 months run from the old expiry date.
  • Scope: a test on a single engine aeroplane up to 5,700 kg covers all single engine aeroplanes up to 5,700 kg. A test on a multi-engine aeroplane up to 5,700 kg covers all multi-engine (and single engine) aeroplanes up to 5,700 kg. Over 5,700 kg, the IR is valid only on the type tested.

Airline Transport Pilot Licence (ATPL)

  • Age 21, Class 12 with Physics and Maths, Class 1 medical, a CPL, a current multi-engine IR and FRTOL, and written exams. A current CPL holder does not repeat Air Regulations.
  • Experience: 1,500 hours of flight time, including 500 hours as PIC under supervision (or 250 hours as PIC, or at least 70 as PIC with the rest as PIC under supervision), 200 hours cross-country (100 as PIC or PIC under supervision), 100 hours at night, 75 hours of instrument time (at least 50 in actual flight) and 20 hours in the last 6 months.
  • Lets the holder act as PIC in commercial air transport of an aeroplane certified for more than one pilot, after command upgrade training as specified by the DGCA.
  • Renewal: as for the CPL, plus a current multi-engine IR.
  • Valid 10 years. At 65, its privileges are limited to those of a PPL.

Old exam answer

Older question banks say an ATPL holder commands aeroplanes over 5,700 kg after 100 hours as co-pilot on the type and 10 route checks (5 by night). The 2026 rules replace this with command upgrade training set by the DGCA. Older books also use different night and instrument hours. The CPL is what the CPL paper tests most.

Credit for flight time

  • When an aeroplane needs a co-pilot, 50% of co-pilot time counts towards the total flight time for a higher licence. Co-pilot time under supervision of a PIC counts 50% towards PIC time.
  • For CPL and ATPL renewal, 50% of co-pilot time counts towards the PIC hours needed.
  • An SPL holder logs as PIC only the time when he is the sole occupant of the aircraft.
  • Instrument time on an approved simulator is instrument ground time, and only up to the limit for each licence counts.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Licensing: Annex 1. Authority in India: Central Government (DGCA). CPL is Section J of the First Schedule of the 2026 rules (old Schedule II).
  • Ages: SPL 16, PPL 17, CPL 18, ATPL 21. Sole control 16. Commercial limit 65 (60 to 65 multi-crew with the other pilot under 60).
  • Class 1: CPL, ATPL. 12 months to 60, then 6. 6 months after 40 for single-crew commercial. Class 2: SPL, PPL. 2 years to 50, then 1.
  • Ill 15 days or more: fresh medical before flying, tell the DGCA.
  • English: Level 4 minimum, retest every 3 years. Level 5 every 6 years. Level 6 never.
  • SPL: under an instructor, VFR, no passengers or goods. Cross-country after 10 hours solo and Nav and Met exams.
  • PPL: 40 hours, 10 solo (old rules: 20), 5 cross-country as sole occupant with a 150 nm flight and two landings, 5 solo in the last 12 months. No hire or reward.
  • CPL: 200 hours, 100 PIC (15 in last 6 months), 20 cross-country with a 300 nm flight and two full stop landings, 10 instrument (max 5 sim), 5 night with 10 take-offs and landings in last 6 months.
  • CPL skill tests: day and night GFT, day cross-country 250 nm with one landing, night cross-country 120 nm back without landing.
  • CPL: PIC of any aeroplane outside commercial air transport, and of single pilot aeroplanes in commercial air transport (old rules: PIC up to 5,700 kg). Night passengers: 10 night take-offs and landings in 6 months. IFR needs IR.
  • CPL and ATPL valid 10 years (was 5). Renewal: 10 hours PIC in 6 months (half of co-pilot time counts).
  • Cross-country: beyond 50 nm for all licences (old rules: 100 nm, PPL 50 nm).
  • Medical or licence renewed within 45 days of expiry: the new period runs from the day after the old expiry.
  • IR: 100 PIC, 50 cross-country, 40 instrument (max 20 ground), 5 in last 6 months. Valid 12 months.
  • ATPL: command in commercial air transport after command upgrade training (old rules: 100 h as co-pilot and 10 route checks).
  • Co-pilot time: 50% credit.

Sample questions

5 of the 43 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The ICAO Annex containing Standards and Recommended Practices for personnel licensing is:

    1. Annex 1
    2. Annex 2
    3. Annex 6
    4. Annex 11
    Show answer

    Answer: A. Annex 1 is Personnel Licensing.

  2. Q2. A CPL holder's medical expires on 30 June. He takes his renewal medical on 1 June and passes. The new 12 months run from:

    1. 1 June
    2. 1 July
    3. 30 June of the next year
    4. The date the DGCA records it
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Rule 79: a renewal done within the 45 days before expiry runs from the day after the old expiry, so from 1 July.

    Old exam answer: Older question banks say a medical always runs from the date of the examination. That is still true for a first medical, or a renewal done more than 45 days early.

  3. Q3. A PPL holder is asked by a friend to fly him to a nearby town for payment of the fuel and a fee. The PPL holder:

    1. May accept, as it is a private flight
    2. May accept only on weekends
    3. May accept if he has a night rating
    4. May not accept any hire or remuneration
    Show answer

    Answer: D. A PPL holder may carry passengers, but must never be paid or rewarded in any way for a flight.

  4. Q4. The night cross-country skill test for a CPL is a flight of at least:

    1. 100 nm with one landing
    2. 300 nm
    3. 250 nm with one landing
    4. 120 nm returning to the departure aerodrome without landing elsewhere
    Show answer

    Answer: D. 120 nm at night, back to the start without landing.

  5. Q5. An Instrument Rating is valid for:

    1. 6 months
    2. As long as the CPL
    3. 2 years
    4. 12 months from the test
    Show answer

    Answer: D. 12 months, renewed by passing the IR test again.
