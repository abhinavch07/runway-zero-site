Who runs civil aviation in India (MoCA, DGCA, AAI, BCAS, AAIB), the laws (the Aircraft Act 1934 and its replacement, the Bharatiya Vayuyan Adhiniyam 2024, the Aircraft Rules 1937 and the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam 2026 that replace them), how CARs are made, and the rules every pilot is asked about: documents, prohibited areas, alcohol, fuelling, dropping articles, age limits, log books and penalties.

Who runs civil aviation in India

BodyRole
Ministry of Civil Aviation (MoCA)Makes national policy for civil aviation and administers the aviation laws. Based at Rajiv Gandhi Bhawan, Safdarjung Airport, New Delhi
DGCA (Directorate General of Civil Aviation)The safety regulator: registers aircraft, sets airworthiness standards and issues certificates of airworthiness, licenses pilots, engineers and controllers, conducts the exams, grants AOCs, certifies aerodromes and issues CARs. Office opposite Safdarjung Airport, New Delhi
Airports Authority of India (AAI)Formed on 1 April 1995 by merging the International Airports Authority and the National Airports Authority. Runs many airports, provides air traffic services and navigation aids, and publishes the AIP, NOTAMs and AIRAC changes
Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (BCAS)The security regulator. The authority for Annex 17 in India
Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB)Investigates accidents and serious incidents (set up in 2012, so DGCA no longer investigates major accidents itself)
Airports Economic Regulatory Authority (AERA)Sets tariffs and charges at major airports
WPC Wing (Ministry of Communications)Wireless Planning and Coordination: licenses radio use and frequencies

Under the Bharatiya Vayuyan Adhiniyam, 2024, the DGCA, BCAS and AAIB became bodies set up by the Act itself, each headed by a Director General.

Old exam answer

From 1 January 2027 the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026 replace the Aircraft Rules, 1937, with new rule numbers. This course gives the new number first and the old 1937 number in brackets. Older question banks, and papers set before the change, use the 1937 numbers. Learn what each rule says, and know both numbers for the rules asked most.

  • The Act and the Rules apply to the whole of India, to Indian citizens wherever they are, to aircraft registered in India wherever they are, and to foreign aircraft while in or over India.
  • For a foreign registered aircraft, its own State's rules on registration, crew licences, airworthiness and log books apply, provided they meet ICAO standards.
  • Power to detain: the Government can detain an aircraft if its flight would be dangerous, or to make it obey the law (Section 8 of the 1934 Act, now Section 17 of the 2024 Act, used through Rule 13 of the 2026 rules).
  • No slaughtering or skinning of animals, and no dumping of rubbish, within 10 km of the aerodrome reference point (Rule 137 of the 2026 rules). The Government can also order buildings and trees near an aerodrome to be cut down to size, within 20 km.
  • Penalties under the 1934 Act, as amended in 2020: up to 2 years in prison and a fine up to ₹1 crore for breaking rules made under it. Slaughter or rubbish within 10 km: up to 3 years. The DGCA can also suspend or cancel a licence or certificate.
  • No suit for trespass or nuisance just because an aircraft flew over a property at a reasonable height.

Civil Aviation Requirements (CARs)

The DGCA issues special directions under Rule 202 (old Rule 133A): CARs, AICs, NOTAMs and the AIP. A draft CAR is put on the DGCA website for 30 days for objections and suggestions before it is issued. In the public interest the DG may cut this period or skip it.

The syllabus asks mainly about Sections 2, 7 and 8.

Rules about the aircraft and the flight

Old exam answer

From 1 January 2027 the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026 replace the Aircraft Rules, 1937, with new rule numbers. This course gives the new number first and the old 1937 number in brackets. Older question banks, and papers set before the change, use the 1937 numbers. Learn what each rule says, and know both numbers for the rules asked most.

Rule (old 1937 number)What it says
4 (old 5)No flying unless the aircraft is registered and bears its marks, except with special written permission of the Central Government
72, 73 (old 6, 6A)Crew must be licensed. No one may fly as pilot of a type not in the aircraft rating of the licence, except to qualify for it or for tests (rules 74 and 75)
5 (old 7, 7B)Valid documents on board. For an Indian aircraft, the documents are as specified by the DGCA. A licence sent for renewal is a valid excuse for not carrying it
6 (old 8)Arms, ammunition, explosives and military stores only with written permission of the Director General. Fee ₹500 for a person, ₹1,000 for an organisation
103 (old 9)Only a licensed person may operate the aircraft radio
173 (old 10)Mails only with written consent of the Director General Postal Services
8 (old 12)Prohibited and restricted areas are those listed in the AIP. A pilot who finds himself over a prohibited area must give the distress signal and land as soon as possible at one of the nearest aerodromes outside it, without going further in or descending over it, unless forced by weather. If ATS advises, or a warning signal is given, turn away at once. If the tower gives light signals, give the distress signal and land
9 (old 13)Photography or video at an aerodrome only with written permission of the aerodrome operator, who keeps a record for 3 years
10 (old 13)Photography or video from an aircraft in flight, including aerial survey, only as specified by the DGCA
11 (old 15)No flight without a valid certificate of airworthiness, maintained as required and carried on board. A special flight permit under rule 57 can allow a flight that does not meet these conditions
12 (old 16)Everyone must obey the Rules of the Air, issued by the DGCA in line with Annex 2
206 (old 17)Documents not required to be carried on board must be produced within 7 days of being asked
15 (old 21)Dangerous flying: no flying so low or so close to people or buildings as to cause unnecessary danger
17 (new)Help to aircraft in distress, in line with Annex 12
18, 19 (old 22, 23)No assault, threats or refusing the lawful instructions of the pilot-in-command or crew. The basis for the no-fly list of unruly passengers. India can also try such an offence on a flight outside India if the next landing is in India and the PIC hands the person over
  • Under the 1937 Rules, Schedule I listed these prohibited areas. Older exam papers still ask about them:
  • Towers of Silence, Malabar Hill, Mumbai: within 1 mile.
  • Rashtrapati Bhawan, New Delhi: ground to unlimited height.
  • An area near Vadodara (Baroda) aerodrome: ground to unlimited height.
  • Mathura refinery: within 10 km, ground to unlimited height.
  • An area near Bhubaneswar: ground to 50,000 ft.
  • The Kalpakkam nuclear plant area.
  • Current prohibited, restricted and danger areas are published in AIP India ENR 5.1.

Old exam answer

The 1937 Rules put the prohibited areas in Schedule I. The 2026 rules have no such schedule. Rule 8 points to the areas published in the AIP instead.

Rules about people on board

Rule (old 1937 number)What it says
174 (old 24)No alcohol, sedative, narcotic or stimulant within 12 hours before a flight, or during it, for any crew member, and no detectable alcohol in breath, blood or urine. The operator must test crew for this. No alcohol may be served on domestic flights in India, and passengers may not drink on them. Licence holders must not fly under any psychoactive substance
20 (old 24A)A person with a mental disorder or epilepsy may fly only with a doctor's certificate that they are fit to fly, no alcohol within 12 hours, kept sedated if excited, and with an attendant. If they needed sedation in the last two weeks, a doctor and an escort must go with them
24B (1937 only)Prisoners only with a written permit from the DGCA or another officer authorised by the Government. The 2026 rules have no separate rule on this
21 (old 24C)Animals, birds and reptiles only with a DGCA permit
175 (old 25)Smoking notices must be shown. Smoking is allowed only if the certificate of airworthiness (or a Government direction) permits it. No smoking anywhere on board during take-off, landing or refuelling
176 (old 27)No one may ride on the wings, undercarriage or any part not meant for people, except to reach parts for repairs or cargo, or with Government permission
112 (old 28)No one under 16 may have sole control of an aircraft in motion
113 (old 28A)No pilot aged 65 or more may fly as pilot-in-command or co-pilot in commercial air transport. From 60, only in a multi-crew aircraft with the other pilot under 60. The 60 year part does not apply to single pilot aircraft up to 5,700 kg flown multi-crew on commercial flights within India
25 (old 29)No interfering with the crew or tampering with the aircraft. No act likely to put the aircraft at risk
26 (old 29A)No civil flight faster than Mach 1 in a way that causes a sonic boom over India
177 (old 29B)Portable electronic devices are not allowed in flight. The PIC may allow mobile phones and smartwatches in flight, or after landing once clear of the runway (not after a low visibility landing). Wi-Fi and mobile services in flight only on aircraft the DGCA has certified

Fuelling and dropping things (Rules 22 and 24)

Rule 22 (old 25A) of the 2026 rules says fuel may be put in only from approved vehicles or hydrant installations, and lets the DGCA set the standards for fuelling. The distances below were written into Rule 25A of the 1937 Rules and are still the safe practice taught for the exam.

  • Fuel outdoors, at least 15 m from any building.
  • No smoking, naked flame or anything that can make a spark within 30 m of the aircraft or the fuelling equipment. A NO SMOKING notice must be shown.
  • Only operator, fuelling company, DGCA, customs and police staff within 15 m.
  • Engines off, ignition off. Radio and radar off, apart from what is needed for fuelling and minimum lights.
  • Aircraft and fuel equipment bonded together and earthed. Fire extinguishers ready.
  • Stop fuelling if a turbo-jet aircraft moves so that its jet exhaust is within 43 m. Stop fuelling if fuel is spilled, and clean it before going on.
  • Passengers may stay on board or board only with a cabin attendant on duty and the steps in place at the exit.

Dropping articles (Rule 24, old 26): nothing may be dropped from an aircraft in motion except ballast of fine sand or water. Exceptions: fuel or cargo in an emergency, message bags or smoke markers for navigation, towing ropes, and leaflets or flower petals with the prior written permission of the District Magistrate or Commissioner of Police. Parachute descent only in an emergency or as allowed by a DGCA order.

Old exam answer

The 15 m, 30 m and 43 m fuelling distances come from Rule 25A of the 1937 Rules. The 2026 rule does not list them. It leaves fuelling standards to the DGCA.

Rules about licences and log books

Rule (old 1937 number)What it says
69 (old 38)The Central Government (through the DGCA) is the licensing authority, and may refuse to grant or renew a licence
70 (old 39A)Disqualification from holding a licence, for example for alcohol or drug addiction, using an aircraft for a crime, irresponsible conduct, getting a licence by false information, being a habitual criminal, moral turpitude, sexual harassment or a heinous crime
78 (old 39B)No licence without passing a medical with an approved medical authority
79 (old 39C)Validity of licences and medicals. CPL, ATPL, SPL and PPL: 10 years. A medical or licence renewed within the 45 days before it expires runs on from the day after the old expiry
80, 81 (old 41, 41A)Applicants must prove experience and pass the checks, tests and exams in the First Schedule (old Schedule II). The DGCA conducts the exams
82 (old 42)Renewal after a lapse: lapsed 2 to 3 years: skill test and Air Regulations exam. Lapsed over 3 years: all the exams and tests
82 (2) (old 42 (2))After sickness or injury lasting 15 days or more, or an accident injury, the licence may not be used until a fresh medical declares the holder fit. The holder or employer must tell the DGCA
83 (old 45)The Government may validate a foreign licence for flying Indian aircraft
84 (old 67)Personal log books for flight crew, kept in the form and manner specified by the DGCA
  • Logging time: an instructor logs the time as PIC (noting it in remarks). Instrument time is logged only when flying solely by instruments. Flying over the top of cloud is not instrument time. Time on an approved simulator is logged as instrument ground time.
  • A co-pilot acting as PIC under supervision logs PIC time only if entitled to command that type. Otherwise it is co-pilot time.

Old exam answer

Rule 67 of the 1937 Rules said log book entries are in ink, kept for at least 5 years after the last entry, certified by the holder each month and by a competent authority (chief pilot, CFI or DGCA officer) each quarter. The 2026 Rule 84 leaves these details to the DGCA.

Operators, aerodromes and penalties

Rule (old 1937 number)What it says
7 (old 11)Aircraft carrying passengers for hire, or on scheduled services, must use licensed or approved aerodromes
39 (4) and 186 (old 30)Registration in Category A (owned by Indian citizens, Indian companies, IFSC leasing companies or Governments, or by a foreign company that leases it to them) or Category B (owned by foreigners resident or doing business in India). Only Category A aircraft may do public transport or aerial work
124 to 130 (old 78 to 80)Aerodrome licences, granted by the DGCA on an application with an Aerodrome Manual
202 (old 133A)DGCA directions: CARs, AICs, NOTAMs and AIP
183 (old 134)Scheduled air transport services need an Air Operator Certificate under the Fourth Schedule (old Schedule XI). Foreign airlines operate under bilateral agreements
195, 196 (old 140 to 140C)Operators keep an Operations Manual approved by the DGCA. The operator names one pilot-in-command for each flight, who has final authority over the aircraft
214, 215 (old 161)Offences under the Fifth Schedule and penalties under the Sixth Schedule (old Schedule VI). A defence: the breach was due to causes beyond the person's control, such as stress of weather or a genuine error

Customs (Indian Aircraft Rules, 1920): an aircraft entering India must make its first landing at a customs aerodrome, and an aircraft leaving India must depart from a customs aerodrome, unless forced to land elsewhere by accident or weather.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • MoCA policy. DGCA safety regulator, licences, CARs. AAI (1 April 1995) ATS and AIP. BCAS security. AAIB accident investigation.
  • Aircraft Act 1934 replaced by the Bharatiya Vayuyan Adhiniyam 2024 from 1 Jan 2025. Aircraft Rules 1937 replaced by the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam 2026 (G.S.R. 957(E), 26 Oct 2026) from 1 Jan 2027. 220 rules, 8 chapters, 6 schedules.
  • CARs under Rule 202 (old 133A), draft on the website for 30 days. Section 2 airworthiness, 7 crew licensing, 8 operations.
  • No slaughter or rubbish within 10 km of the ARP. Buildings and trees: within 20 km. Penalty up to 2 years and ₹1 crore.
  • Unregistered aircraft fly only with Central Government written permission. Arms only with DGCA permission (Rule 6, old 8, which said Central Government).
  • Mails: DG Postal Services. Photography at an aerodrome: aerodrome operator. From an aircraft in flight: as the DGCA specifies. Leaflets and petals: District Magistrate or Commissioner of Police.
  • Over a prohibited area (listed in the AIP): distress signal, land at the nearest aerodrome outside it.
  • Documents not carried: produce within 7 days.
  • Alcohol: none within 12 hours (Rule 174, old 24). No alcohol served on domestic flights. No smoking during take-off, landing or refuelling.
  • Fuelling: approved vehicles only, standards set by the DGCA. Old Rule 25A distances: 15 m from buildings and for outsiders, 30 m no smoking, 43 m jet exhaust stops fuelling. Bond and earth.
  • Sole control: 16 years (Rule 112). Commercial pilots: 65 maximum, 60 to 65 only multi-crew with the other pilot under 60 (Rule 113, old 28A).
  • Licence lapsed 2 to 3 years: skill test and Air Regs. Over 3 years: everything.
  • Sick or injured 15 days or more: fresh medical first.
  • Log books (Rule 84, old 67): form set by the DGCA. Old rule: ink, kept 5 years, certified monthly and quarterly. Over the top is not instrument time.
  • Entering India: first landing at a customs aerodrome.

Sample questions

5 of the 44 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The regulatory body in India dealing mainly with aviation safety, licensing and airworthiness is the:

    1. Airports Authority of India
    2. Directorate General of Civil Aviation
    3. Bureau of Civil Aviation Security
    4. Ministry of External Affairs
    Show answer

    Answer: B. The DGCA is the safety regulator. It licenses crew, registers aircraft, issues certificates of airworthiness and CARs.

  2. Q2. Slaughtering animals or dumping rubbish is prohibited within what radius of the aerodrome reference point?

    1. 5 km
    2. 10 km
    3. 20 km
    4. 50 km
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Within 10 km of the ARP, because it attracts birds. Breaking this can mean up to 3 years in prison.

    Old exam answer: This comes from the Aircraft Act, 1934, now replaced by the Bharatiya Vayuyan Adhiniyam, 2024.

  3. Q3. A document that is not required to be carried in the aircraft must be produced, when asked by an authorised person, within:

    1. 24 hours
    2. 3 days
    3. 7 days
    4. 30 days
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Rule 206 (old Rule 17): within seven days of the demand.

  4. Q4. Under Rule 25A of the 1937 Rules, during fuelling, smoking or a naked flame is not allowed within:

    1. 15 m
    2. 20 m
    3. 30 m
    4. 43 m
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Within 30 m of the aircraft or the fuelling equipment.

    Old exam answer: The 2026 Rule 22 does not list distances. It leaves fuelling standards to the DGCA.

  5. Q5. A pilot is ill and unable to fly for 20 days. Before flying again he must:

    1. Simply tell the chief pilot
    2. Wait for his licence to be renewed
    3. Do a simulator check only
    4. Be declared fit after a fresh medical examination
    Show answer

    Answer: D. Rule 82 (2) (old 42 (2)): sickness or injury of 15 days or more needs a fresh medical, and the DGCA must be told.
