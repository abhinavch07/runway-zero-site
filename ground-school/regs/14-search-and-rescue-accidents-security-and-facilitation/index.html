What happens when things go wrong: search and rescue in India, ELTs and COSPAS-SARSAT, signals between survivors and aircraft and with ships, accident and incident definitions, who to notify and how investigations work, aviation security and BCAS, dangerous goods, and the facilitation of international flights.

Search and rescue

  • SAR is in Annex 12. In India, the AAI runs aeronautical SAR through Rescue Coordination Centres (RCCs) at Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai. The Indian Coast Guard runs maritime SAR.
  • A Rescue Sub-Centre (RSC) helps an RCC in part of its area.
  • The SAR process starts with the alerting phases: INCERFA, ALERFA and DETRESFA (see Module 9).
  • ELTs transmit on 406 MHz, picked up by the COSPAS-SARSAT satellites, and on 121.5 MHz for homing. India's Mission Control Centre is at Bengaluru.

A PIC who sees an aircraft or ship in distress should, unless it is unsafe or unreasonable: keep it in sight, fix its position, report to the ATS unit or RCC with as much detail as possible, and act as the RCC instructs. If first on the scene, he may need to take charge until help arrives.

  • An aircraft shows it has understood a ground signal by day by rocking its wings, and at night by flashing its landing lights or nav lights twice. No such signal means it has not understood.
  • To direct a ship to a scene: circle the ship at least once, cross low ahead of its bow while rocking wings or opening and closing the throttle, then fly off in the direction it should go.
  • To show the ship's help is no longer needed: cross low close astern of it, rocking wings or opening and closing the throttle.

Accidents and incidents

  • Accident investigation is in Annex 13. In India: the Aircraft (Investigation of Accidents and Incidents) Rules, 2017, and the Aircraft Accident Investigation Bureau (AAIB), under the Ministry of Civil Aviation.
  • The only purpose of an investigation is to prevent future accidents. It is not to give blame or decide liability.

  • Notification in India: the PIC (or if he cannot, the operator or owner) tells the AAIB and the DGCA by the quickest means, as soon as possible and within 24 hours. For an occurrence in India, also tell the District Magistrate and the nearest police station.
  • The State of Occurrence runs the investigation. The States of Registry, Operator, Design and Manufacture may send accredited representatives.
  • Nothing at the site may be moved except to rescue people or animals, to protect the wreckage from fire or other damage, or to clear an obstruction, unless the AAIB allows. Flight recorders are protected.
  • CVR recordings are not used for blame or discipline.
  • The preliminary report goes to the States concerned and ICAO within 30 days. The final report should come within 12 months. If not, an interim statement is published on each anniversary.
  • An air traffic incident is first reported by radio, then on the Air Traffic Incident Report form soon after landing.

Aviation security

  • Security is in Annex 17. In India the Bureau of Civil Aviation Security (BCAS) is the regulator. The CISF guards most airports.
  • Unlawful interference includes hijacking, sabotage, hostage taking, bringing weapons on board, and false information that puts safety at risk.
  • All passengers and their cabin and hold bags are screened. Hold bags fly only with their passenger on board, unless screened again as unaccompanied.
  • An aircraft under unlawful interference squawks 7500 and is parked at an isolated stand at least 100 m from other aircraft, buildings and public areas.
  • The PIC may restrain a person who threatens safety or good order, and disembark him, under the Tokyo Convention (Module 1).
  • Carrying weapons on board needs special permission. The PIC must be told who is armed and where they sit.
  • The flight deck door stays locked in flight. Crew are trained to deal with unruly passengers.

Dangerous goods

  • Dangerous goods are in Annex 18 and the detailed Technical Instructions (Doc 9284). In India, the Aircraft (Carriage of Dangerous Goods) Rules, 2003.
  • Some goods are forbidden on any aircraft. Others are allowed only on cargo aircraft, or in limited amounts.
  • The operator gives the PIC written information about the dangerous goods on board, where they are loaded and how much, before the flight. This is the NOTOC (notification to captain).
  • In an in-flight emergency, the PIC tells ATS about the dangerous goods on board, so that the aerodrome can prepare.
  • Any accident or incident with dangerous goods is reported to the DGCA and the State of origin.

Facilitation

  • Facilitation is in Annex 9. It aims to make border formalities for aircraft, passengers and cargo quick and simple.
  • Documents an international flight may need: the General Declaration, a passenger manifest and a cargo manifest. States should not ask for more than the Annex allows.
  • International flights to and from India use customs aerodromes, under the Indian Aircraft Rules, 1920 (Module 2).
  • States handle inadmissible persons (refused entry) and deportees. The operator returns an inadmissible passenger to where he started.
  • Disinsection (spraying against insects) is done where health rules require, in a way that is safe for passengers.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • SAR: Annex 12. India: AAI RCCs at Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai. Coast Guard for sea.
  • ELT: 406 MHz to COSPAS-SARSAT, 121.5 MHz homing.
  • Ground signals: V assistance, X medical, N no, Y yes, arrow going this way.
  • Understood: rock wings by day, lights twice at night.
  • Direct a ship: circle, cross ahead of the bow low, fly off in the direction. Not needed: cross astern.
  • Accidents: Annex 13. India: 2017 Rules, AAIB. Purpose: prevention, not blame.
  • Fatal: death within 30 days. Serious: hospital over 48 hours within 7 days, fractures (not simple fingers, toes, nose), burns over 5%.
  • Notify AAIB and DGCA within 24 hours. Also the District Magistrate and police.
  • State of Occurrence investigates. Preliminary report 30 days, final 12 months, then yearly interim statements.
  • Security: Annex 17, BCAS. Hijacked aircraft stand at least 100 m away.
  • Dangerous goods: Annex 18, Doc 9284, India 2003 Rules. PIC gets written NOTOC.
  • Facilitation: Annex 9. General Declaration, passenger and cargo manifests.

    2. Annex 12
    3. Annex 13
    4. Annex 17

    Answer: B. Annex 12. Annex 13 is accident investigation.

  2. Q2. By day, an aircraft shows it has understood a ground signal by:

    1. Flying a right-hand circuit
    2. Rocking its wings
    3. Flashing its landing lights
    4. Climbing
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Rock wings by day, flash lights twice at night.

  3. Q3. A fatal injury is one that results in death within:

    1. 7 days
    2. 15 days
    3. 30 days
    4. 90 days
    Show answer

    Answer: C. 30 days of the accident.

  4. Q4. An accident investigation is run by the State of:

    1. Registry
    2. Design
    3. the Operator
    4. Occurrence
    Show answer

    Answer: D. The State where it happened, which may delegate.

  5. Q5. The ICAO document with the detailed dangerous goods rules is:

    1. Doc 4444
    2. Doc 8168
    3. Doc 9284
    4. Doc 7030
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Doc 9284, the Technical Instructions under Annex 18.
