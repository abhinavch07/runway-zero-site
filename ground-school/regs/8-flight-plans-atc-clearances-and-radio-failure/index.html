When and how to file a flight plan in India, the main items of the ICAO flight plan form, delays and changes, closing the plan, ATC clearances and read-back, deviations, position reports, what to do when the radio fails, UTC time checks, and Air Defence Clearance.

When a flight plan is needed

  • Any flight, or part of a flight, that gets air traffic control service.
  • Any IFR flight in advisory airspace.
  • Any flight in areas or along routes where ATS needs it for flight information, alerting and search and rescue.
  • Any flight where ATS needs it to coordinate with the military or with ATS of a neighbouring State, so the aircraft is not intercepted.
  • Any flight across international borders.
  • In practice in India, every flight going beyond the vicinity of the aerodrome (5 nm, 3,000 ft) files a plan.
TermMeaning
Filed flight plan (FPL)The plan as first filed, with no later changes
Current flight plan (CPL)The plan including changes from later clearances
Repetitive flight plan (RPL)For regular IFR flights with the same details, flown on the same days of consecutive weeks on at least 10 occasions, or every day for at least 10 days in a row. Filed by the operator and kept by ATS
EOBTEstimated off-block time: when the aircraft expects to start moving for departure

Filing, delays and changes

  • In India, file at least 180 minutes before EOBT, and not earlier than 120 hours before it, unless there is an operational reason. Plans for remotely piloted aircraft need 24 hours.
  • A plan filed in the air must reach ATS at least 10 minutes before the aircraft reaches the point where it enters a control area or advisory airspace, or crosses an airway.
  • Flying club training flights inside the aerodrome traffic zone may file by phone or fax.
  • A delay of 15 minutes or more to the departure time: tell ATC the revised time.
  • A delay of 30 minutes beyond EOBT for a controlled flight, or 1 hour for an uncontrolled one: amend the plan or file a new one and cancel the old.
  • Any change to the plan of an IFR or controlled VFR flight is reported as soon as practicable. For other VFR flights, report significant changes. A wrong endurance or persons on board figure counts as significant.

Old exam answer

The ICAO standard in Doc 4444 asks for a flight plan at least 60 minutes before departure. India's AIP asks for 180 minutes. Answer by the country the question names.

  • Total EET for an IFR flight runs from take-off to the point where the instrument approach begins, or if none, to overhead the destination. For VFR, from take-off to overhead the destination.
  • In Item 19, endurance is fuel time in hours and minutes (E/0430). Persons on board includes crew (P/005). Strike out what is not carried.
  • All times are 4 figures UTC. Use block capitals.

Closing the plan

After landing, make an arrival report to the ATS unit at the arrival aerodrome as soon as possible, in person, by radio or by data link. If the report does not come, ATS starts the alerting process, so landing somewhere else without telling ATS can start a search.

ATC clearances

  • A clearance is needed before any controlled flight. It is asked for by filing a flight plan.
  • A clearance lets an aircraft go on under stated conditions, only to separate traffic. It does not relieve the PIC of responsibility for safety or for following the rules.
  • The clearance gives: aircraft identification, clearance limit, route, levels, and other details such as the SSR code, SID, frequency, and any time limits.
  • A clearance limit is the point to which the aircraft is cleared. A clearance expiry time is when it becomes void if the flight has not started.
  • If a clearance is not acceptable, the PIC should say so and ask for a different one.
  • Read back in full: the route clearance, any clearance to enter, land on, take off from, cross, taxi on or backtrack on a runway, the runway in use, altimeter settings, SSR codes, level instructions, heading and speed instructions, and transition levels.
  • An aircraft follows the current flight plan unless it asks for a change and gets a clearance.
DeviationAction
Off trackTurn to regain track as soon as practicable
Assigned Mach or IAS not being keptTell ATS at once
TAS or Mach at cruise differs from the plan by 5%, or Mach 0.02, or 10 ktTell ATS
ETA for the next reporting point, FIR boundary or destination changes by more than 2 minutesTell ATS the revised estimate as soon as possible

Old exam answer

Older question banks give the ETA change limit as 3 minutes. The current figure is 2 minutes. If 2 minutes is not an option, pick 3.

Position reports

  • A controlled flight reports at compulsory reporting points (a filled triangle on charts), and at on-request points (an open triangle) when ATS asks.
  • A position report gives: aircraft identification, position, time, flight level or altitude, next position and time, and the next significant point after that.
  • Under radar control, ATS may tell the pilot to stop routine reports except at stated points.
  • Controlled flights keep a continuous listening watch on the right frequency.

Radio failure

Squawk 7600, keep a watch for light signals, and try to call ATS on other frequencies. Keep transmitting blind, as ATS may hear you.

WeatherWhat to do
In VMCStay in VMC, land at the nearest suitable aerodrome, and report arrival to ATS as soon as possible
In IMC, no radar in useKeep the last assigned speed and level (or the minimum flight altitude if higher) for 20 minutes after failing to report at a compulsory reporting point, then follow the filed flight plan
In IMC, radar in useKeep the last speed and level for 7 minutes after the latest of: reaching the last assigned level, setting 7600, or missing a compulsory report. Then follow the filed flight plan
  • Fly to the navigation aid or fix serving the destination and hold there if needed.
  • Start descent from that fix at the last expected approach time (EAT) you acknowledged, or if none, at the ETA from your plan.
  • Fly a normal instrument approach, and land within 30 minutes after that ETA or EAT, whichever is later.
  • If being radar vectored, rejoin the flight plan route by the next significant point, keeping to the minimum altitude.

Memory trick

20 without radar, 7 with radar, 30 to land. The radar number is smaller because ATC can see you.

Time

  • All aviation uses UTC on the 24 hour clock, from midnight.
  • Get a time check before a controlled flight.
  • ATS clocks are kept within 30 seconds of UTC. For data link, within 1 second.
  • India is UTC plus 5 hours 30 minutes.

Air Defence Clearance (India)

  • India has Air Defence Identification Zones (ADIZ) where every aircraft must be identified. Flying in one needs an Air Defence Clearance (ADC) number.
  • The ADC is given through the Flight Information Centre after the flight plan is filed, and goes in the plan or is given to ATC.
  • An ADC is normally valid from 15 minutes before to 45 minutes after EOBT. Outside this window, get a fresh one.
  • Aircraft coming into India get the ADC at least 10 minutes before entering the Indian ADIZ.
  • Local flights below 1,000 ft within 5 nm of the aerodrome reference point do not need an ADC.
  • An aircraft without a valid ADC can be intercepted.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • India: file 180 min before EOBT, not earlier than 120 hours. Airborne plan: 10 min before entering controlled airspace.
  • Delay 15 min: tell ATC. 30 min (controlled) or 1 hour (uncontrolled): amend or refile.
  • RPL: IFR, regular, at least 10 flights.
  • Item 8: Y IFR then VFR, Z VFR then IFR. Type: S, N, G, M, X.
  • Wake: L 7,000 kg or less, H 136,000 kg or more, J A380.
  • Item 15: N0120 knots, K km/h, M Mach. F085, A045.
  • IFR EET: to the start of the instrument approach. VFR: to overhead destination.
  • Item 19 is not transmitted. It is for SAR.
  • Read back: route, runway clearances, altimeter, SSR code, level, heading, speed, transition level.
  • Tell ATS: TAS 5%, Mach 0.02, ETA 2 min.
  • Radio failure: 7600. VMC: land nearest suitable. IMC: 20 min (no radar) or 7 min (radar), descend at EAT or ETA, land within 30 min.
  • Time: UTC, time check before controlled flight, clocks within 30 s. India is UTC +5:30.
  • ADC: valid 15 min before to 45 min after EOBT. Not needed below 1,000 ft within 5 nm.

Sample questions

5 of the 35 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. A flight plan must be filed for:

    1. Only IFR flights
    2. Any flight that will get air traffic control service
    3. Only international flights
    4. Only flights at night
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Any controlled flight needs a plan, as do IFR flights in advisory airspace and border crossings.

  2. Q2. In Item 8, a flight that starts IFR and changes to VFR enters:

    1. I
    2. V
    3. Y
    4. Z
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Y is IFR first, Z is VFR first.

  3. Q3. If the departure aerodrome has no ICAO location indicator, Item 13 shows:

    1. XXXX
    2. ZZZZ
    3. NNNN
    4. 0000
    Show answer

    Answer: B. ZZZZ, with DEP/ and the name in Item 18.

  4. Q4. Your ETA for the next reporting point changes by 3 minutes. You should:

    1. Do nothing
    2. Squawk 7600
    3. Change your level
    4. Tell ATS the revised estimate
    Show answer

    Answer: D. A change of more than 2 minutes is passed to ATS.

    Old exam answer: Some older banks say 3 minutes. Either way, 3 minutes must be reported.

  5. Q5. With a radio failure in IMC, the aircraft should land, if possible, within how long of the ETA or last acknowledged EAT, whichever is later?

    1. 10 minutes
    2. 15 minutes
    3. 30 minutes
    4. 60 minutes
    Show answer

    Answer: C. 30 minutes.
