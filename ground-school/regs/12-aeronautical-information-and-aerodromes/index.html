Where flight information comes from: the AIP and its parts, supplements, AICs, NOTAMs and AIRAC dates. Then the aerodrome: reference codes, declared distances, runway and taxiway markings, signs, lights, PAPI, beacons and fire cover.

Aeronautical Information Service

  • AIS is in Annex 15. In India the AAI runs it, except AICs, which the DGCA issues.
  • The Integrated Aeronautical Information Package is: the AIP with its amendments and supplements, NOTAMs, pre-flight information bulletins (PIB), AICs, and checklists and lists of valid NOTAMs.
DocumentWhat it holds
AIPInformation of a lasting nature. Three parts: GEN (general), ENR (en route), AD (aerodromes). India publishes it in two volumes, with AD in the second
AIP AmendmentPermanent changes to the AIP
AIP SupplementTemporary changes lasting 3 months or more, or short ones with long text or drawings. Printed on yellow paper. A checklist comes out at least once a month
NOTAMTemporary or short notice information that pilots must know in time: a runway closure, a navaid out of service, a danger area being active
AICInformation that does not fit the AIP or a NOTAM: safety advice, explanations, administrative or legal matters, and advance warning of big changes
PIBA pre-flight summary of the NOTAMs and urgent information for a route or aerodrome
  • In India NOTAMs come from the International NOTAM Offices at Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai. A checklist of valid NOTAMs goes out every month.
  • NOTAM types: N new, R replaces an earlier one, C cancels one. SNOWTAM covers snow, ice and water on the movement area. ASHTAM covers volcanic ash.
  • Activating a danger area by NOTAM needs at least 7 days' notice where possible.
AIRACRule
WhatA system of fixed common dates, every 28 days, for bringing in planned changes such as new procedures, airspace, frequencies or navaids
DistributionAt least 42 days before the effective date, so it arrives at least 28 days before. For major changes, 56 days ahead
FreezeNo further change for at least 28 days after the effective date, unless temporary

Aerodrome reference code

The code is used to plan an aerodrome to suit the aeroplanes using it. An A320 is 4C, a Boeing 777 is 4E, an A380 is 4F.

Pavement strength for aircraft over 5,700 kg is reported by the ACN-PCN system (now ACR-PCR). An aircraft may use the pavement without limit if its ACN is equal to or less than the PCN.

Areas and declared distances

TermMeaning
Manoeuvring areaUsed for take-off, landing and taxiing, not including aprons
Movement areaManoeuvring area plus aprons
ApronWhere aircraft load, unload, refuel, park or get maintenance
StopwayA prepared area beyond the runway where an aircraft can stop after an abandoned take-off
ClearwayAn area beyond the runway, clear of obstacles, over which an aeroplane can make part of its initial climb
Declared distanceIs
TORA (take-off run available)The runway length usable for the take-off ground run
TODA (take-off distance available)TORA + clearway
ASDA (accelerate-stop distance available)TORA + stopway
LDA (landing distance available)The runway length usable for the landing ground run, from the threshold

Memory trick

Clearway is for climbing over, so it adds to the take-off distance. Stopway is for stopping on, so it adds to the accelerate-stop distance.

Markings and signs

  • Runway markings are white. Taxiway and aircraft stand markings are yellow.
  • The runway designator is the magnetic heading of the runway to the nearest 10 degrees, in two digits. Parallel runways add L, C or R.
  • Threshold marking: white stripes along the runway, starting 6 m in. A displaced threshold has arrows leading up to it. The part before it can be used for take-off and taxiing but not for landing.
  • Yellow chevrons mark a pre-threshold area not fit for normal use.
  • Aiming point marking: two large stripes. Touchdown zone markings: pairs of stripes, more pairs on longer runways.
  • Runway-holding position marking: two solid and two dashed yellow lines across the taxiway, with the dashed lines on the runway side. Stop at the solid lines unless cleared.
  • A white or yellow cross on a runway or taxiway: that part is closed.

  • An aerodrome beacon at a land aerodrome flashes white and green, or white only. At a water aerodrome, white and yellow.
  • A simple approach lighting system is a line of lights 420 m long with a crossbar at 300 m. A precision system is longer, about 900 m, with more crossbars.
  • A wind direction indicator is a cone, usually white and red, or white and orange.
  • Obstacle lights: low intensity on fixed objects are steady red, on vehicles flashing yellow or blue. High intensity are flashing white.

Memory trick

Red over white, you're all right. All red, you're dead. For PAPI: two and two is on the slope.

Rescue and fire fighting

  • The aerodrome RFF category (1 to 10) depends on the overall length and fuselage width of the largest aeroplanes that normally use it.
  • The aim is a response time of 2 minutes, and never more than 3 minutes, to the end of each runway in good conditions.
  • Emergency vehicles are painted a bright colour, preferably red or yellow-green.
  • A parking stand for an aircraft under unlawful interference should be at least 100 m from other stands, buildings and public areas.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • AIS: Annex 15. AIP parts: GEN, ENR, AD. AIC issued by the DGCA in India.
  • AIP Supplement: temporary, 3 months or more, yellow pages.
  • NOTAM: temporary, short notice. N new, R replace, C cancel. Checklist monthly.
  • AIRAC: every 28 days. Send 42 days ahead (56 for major), arrive 28 days ahead.
  • Code number by field length: 1 under 800 m, 4 from 1,800 m. Letter by wingspan: A under 15 m, C to 36 m, F to 80 m.
  • ACN equal to or less than PCN: OK.
  • Movement = manoeuvring + aprons.
  • TODA = TORA + clearway. ASDA = TORA + stopway.
  • Runway markings white, taxiway yellow. Designator: magnetic heading to 10 degrees.
  • Holding position: dashed lines on the runway side.
  • Signs: red with white mandatory, black with yellow location, yellow with black direction.
  • Lights: edge white (last 600 m yellow), threshold green, end red, taxi edge blue, taxi centre green.
  • Centre line: red and white 900 to 300 m from the end, red last 300 m.
  • PAPI: 2 white 2 red on slope. Beacon: white and green.
  • RFF response 2 minutes, never over 3.

Sample questions

5 of the 31 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The ICAO Annex on Aeronautical Information Services is:

    1. Annex 11
    2. Annex 14
    3. Annex 15
    4. Annex 4
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Annex 15. Annex 14 is aerodromes, Annex 4 is charts.

  2. Q2. A NOTAM marked 'R' is one that:

    1. Is a reminder
    2. Replaces an earlier NOTAM
    3. Restricts an area
    4. Is for a runway
    Show answer

    Answer: B. N new, R replace, C cancel.

  3. Q3. The movement area is:

    1. The runways only
    2. The manoeuvring area plus the aprons
    3. The aprons only
    4. The taxiways only
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Manoeuvring area excludes aprons. Movement area includes them.

  4. Q4. Arrows on the runway centre line leading to the threshold mean:

    1. The runway is closed
    2. A taxiway
    3. The threshold is displaced
    4. Wind shear
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Before a displaced threshold, the runway may be used for take-off and taxi, not landing.

  5. Q5. On final approach you see three white lights and one red on the PAPI. You are:

    1. On the slope
    2. Well below
    3. Slightly below
    4. Slightly above the slope
    Show answer

    Answer: D. More white means higher.
