PANS-OPS for the pilot: aircraft approach categories, instrument departures, the five segments of an instrument approach, precision and non-precision minima, circling and missed approaches, holding patterns with speeds and entries, ILS categories, and altimeter setting with transition altitude and level as used in India.

Aircraft categories

Procedures are designed for categories of aircraft based on Vat: the indicated airspeed at the threshold, which is 1.3 times the stall speed in landing configuration at maximum landing mass.

Instrument departures

  • Departures follow a Standard Instrument Departure (SID) where one is published.
  • A straight departure has an initial track within 15 degrees of the runway direction. A turning departure turns more than that.
  • The standard procedure design gradient is 3.3%. That gives a clearance above the obstacle surface of 2.5%, growing with distance. A steeper gradient is published where obstacles need it.
  • No turn is planned until the aircraft is at least 394 ft (120 m) above the departure end of the runway.
  • Where there is no SID, an omnidirectional departure may let you climb in any direction once you have reached a set height.

Old exam answer

Older books give 200 m for the CAT IIIA RVR limit. The current ICAO figure is 175 m.

Circling and missed approach

  • Circling is the visual part of an instrument approach used to land on a runway not lined up with the final approach.
  • Keep the runway environment in sight throughout, and stay at or above the circling MDA until the landing is assured.
  • If you lose visual reference while circling, make an initial climbing turn towards the landing runway and then follow the missed approach for the instrument procedure you flew.
  • The missed approach climb gradient is normally 2.5%.

Holding

  • A standard hold uses right turns. A non-standard hold uses left turns and is shown as such.
  • The inbound leg is 1 minute at or below 14,000 ft, and 1.5 minutes above.
  • Turns are at 3 degrees per second or a 25 degree bank, whichever needs less bank.
  • There are three entries, picked by your heading as you reach the fix, with 5 degrees of flexibility at the sector edges.

ATC gives an expected approach time (EAT) when the delay is likely to be 20 minutes or more, or an expect further clearance time when the aircraft is held short of its destination. A revised EAT is given if it changes by 5 minutes or more.

Altimeter setting

SettingAltimeter readsCalled
QNHHeight above mean sea levelAltitude
QFEHeight above the aerodrome (threshold)Height
1013.2 hPa (standard)Height above the 1013.2 hPa pressure levelFlight level
  • Transition altitude (TA): at and below it, vertical position is given as altitude on QNH. It is published in the AIP for each aerodrome. In India it is usually 4,000 ft, higher near high ground.
  • Transition level (TL): the lowest flight level usable above the TA. ATC sets it and passes it to arriving aircraft, often in the ATIS. In India the lowest is normally FL 50.
  • Transition layer: the airspace between the TA and the TL.
  • Climbing: set 1013.2 on passing the TA, and report in flight levels from then.
  • Descending: set QNH on passing the TL, and report in altitude from then.
  • Within the transition layer, report as flight level when climbing and altitude when descending.
  • QNH and QFE are given rounded down to a whole hPa.
  • Pre-flight check: set QNH at a known elevation. The altimeter should read the elevation of the spot, within the allowed tolerance.

Memory trick

Climb through altitude, set standard. Descend through level, set local. The pressure setting changes at the boundary you cross first.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Categories by Vat: A under 91, B 91 to 120, C 121 to 140, D 141 to 165, E 166 to 210 kt.
  • Straight departure: within 15 degrees. PDG 3.3%. No turn below 394 ft.
  • Segments: arrival, initial (IAF to IF), intermediate (IF to FAF), final, missed approach.
  • Precision: DA/DH. Non-precision: MDA/MDH. OCA/H plus margins.
  • ILS: stay within half scale. CAT I 200 ft, 550 m. CAT II 100 ft, 300 m. CAT IIIA RVR 175 m.
  • RVR drops after starting: continue to 1,000 ft or the final segment.
  • Lost visual while circling: climbing turn towards the runway, then missed approach. Missed approach gradient 2.5%.
  • Hold: right turns, 1 min to 14,000 ft, 1.5 min above.
  • Entries: parallel 110, offset 70 (30 degree offset), direct 180.
  • Hold speeds: 230 to 14,000 ft, 240 to 20,000, 265 to 34,000, M0.83 above.
  • TA: altitude on QNH. TL: lowest FL above TA, set by ATC. India: TA 4,000 ft, TL FL 50.
  • Climbing: 1013.2 at TA. Descending: QNH at TL.

    1. The IAF
    2. The FAF
    3. The MAPt
