When you may fly VFR in India and when you must fly IFR: the VMC visibility and cloud table, ceiling and visibility at controlled aerodromes, VFR hours and height limits, special VFR, minimum heights, changing between VFR and IFR, and the semicircular cruising level rule with RVSM.

  • In the lowest band in Classes F and G, the ATS authority may allow a lower flight visibility for aircraft flying slowly enough to see and avoid traffic and obstacles, or where little traffic is expected. Helicopters may be allowed even less.
  • VFR flights must not take off, land, or enter the aerodrome traffic zone or circuit at an aerodrome inside a control zone when the ceiling is below 1,500 ft or the ground visibility is below 5 km, unless ATC clears them.

Old exam answer

Older books give the reduced visibility in Classes F and G as 3,000 m. The current ICAO figure is 1,500 m. Read the question carefully, and if it asks about India, follow the AIP figure your course gives.

Memory trick

Remember 8, 5, 5: 8 km high up, 5 km lower down. Cloud is 1,500 and 1,000 everywhere, except low down in F and G where clear of cloud with the surface in sight is enough.

Visual Flight Rules

  • Hours: in India, VFR flights operate from 20 minutes before sunrise to 20 minutes after sunset. ATC may exempt local flights and flying club training flights it has cleared.
  • Unless the ATS authority allows, VFR flights must not fly: above FL 150, at transonic or supersonic speeds, or more than 100 nm out to sea from the coast inside controlled airspace.
  • VFR above FL 290 is never allowed where 1,000 ft vertical separation is used above FL 290.
  • VFR in level cruise above 3,000 ft above ground or water uses the VFR cruising levels for its track.
  • VFR flights must follow ATC instructions when flying in Classes B, C and D, as part of aerodrome traffic at controlled aerodromes, and as special VFR flights.
  • Where the ATS authority says so, a VFR flight keeps a listening watch on the right frequency and reports its position.

If a controlled VFR flight finds it cannot stay in VMC, the pilot should: ask for a changed clearance to go on in VMC by another route or to another aerodrome, or land at the nearest suitable aerodrome, or, inside a control zone, ask for a special VFR clearance, or ask for a clearance to fly IFR.

To change from VFR to IFR, tell ATS the changes to the flight plan (or file one), and get a clearance before flying IFR in controlled airspace.

Special VFR

  • A special VFR flight is a VFR flight that ATC has cleared to fly inside a control zone in weather below VMC.
  • Allowed only when traffic permits, with approval of the unit giving approach control. Each request is handled on its own.
  • In India the ground visibility must be at least 1,500 m. Certain helicopters may be allowed with 1,000 m.
  • It may be given to enter a control zone to land, to take off and leave it, to cross it, or to fly locally in it.
  • In India only pilots with an Instrument Rating, or an Assistant Flight Instructor or Flight Instructor rating, may fly special VFR.
  • The pilot stays clear of cloud and in sight of the surface. ATC separates special VFR flights from IFR flights.

Instrument Flight Rules

  • The aircraft must have the instruments and navigation equipment suited to the route.
  • Minimum level: not below the minimum flight altitude set by the State. Where none is set, at least 2,000 ft above the highest obstacle within 8 km of the estimated position over high terrain or mountains, and 1,000 ft above it elsewhere.
  • In controlled airspace, an IFR flight cruises at the level ATC clears, normally from the table of cruising levels.
  • Outside controlled airspace, an IFR flight in level cruise uses the cruising level for its track. Where the ATS authority requires, it keeps a listening watch and reports its position.
  • To change from IFR to VFR, tell ATS that the IFR flight is cancelled and give the flight plan changes. Do not cancel IFR just because you have flown into VMC, unless you expect to stay in uninterrupted VMC for a reasonable time.

Cruising levels: the semicircular rule

Below the transition altitude the same levels are flown as altitudes. In India VFR stops at FL 150, so FL 145 is the highest VFR level.
Magnetic trackIFRVFR
000 to 179 degrees (eastbound)Odd thousands: FL 50, 70, 90 and so on. With RVSM: FL 290, 310, 330, 350, 370, 390, 410Odd thousands plus 500 ft: FL 35, 55, 75, 95, 115, 135
180 to 359 degrees (westbound)Even thousands: FL 40, 60, 80 and so on. With RVSM: FL 300, 320, 340, 360, 380, 400Even thousands plus 500 ft: FL 45, 65, 85, 105, 125, 145
  • The levels use magnetic track, not heading.
  • RVSM (Reduced Vertical Separation Minimum) gives 1,000 ft separation between FL 290 and FL 410, both included. Without RVSM, separation above FL 290 is 2,000 ft.
  • Above FL 410, separation is 2,000 ft again: eastbound FL 450, 490, westbound FL 430, 470, 510.
  • To fly in RVSM airspace, an aircraft needs two independent altimetry systems, an altitude alerting system, an automatic height-keeping (autopilot altitude hold) system, and an SSR transponder with altitude reporting. The operator must also hold an RVSM approval.
  • RVSM may be suspended in more than moderate turbulence, and 2,000 ft separation used.

Worked example: Picking a level

  1. IFR, magnetic track 095: eastbound, odd. FL 70, 90, 110 and so on, or FL 330 in RVSM.
  2. IFR, magnetic track 270: westbound, even. FL 80, 100, or FL 360 in RVSM.
  3. VFR, magnetic track 179: eastbound, odd plus 500. FL 55, 75, 95.
  4. VFR, magnetic track 180: westbound, even plus 500. FL 65, 85, 105.

Memory trick

East is odd. Think of 'E' and 'O' both being round letters, or of the sun rising odd.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • VMC: 8 km at and above 10,000 ft, 5 km below. Cloud 1,500 m across, 1,000 ft up and down.
  • Low down in F and G: 5 km, clear of cloud, surface in sight.
  • Controlled aerodrome VFR: ceiling 1,500 ft, ground visibility 5 km.
  • Ceiling: lowest layer below 20,000 ft covering more than half the sky.
  • India VFR hours: 20 min before sunrise to 20 min after sunset.
  • India VFR: not above FL 150, not transonic, not more than 100 nm to sea in controlled airspace.
  • VFR minimum height: 1,000 ft above the highest obstacle within 600 m over towns, 500 ft elsewhere.
  • Special VFR: inside a CTR, ground visibility 1,500 m, approach control approval, pilot with IR or instructor rating.
  • IFR minimum: 2,000 ft over mountains, 1,000 ft elsewhere, above obstacles within 8 km.
  • Cruising levels use magnetic track. 000 to 179 odd, 180 to 359 even. VFR adds 500 ft.
  • RVSM: FL 290 to FL 410, 1,000 ft. Needs two altimeters, alerter, height-keeping, transponder, approval.
  • IFR to VFR: cancel IFR only if you expect to stay in VMC.

VMC minima

  • 8 km at and above 10,000 ft
  • 5 km below
  • Cloud: 1,500 m across, 1,000 ft vertical
  • Low in F and G: clear of cloud, surface in sight

VFR at controlled aerodromes

  • Ceiling at least 1,500 ft
  • Ground visibility at least 5 km
  • Otherwise only with ATC clearance

VFR limits in India

  • 20 min before sunrise to 20 min after sunset
  • Not above FL 150
  • Not transonic
  • Not more than 100 nm to sea

Minimum heights

  • VFR over towns: 1,000 ft within 600 m
  • VFR elsewhere: 500 ft
  • IFR mountains: 2,000 ft within 8 km
  • IFR elsewhere: 1,000 ft

Special VFR

  • Inside a control zone, below VMC
  • Ground visibility 1,500 m
  • Approach control approval
  • IR or instructor rating

Cruising levels

  • Magnetic track
  • 000 to 179: odd
  • 180 to 359: even
  • VFR: add 500 ft

RVSM

  • FL 290 to FL 410
  • 1,000 ft separation

Sample questions

5 of the 34 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. VMC minimum flight visibility at and above 10,000 ft AMSL is:

    1. 5 km
    2. 8 km
    3. 1,500 m
    4. 10 km
    Show answer

    Answer: B. 8 km at and above 10,000 ft, 5 km below.

  2. Q2. In India, VFR flights are normally allowed from:

    1. Sunrise to sunset
    2. 20 minutes before sunrise to 20 minutes after sunset
    3. 30 minutes before sunrise to 30 minutes after sunset
    4. Any time in VMC
    Show answer

    Answer: B. 20 minutes either side, with exemptions for local and club training flights cleared by ATC.

  3. Q3. In India, who may fly a special VFR flight?

    1. A pilot with an Instrument Rating, or an Assistant Flight Instructor or Flight Instructor rating
    2. Any licensed pilot
    3. Only an ATPL holder
    4. Only a student under supervision
    Show answer

    Answer: A. IR or an instructor rating is needed.

  4. Q4. An IFR flight on a magnetic track of 090 may cruise at:

    1. FL 90
    2. FL 80
    3. FL 95
    4. FL 100
    Show answer

    Answer: A. 000 to 179 uses odd thousands, so FL 90.

  5. Q5. An eastbound IFR aircraft in RVSM airspace may cruise at:

    1. FL 300
    2. FL 340
    3. FL 350
    4. FL 400
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Eastbound uses odd: FL 290, 310, 330, 350, 370, 390, 410.
