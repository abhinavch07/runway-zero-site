How ATC keeps aircraft apart: vertical separation, lateral and longitudinal separation by time and distance, separation near aerodromes, radar separation, wake turbulence spacing, the SSR transponder and its codes, ACAS resolution advisories, and speed control in India.

Who gets separated

  • All flights in Classes A and B.
  • IFR from IFR in Classes C, D and E.
  • IFR from VFR in Class C.
  • IFR from special VFR, and special VFR from each other when the ATS authority says so.
  • Separation is vertical or horizontal. Horizontal is lateral (side by side), longitudinal (one behind the other) or based on radar and other surveillance.
  • In Classes D and E, by day in VMC, ATC may clear a flight to climb or descend maintaining its own separation from one other aircraft, if that pilot agrees.
  • Essential traffic is controlled traffic not separated by the minimum. ATC gives essential traffic information to the aircraft concerned.

  • An aircraft is taken to be at its cleared level while its Mode C shows it within 300 ft of it (200 ft in RVSM airspace).
  • An aircraft has left a level when Mode C shows it has moved more than 300 ft in the expected direction.
  • An aircraft may be cleared to a level just vacated by another once that one reports leaving it, except in severe turbulence, when both are in the same holding pattern, or when the climb rates differ a lot.
  • Cruise climb is not used in Indian FIRs.

  • Crossing tracks, same level: 15 minutes, or 10 minutes with good navaids.
  • Reciprocal tracks without lateral separation: vertical separation must exist from 10 minutes before to 10 minutes after the time they are expected to pass.
  • Climbing or descending on the same track through another's level: 15 minutes, 10 minutes with good navaids, or 5 minutes if the level change starts within 10 minutes of the second aircraft reporting over the same point.

Departures and arrivals

Departing aircraftMinimum between take-offs
Tracks diverge by at least 45 degrees straight after take-off1 minute
Same track, leading aircraft 40 kt or more faster2 minutes
Same track, the second will climb through the level of the first5 minutes
  • A take-off normally starts only after the aircraft ahead has crossed the end of the runway or started a turn, or all landing aircraft are clear of the runway.
  • A landing aircraft normally crosses the threshold only after the departure ahead has crossed the runway end or turned, or landed aircraft are clear. Clearance to land is not given until the aircraft ahead has crossed the threshold, and it includes the runway designator.
  • Separation near an aerodrome may be reduced when the tower can see each aircraft all the time, or when pilots can see each other and say they will keep their own separation.
  • A visual approach may be flown by an IFR flight that can see the runway or the aircraft ahead. ATC still separates it from other traffic, but the pilot is responsible for terrain clearance.

Radar and surveillance

  • Primary radar (PSR) shows a blip from the reflected pulse. It needs nothing in the aircraft but gives no identity or level.
  • Secondary radar (SSR) sends an interrogation that the aircraft's transponder answers. Mode A gives the code. Mode C adds pressure altitude. Mode S adds a unique address and data.
  • ADS-B is broadcast by the aircraft itself from its own navigation data, for anyone to receive. ADS-C sends similar data only to the units it has a contract with. Multilateration finds an aircraft from the timing of its signals at several ground stations.
  • Radar separation: normally 5 nm. It may be 3 nm where the system allows, and 2.5 nm between aircraft on the same final within 10 nm of the threshold. Some States use 10 nm at long range.
  • Radar contact means your aircraft has been identified. ATC gives radar vectors as magnetic headings. While vectoring an IFR flight, ATC gives terrain clearance.
  • Resume own navigation means you are back in charge of your own navigation.

  • Keep the transponder on in flight at all times, inside or outside SSR airspace, unless ATC says otherwise.
  • Read back the mode and code when told to set them.
  • Press IDENT only when ATC asks.
  • Codes use the digits 0 to 7 only, so 8 and 9 never appear.
  • When changing codes, avoid passing through 7500, 7600 or 7700 by mistake. On sets without a standby switch, select STBY, change, then back to ON.
  • If ATC sees Mode C off by more than 300 ft, it may ask you to switch Mode C off.

ACAS (TCAS)

  • ACAS uses the SSR transponders of other aircraft to warn of collisions. ACAS II gives vertical resolution advisories only.
  • A traffic advisory (TA) is a warning only. Do not manoeuvre on a TA alone. Look for the traffic.
  • A resolution advisory (RA): respond at once, as shown, unless that is unsafe. Follow the RA even if ATC says something different. Never manoeuvre opposite to it.
  • Tell ATC as soon as possible about any RA that made you leave your clearance. Go back to the clearance when clear of conflict.

Memory trick

Wake is worst behind an aircraft that is heavy, clean and slow, so the gaps grow as the leader gets bigger and the follower smaller.

Speed control in India

  • All aircraft below 10,000 ft: not more than 250 kt IAS.
  • Arriving aircraft below 10,000 ft within 16 nm of the aerodrome's VOR/DME: not more than 220 kt IAS, unless ATC says otherwise.
  • ATC may add more limits, or cancel them with 'no speed restriction'.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Vertical: 1,000 ft to FL 290 and in RVSM to FL 410, 2,000 ft above.
  • Mode C level tolerance 300 ft (200 ft RVSM).
  • Lateral: VOR 15 degrees, NDB 30, DR 45, all with one aircraft 15 nm out.
  • Same track below 45 degrees, reciprocal above 135 degrees.
  • Time, same track: 15 basic, 10 with navaids, 5 if leader 20 kt faster, 3 if 40 kt faster. Mach technique 10.
  • DME: 20 nm, or 10 nm if leader 20 kt faster.
  • Departures: 1 min (45 degrees diverging), 2 min (40 kt faster), 5 min (climbing through).
  • Radar: 5 nm, may be 3 nm, 2.5 nm on final within 10 nm.
  • Squawk 2000 no code, 7500 hijack, 7600 radio, 7700 emergency. Digits 0 to 7.
  • TA: no manoeuvre. RA: follow at once, even against ATC, then tell ATC.
  • Wake radar: heavy behind heavy 4, medium 5, light 6. Light behind medium 5.
  • Wake time: 2 min behind heavy, 3 min light behind medium or from an intermediate point.
  • India: 250 kt below 10,000 ft, 220 kt within 16 nm for arrivals.

Sample questions

5 of the 32 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. Separation is given between all flights in Class:

    1. A and B
    2. C and D
    3. D and E
    4. F and G
    Show answer

    Answer: A. In A and B every flight is separated from every other.

  2. Q2. For longitudinal separation, two tracks differing by 30 degrees are treated as:

    1. Same track
    2. Crossing tracks
    3. Reciprocal tracks
    4. Parallel tracks
    Show answer

    Answer: A. Less than 45 degrees counts as the same track.

  3. Q3. Two departures on the same track, the first 40 kt faster. The minimum between take-offs is:

    1. 1 minute
    2. 2 minutes
    3. 5 minutes
    4. 10 minutes
    Show answer

    Answer: B. 2 minutes.

  4. Q4. The IDENT button should be pressed:

    1. On every call
    2. At each reporting point
    3. When entering a new FIR
    4. Only when ATC asks
    Show answer

    Answer: D. Only on request, or it confuses the controller's screen.

  5. Q5. Using radar, the wake turbulence minimum for a light aircraft behind a heavy on approach is:

    1. 4 nm
    2. 5 nm
    3. 6 nm
    4. 8 nm
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Heavy behind heavy 4, medium 5, light 6.
