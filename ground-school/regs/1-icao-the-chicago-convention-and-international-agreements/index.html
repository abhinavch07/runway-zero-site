How international civil aviation is organised: the Chicago Convention and its key articles, ICAO and how it makes rules (Standards, Recommended Practices, PANS and the 19 Annexes), the freedoms of the air, the conventions on crimes on board and airline liability, the powers of the pilot-in-command, and leasing terms.

From Paris to Chicago

  • Paris Convention, 1919: the first international agreement on air navigation. It set up ICAN (International Commission for Air Navigation), the body before ICAO.
  • Chicago Convention, 1944 (Convention on International Civil Aviation): signed on 7 December 1944, it has 96 articles. It replaced the Paris Convention and created ICAO.
  • The Convention came into force on 4 April 1947, the day ICAO formally came into being. Later in 1947 ICAO became a specialised agency of the United Nations.
  • India was one of the original signatories and has ratified the Convention.

Key articles of the Chicago Convention

3 Civil and State aircraftThe Convention applies to civil aircraft only. Aircraft used in military, customs and police services are State aircraft
5 Non-scheduled flightsMay fly into or across a State and make stops for non-traffic purposes without prior permission, subject to that State's rules

10 Customs airportsAircraft entering a State must land at a designated customs airport, unless allowed to cross without landing
12 Rules of the airEach State keeps its rules close to ICAO's. Over the high seas, the ICAO rules (Annex 2) apply without change
17, 18 NationalityAn aircraft has the nationality of the State where it is registered, and cannot be validly registered in more than one State
20 Display of marksAircraft on international flights carry their nationality and registration marks
25 Aircraft in distressA State must help any aircraft in distress over its land
26 AccidentsThe State where the accident happens starts the inquiry. The State of Registry may send observers

33 RecognitionCertificates and licences from the State of Registry are recognised by other States if they meet ICAO minimum standards

Documents to carry (Article 29) on an international flight: the certificate of registration, the certificate of airworthiness, the crew licences, the journey log book, the aircraft radio station licence, a list of passengers with where they boarded and where they are going, and for cargo a manifest and detailed declarations.

Memory trick

Article 1 is the first thing a country owns: its sky. Article 7 sounds like "cabota-seven": domestic traffic is for home airlines only.

ICAO and how it is organised

  • Headquarters: Montreal, Canada. ICAO has 193 member States (Contracting States).
  • Assembly: all Contracting States, one State one vote. The sovereign body. Meets at least once every three years.
  • Council: 36 States elected by the Assembly for three years. A permanent body. It adopts the Standards and Recommended Practices and makes them Annexes. Its President is elected by the Council.
  • Air Navigation Commission (ANC): 19 members appointed by the Council. It works out the technical Annexes and recommends them to the Council.
  • Other committees include the Air Transport Committee, the Legal Committee, the Finance Committee and the committee on unlawful interference.
  • Secretariat: the staff, headed by the Secretary General.
  • Seven regional offices. India is served by the Asia and Pacific office in Bangkok.

How ICAO makes rules

TypeMeaningIf a State does not follow it
StandardNecessary for safety or regularity. Written with "shall"The State must notify ICAO of the difference (Article 38)
Recommended PracticeDesirable. Written with "should"States are invited to notify, but it is not compulsory
PANS (Procedures for Air Navigation Services)Detailed procedures approved by the Council, such as Doc 4444 (PANS-ATM) and Doc 8168 (PANS-OPS)Recommended for worldwide use
SUPPS (Regional Supplementary Procedures)Procedures for one region only, in Doc 7030Approved by the Council for that region

Article 38: a State that finds it cannot comply with a Standard must notify ICAO immediately. If an Annex is amended and the State will not follow the change, it must notify ICAO within 60 days of the amendment being adopted. The State also publishes its differences in its AIP (in India, AIP India GEN 1.7).

The freedoms of the air

Two more agreements were signed at Chicago on 7 December 1944 to give airlines rights over other countries. Each right is a freedom of the air.

AgreementFreedomsIndia
International Air Services Transit Agreement (the two freedoms agreement)1st and 2nd: the technical freedomsAccepted
International Air Transport Agreement (the five freedoms agreement)1st to 5th: adds the commercial freedomsNot accepted. India grants 3rd to 5th only through bilateral agreements
FreedomThe privilege toExample (Indian airline)
1stFly across a foreign country without landingDelhi to Singapore, overflying Bangladesh and Myanmar
2ndLand for non-traffic purposes: fuel, repairs. No passengers, cargo or mail on or offDelhi to London with a fuel stop in the Gulf
3rdPut down traffic taken on in the home countryPassengers flown Mumbai to Dubai and set down there
4thTake on traffic going to the home countryPassengers picked up in Dubai for Mumbai
5thCarry traffic between two foreign countries on a flight that starts or ends at homeDelhi to Bangkok to Tokyo, selling Bangkok to Tokyo seats

Further rights are described but are not in any multilateral agreement: the 6th (between two foreign countries via home), the 7th (between two foreign countries without touching home), and cabotage, the right to carry domestic traffic inside another country (the 8th and 9th). India does not allow cabotage by foreign airlines.

Old exam answer

Some notes give the Montreal Convention on unlawful acts as 1974. It was signed in Montreal on 23 September 1971.

Powers of the pilot-in-command

Under the Tokyo Convention, an aircraft is in flight from the moment all its external doors are closed after boarding until any such door is opened for getting off. During this time, if the commander has reasonable grounds to believe a person has committed, or is about to commit, an offence or an act that endangers safety or good order on board, the commander may:

  • Impose reasonable measures, including restraint, on that person.
  • Require or authorise other crew members to help, and request or authorise, but not require, passengers to help.
  • Disembark the person in any State where the aircraft lands, or deliver them to the competent authorities if the act is serious.
  • Tell the authorities of the landing State that a restrained person is on board, before landing if possible.

The commander, crew and passengers who act within the Convention cannot be held responsible in later proceedings for those actions. Generally the State of Registry has jurisdiction over offences on board. The pilot-in-command is responsible for the safety of the aircraft and everyone on board, and has the final authority on its operation (Annex 2 and Annex 6).

Airline liability and damage on the ground

AgreementDeals withIndia
Warsaw Convention, 1929 (with the Hague Protocol, 1955)An international airline's liability to passengers and for baggage and cargo. Standard tickets and documentsRatified
Montreal Convention, 1999Brings the Warsaw rules up to date and replaces them between States that join. No upper limit for death or injury of a passengerJoined in 2009
Rome Convention, 1952Damage to third parties on the surface caused by foreign aircraft, such as a part falling offNot ratified
Geneva Convention, 1948International recognition of rights in aircraft (ownership, mortgage, hire purchase)
Cape Town Convention, 2001International interests in aircraft, engines and helicopters: finance and leasing, including fast repossession and de-registrationJoined in 2008. Given effect by the Protection of Interests in Aircraft Objects Act, 2025

In India these airline liability rules are applied through the Carriage by Air Act, 1972 (amended in 2009 for the Montreal Convention). It covers both international and domestic carriage.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Paris 1919 set up ICAN. Chicago, 7 Dec 1944, 96 articles, in force 4 April 1947, created ICAO.
  • ICAO: Montreal, 193 States, UN specialised agency. India's regional office: Bangkok.
  • Assembly: all States, at least every 3 years. Council: 36 States, adopts SARPs. ANC: 19 members.
  • Standard = shall, notify differences. Recommended Practice = should.
  • Article 38: notify differences immediately, or within 60 days of an amendment.
  • 19 Annexes. Annex 2 Rules of the Air has Standards only.
  • Art 1 sovereignty, Art 7 cabotage, Art 12 ICAO rules over the high seas, Art 17 nationality = State of Registry, Art 26 accident inquiry by State of occurrence, Art 29 documents.
  • Freedoms 1 and 2: Transit Agreement, India accepted. 1 to 5: Transport Agreement, India did not.
  • 1 overfly, 2 technical stop, 3 put down home traffic, 4 pick up for home, 5 between two foreign States.
  • Tokyo 1963 on board offences, Hague 1970 hijacking, Montreal 1971 unlawful acts, 1988 airports, 1991 plastic explosives.
  • Tokyo: in flight = doors closed to doors opened. Commander may require crew, only request passengers.
  • Warsaw 1929 and Montreal 1999: airline liability. Rome 1952: third parties on the ground, India not ratified.
  • Cape Town 2001: aircraft finance and leasing. Geneva 1948: rights in aircraft.
  • Dry lease: no crew, lessee's AOC. Wet lease: with crew, lessor's AOC.

Sample questions

5 of the 41 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The International Civil Aviation Organization was set up by the:

    1. Paris Convention, 1919
    2. Chicago Convention, 1944
    3. Warsaw Convention, 1929
    4. Tokyo Convention, 1963
    Show answer

    Answer: B. The Convention on International Civil Aviation, signed at Chicago in 1944, created ICAO.

  2. Q2. The Air Navigation Commission has:

    1. 36 members elected by the Assembly
    2. 19 members appointed by the Council
    3. All Contracting States
    4. 9 members from the founding States
    Show answer

    Answer: B. The ANC's 19 members are appointed by the Council and work on the technical Annexes.

  3. Q3. A State that will not follow an amendment to an Annex must notify ICAO within:

    1. 30 days
    2. 45 days
    3. 60 days
    4. 6 months
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Within 60 days of the amendment being adopted.

  4. Q4. An airline lands in a neighbouring State only to refuel, with no passengers getting on or off. This uses the:

    1. First freedom
    2. Fifth freedom
    3. Third freedom
    4. Second freedom
    Show answer

    Answer: D. A landing for non-traffic purposes, such as fuel or repairs, is the second freedom.

  5. Q5. For the Tokyo Convention, an aircraft is considered in flight:

    1. From take-off power to touchdown
    2. From engine start to engine shutdown
    3. Only while airborne
    4. From the moment all external doors are closed after boarding until any such door is opened for getting off
    Show answer

    Answer: D. The commander's powers apply from doors closed to doors opened.
