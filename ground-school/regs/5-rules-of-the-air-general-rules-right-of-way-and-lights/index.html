Annex 2 and how it applies in India: the PIC's duties, things you may not do without permission, avoiding collisions, who gives way to whom in the air and on the ground, aircraft lights and what they tell you at night, and flying at and near an aerodrome.

Where the rules apply

  • The international Rules of the Air are in Annex 2. In India, Rule 12 of the Bharatiya Vayuyan Niyam, 2026 (which replace the Aircraft Rules, 1937 from 1 January 2027) makes every pilot follow the Rules of the Air issued by the DGCA in line with Annex 2.
  • They apply to all aircraft over India, and to Indian registered aircraft wherever they are, as long as they do not clash with the rules of the State being flown over.
  • Over the high seas, Annex 2 applies without any exception. No State can change it there.
  • An aircraft flies under either the Visual Flight Rules (VFR) or the Instrument Flight Rules (IFR). Both also follow the general rules in this module.

The pilot-in-command

  • The PIC is responsible for operating the aircraft under the Rules of the Air, whether or not he is at the controls.
  • The PIC may depart from the rules when that is absolutely needed for safety.
  • The PIC has the final authority over how the aircraft is used while he is in command.
  • Before a flight, the PIC must study all the information he needs. For a flight away from the aerodrome and for every IFR flight, this includes weather reports and forecasts, fuel needed, and a plan B if the flight cannot be completed as planned.
  • No one may fly, or work as crew, while under the influence of alcohol or any drug that affects his ability to do the job safely. Problematic use of such substances is not allowed for anyone in a safety-sensitive job.
  • No one may fly an aircraft in a careless or reckless way that puts life or property at risk.

Aerobatic flight means manoeuvres done on purpose that involve a sudden change of attitude, an unusual attitude, or an unusual change in speed, not needed for normal flight.

Avoiding collisions

  • No rule takes away the PIC's duty to do whatever is needed to avoid a collision, including following an ACAS resolution advisory.
  • Keep a lookout at all times, whatever the flight rules or class of airspace, and whether on the ground or in the air.
  • No aircraft may fly so close to another that it creates a collision hazard.
  • The aircraft with the right of way keeps its heading and speed. The other one gives way and must not pass over, under or in front of it unless well clear, keeping wake turbulence in mind.

An aircraft is overtaking when it comes up from behind on a line within 70 degrees of the other's centre line. At night it would see only the other's white tail light, not its red or green wing lights.

Memory trick

The less an aircraft can manoeuvre, the more right of way it has. A balloon cannot steer at all, so everyone gives way to it.

On the ground and on water

  • Two aircraft taxiing head-on: both stop, or where possible turn right to keep well clear.
  • Two aircraft converging on the ground: the one with the other on its right gives way.
  • An aircraft being overtaken on the ground has the right of way. The overtaking one keeps well clear.
  • A taxiing aircraft stops and holds at every runway-holding position unless ATC clears it on, and stops at a lit stop bar, going on only when the lights are switched off.
  • On the movement area, an aircraft taxiing has right of way over vehicles, including a vehicle towing an aircraft. Vehicles towing aircraft give way to aircraft landing, taking off or taxiing.
  • On water, aircraft follow rules like ships: give way to the one on the right, both turn right when head-on, and the one being overtaken has the right of way.

Aircraft lights

From sunset to sunrise, or at any other time the DGCA decides, every aircraft in flight must show anti-collision lights and navigation lights. No other light may be shown if it could be mistaken for these.

LightColour and whereShows over
Left (port) wingtipRed110 degrees, from dead ahead to 110 degrees left
Right (starboard) wingtipGreen110 degrees, from dead ahead to 110 degrees right
TailWhite140 degrees to the rear, 70 degrees each side
Anti-collisionRed flashing beacon, or white strobesAll round, to attract attention
  • Navigation lights show the relative path of the aircraft to someone watching. Anti-collision lights are there to catch the eye.
  • On the movement area, at night, an aircraft must also show lights for the ends of its structure unless it is stopped and well lit, and lights showing its engines are running.
  • The anti-collision beacon goes on before the engines start and stays on while they run.
  • A pilot may switch off or dim strobes that dazzle others or himself, such as in cloud, but must then still keep the navigation lights on.
  • If a navigation light fails at night, tell ATC and land as soon as practicable to get it fixed. If an anti-collision light fails, the flight may go on, but it must be fixed before the next flight.

Memory trick

Red on the right, give way to the right. A red light that does not move across your windscreen means a collision course.

Flying at and near an aerodrome

  • Watch other aerodrome traffic to avoid collisions.
  • Join or avoid the traffic pattern that other aircraft are flying.
  • Make all turns to the left when approaching to land and after take-off, unless told otherwise. That is a left-hand circuit.
  • Land and take off into wind, unless safety, the runway layout or ATC needs a different direction.
  • In India, where no aerodrome traffic zone is set up, the vicinity of an aerodrome for VFR flights is 5 nm from the aerodrome reference point and up to 3,000 ft above it.
  • Night in India means the time from 30 minutes after sunset to 30 minutes before sunrise.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Rules of the Air: Annex 2. India: Rule 12 of the 2026 rules. Over the high seas, Annex 2 applies without exception.
  • PIC is responsible whether at the controls or not. May depart from the rules only for safety.
  • Head-on: both turn right. Converging: the one with the other on its right gives way.
  • Overtaking: within 70 degrees of the tail. The one being overtaken has the right of way. Overtaker turns right.
  • Order: power-driven gives way to airships, gliders, balloons. Airships to gliders, balloons. Gliders to balloons. All power-driven give way to aircraft towing.
  • Landing aircraft have priority. Of two landing, the lower has priority. Forced landing has priority over all.
  • On the ground, head-on: both stop or turn right. Stop at runway-holding positions and lit stop bars.
  • Nav lights: red left 110, green right 110, white tail 140.
  • Lights from sunset to sunrise. Anti-collision on before engine start.
  • Red and green: head-on. Steady red: you give way.
  • Circuit turns: left unless told otherwise. Land and take off into wind.
  • Simulated instrument flight: dual controls and a safety pilot.
  • India: aerobatics not within 2 nm of an aerodrome below 6,000 ft AGL, finish by 2,000 ft AGL.
  • Night in India: 30 minutes after sunset to 30 minutes before sunrise.

Sample questions

5 of the 35 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The ICAO Annex that contains the Rules of the Air is:

    1. Annex 2
    2. Annex 6
    3. Annex 11
    4. Annex 14
    Show answer

    Answer: A. Annex 2 is Rules of the Air.

  2. Q2. When overtaking in the air, the overtaking aircraft should:

    1. Pass on the left
    2. Keep out of the way by turning right
    3. Climb above the other
    4. Flash its landing light and continue
    Show answer

    Answer: B. The aircraft being overtaken has the right of way. The overtaker alters heading to the right.

  3. Q3. An aircraft taxiing on the manoeuvring area must give way to:

    1. Aircraft taking off or about to take off
    2. Vehicles
    3. Aircraft parked on the apron
    4. Ground staff walking
    Show answer

    Answer: A. Aircraft taking off or about to take off have priority over taxiing ones.

  4. Q4. At night you see a red light ahead that stays in the same place on your windscreen. You should:

    1. Hold heading, you have right of way
    2. Turn left
    3. Climb at once
    4. Give way, turning right
    Show answer

    Answer: D. A steady red means the other aircraft is on your right on a converging path. You give way, normally by turning right.

  5. Q5. In India, aerobatics within 2 nm of an aerodrome are allowed only above:

    1. 2,000 ft AGL
    2. 3,000 ft AGL
    3. 6,000 ft AGL
    4. 10,000 ft AGL
    Show answer

    Answer: C. 6,000 ft above ground. They must also end by 2,000 ft above ground.
