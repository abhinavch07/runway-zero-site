Annex 6 and India's operations rules: documents on board, the PIC's duties, fuel planning, alternates, oxygen, flight recorders and ELTs, MEL and MMEL, noise abatement, fire classes, and the flight and duty time limits for pilots.

Annex 6 and the PIC

Annex 6 partCovers
Part IInternational commercial air transport, aeroplanes
Part IIInternational general aviation, aeroplanes
Part IIIHelicopters
  • In India, operations rules are in CAR Section 8.
  • The PIC is responsible for the safety of everyone on board from the moment the doors close, and for the operation and safety of the aircraft.
  • The PIC makes sure the checklists are followed, reports accidents, keeps the journey log and reports defects.
  • No one but the flight crew goes on the flight deck unless allowed by the operator and the PIC.
  • The flight deck door stays locked in flight on passenger aircraft that have one.

Fuel planning

PartWhat it covers
Taxi fuelStart-up and taxi
Trip fuelTake-off, climb, cruise, descent, approach and landing at the destination
Contingency fuelUnplanned extras: normally 5% of trip fuel, but not less than 5 minutes' holding at 1,500 ft above the destination
Destination alternate fuelMissed approach, climb, cruise, descent and approach to the alternate
Final reserve fuelTurbine: 30 minutes holding at 1,500 ft above the aerodrome. Piston: 45 minutes
Additional fuelExtra fuel to cope with an engine failure or pressurisation loss at the worst point, if the rest is not enough
Discretionary (extra) fuelAnything more the PIC decides to carry
  • If there is no destination alternate, carry at least 15 minutes' holding at 1,500 ft above the destination, plus the reserves.
  • For an isolated aerodrome with no alternate: turbine aircraft carry fuel for 2 hours at normal cruise including final reserve. Piston aircraft carry 45 minutes plus 15% of flight time, or 2 hours, whichever is less.
  • A take-off alternate is needed when the weather at departure is below landing minima. For a twin, it must be within 1 hour flying at one-engine cruise speed. For three or four engines, within 2 hours.
  • Declare 'MINIMUM FUEL' when any change to your clearance could make you land below final reserve. Declare MAYDAY FUEL when you expect to land with less than final reserve.

Oxygen

Unpressurised aeroplane, cabin altitudeOxygen for
Below 10,000 ftNot needed
10,000 to 13,000 ft, for more than 30 minutesAll crew and 10% of passengers
Above 13,000 ftAll crew and all passengers

A pressurised aeroplane carries enough oxygen for everyone for any time the cabin pressure would fall below 700 hPa (about 10,000 ft) after a pressurisation failure, enough for a descent to a safe level.

Recorders, ELTs and the MEL

  • A flight data recorder (FDR) keeps at least the last 25 hours.
  • A cockpit voice recorder (CVR) keeps at least the last 2 hours. Aeroplanes first certified from 1 January 2022 keep 25 hours.
  • Recorders are switched off after an accident or incident so the data is kept for the investigators.
  • Aeroplanes allowed to carry more than 19 passengers carry at least two ELTs, one of them automatic. ELTs transmit on 406 MHz and 121.5 MHz.
  • The Master Minimum Equipment List (MMEL) is drawn up by the manufacturer. The Minimum Equipment List (MEL) is made by the operator from it, approved by the DGCA, and may be stricter but never more lenient.
  • The MEL tells the PIC whether a flight may start with an item unserviceable.

Noise abatement

  • Noise abatement procedures are not started below 800 ft above the aerodrome.
  • They are not used when wind shear is reported or suspected.
  • Noise is not the reason for choosing a runway when the crosswind is more than 15 kt, the tailwind more than 5 kt, or the runway is not clean and dry.
  • Noise abatement on landing never uses reverse thrust differently from normal, and the aircraft must be in final landing configuration by the outer marker or 5 nm from the threshold.

Fire classes

ClassBurningUse
ASolids: paper, wood, cloth, seatsWater, Halon
BLiquids: fuel, oil, hydraulic fluidFoam, Halon, CO2, dry powder. Not water
CElectrical equipmentHalon, CO2, dry powder. Not water
DMetals: magnesium, sodiumSpecial dry powder only

A Halon (BCF) extinguisher works on most cabin fires. In a closed space, crew should wear protective breathing equipment.

Flight and duty time limits

TermMeaning
Flight timeFrom when the aircraft first moves to take off until it finally stops after landing. Block to block
Flight duty period (FDP)From reporting for duty for a flight until the engines stop at the end of the last flight
Duty periodFrom reporting for any duty until free of all duties
Rest periodA continuous time free of all duties, before a duty
Night dutyAny duty that touches 0000 to 0600 local time
Cumulative flight time (two-pilot crew)Maximum
7 consecutive days35 hours
28 consecutive days100 hours
90 consecutive days300 hours
365 consecutive days1,000 hours
  • In a day (24 hours), a two-pilot crew may fly at most 8 hours. The longest FDP, about 13 hours, shrinks as the number of landings goes up.
  • Night operations have a shorter FDP and allow only a few landings.
  • In unforeseen circumstances the PIC, with the crew's agreement, may extend flight time by up to 1 hour and FDP by up to 2 hours. Rest after it goes up too.
  • Operators keep FDTL records for 18 months.
  • Crew must report if they are too tired to fly. Flying while fatigued is not allowed.

Old exam answer

The DGCA revised the FDTL CAR in 2024, bringing in a 48 hour weekly rest, a wider night duty window and fewer night landings, phased in from 2025. Older books give older figures. Check the current CAR Section 7 Series J Part I before your exam.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Annex 6: Part I commercial, Part II general aviation, Part III helicopters. India: CAR Section 8.
  • Contingency 5% of trip (min 5 min hold). Final reserve: turbine 30 min, piston 45 min, at 1,500 ft.
  • No destination alternate: 15 min hold extra. Isolated aerodrome turbine: 2 hours.
  • Take-off alternate: twin within 1 hour one-engine. 3-4 engines 2 hours.
  • Oxygen: 10,000 to 13,000 ft over 30 min: crew + 10% pax. Above 13,000: all.
  • FDR 25 hours, CVR 2 hours (25 for new aircraft). Over 19 pax: 2 ELTs, one automatic.
  • MMEL: manufacturer. MEL: operator, DGCA approved, never more lenient.
  • Noise abatement: not below 800 ft, not with windshear, not if crosswind over 15 kt or tailwind over 5 kt.
  • Fire: A solids, B liquids, C electrical, D metals. No water on B or C.
  • Flight time is block to block. Night duty: 0000 to 0600.
  • Limits: 35 h in 7 days, 100 h in 28, 300 h in 90, 1,000 h in 365.
  • Extension: flight time 1 hour, FDP 2 hours. Records 18 months.

Sample questions

5 of the 30 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. Annex 6 Part II covers:

    1. International commercial air transport, aeroplanes
    2. International general aviation, aeroplanes
    3. Helicopters
    4. Dangerous goods
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Part I commercial, Part II general aviation, Part III helicopters.

  2. Q2. A pilot expects any change to the clearance could make him land with less than final reserve. He should declare:

    1. MAYDAY
    2. MINIMUM FUEL
    3. PAN PAN
    4. Nothing
    Show answer

    Answer: B. MINIMUM FUEL is an advisory. MAYDAY FUEL is for landing below final reserve.

  3. Q3. The MEL is prepared by:

    1. The manufacturer
    2. The PIC
    3. The ICAO
    4. The operator, and approved by the DGCA
    Show answer

    Answer: D. The manufacturer makes the MMEL. The operator makes the MEL from it.

  4. Q4. A fire involving burning magnesium is Class:

    1. A
    2. B
    3. C
    4. D
    Show answer

    Answer: D. D is metals, which need special powder.

  5. Q5. A two-pilot crew may fly at most how many hours in a day?

    1. 6
    2. 12
    3. 10
    4. 8
    Show answer

    Answer: D. 8 hours of flight time.
