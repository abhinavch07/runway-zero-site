The two classic radio aids: the NDB with the aircraft's ADF, relative bearings, the RBI and RMI and the ADF's errors, and the VOR with radials, the CDI and TO/FROM flag, the RMI, range, accuracy and the VOR's errors.

NDB and ADF

  • A non-directional beacon (NDB) on the ground sends a signal in all directions on LF/MF, about 190 to 1,750 kHz. It is identified by 2 or 3 letters in Morse.
  • The aircraft's automatic direction finder (ADF) finds the direction the signal comes from, using a loop antenna and a sense antenna.
  • Emission types: NON A1A (identity by switching the carrier on and off, the pilot selects BFO to hear it) and NON A2A (identity by a tone, BFO not needed).
  • Range depends on power: over the sea, roughly 3 x square root of power (W) nm, and 2 x over land. Most NDBs are rated by a published range, protected from interference by day.
  • Accuracy within the published range by day is about plus or minus 5 degrees.
IndicatorShows
RBI (relative bearing indicator)A fixed card with 0 at the top. The needle gives the relative bearing of the NDB from the nose
RMI (radio magnetic indicator)A card turned by the compass, so the needle head gives the QDM (magnetic bearing to the station) and the tail the QDR

QDM = magnetic heading + relative bearing (subtract 360 if over 360)

Worked example: Reading the ADF

  1. Heading 060(M), RBI shows relative bearing 030. QDM = 060 + 030 = 090. QDR = 270.
  2. Heading 300(M), relative bearing 090. 300 + 090 = 390, minus 360 = QDM 030.
  3. To find the true bearing of the NDB from the aircraft for plotting, use true heading + relative bearing. Then add 180 to get the bearing from the NDB.
ADF errorCause and effect
Night effectSky waves come back at night and mix with the ground wave. The needle wanders. Worst around dusk and dawn
Coastal refractionThe wave bends as it crosses the coast at an angle. Least when the signal crosses at 90 degrees. Fly high, use beacons near the coast
ThunderstormsThe needle may point to a storm
Mountain effectReflections from high ground
Quadrantal errorThe aircraft's metal bends the signal. Biggest on relative bearings of 045, 135, 225, 315. Corrected when fitted
Station interferenceTwo beacons on near frequencies, worse at night
Dip errorWhen the aircraft banks, the loop tilts. Read bearings with wings level

VOR

  • A VHF omni-directional range (VOR) works on 108.0 to 117.95 MHz and is identified by 3 letters in Morse.
  • It sends a reference signal (same phase in all directions) and a variable signal whose phase depends on direction. The phase difference is the magnetic bearing from the station: the radial.
  • A VOR is aligned to magnetic north at the station. Radials are QDRs.
  • A Doppler VOR (DVOR) works the same way for the pilot, but is much less affected by site errors.
  • Accuracy: the ground station within about 2 degrees. The total for the pilot, with the receiver, about plus or minus 5 degrees.
  • Range is line of sight, so it grows with height. There is a cone of confusion overhead where the signal is unreliable.
  • Errors: site error (reflections near the station), propagation error (reflections from terrain), and scalloping (the needle wobbles).
CDI partMeaning
OBS (course knob)Select the course (radial or its reciprocal) you want
Needle (CDI)Where the selected course is: fly towards the needle. Each dot is about 2 degrees (full scale about 10 degrees)
TO / FROM flagWhether the selected course, flown, takes you to or from the station. It depends on position, not heading
OFF flagNo usable signal
  • The CDI does not depend on heading. It shows where you are against the selected course. If you fly the opposite way with TO showing for the reciprocal course, the needle works backwards (reverse sensing).
  • To find which radial you are on: turn the OBS until the needle centres with a FROM flag. The course shown is your radial.
  • On an RMI, the VOR needle head shows the QDM and the tail the radial, whatever the heading.

Memory trick

Radial is FROM. A radial is like a spoke going out from the hub. You fly inbound on the reciprocal of the radial.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • NDB 190 to 1,750 kHz, 2 or 3 letter ident. A1A needs BFO. Accuracy about 5 degrees by day.
  • Range over sea about 3 x root power, over land 2 x.
  • QDM = MH + RB. True bearing to plot = TH + RB.
  • RBI: relative bearing. RMI: needle head QDM, tail QDR.
  • ADF errors: night effect (dusk and dawn), coastal refraction (least at 90 degrees), storms, mountains, quadrantal (045, 135...), interference, dip in a bank.
  • VOR 108.0 to 117.95 MHz, 3 letter ident, phase comparison, radials are magnetic from.
  • VOR accuracy about 5 degrees total. DVOR cuts site error. Cone of confusion overhead.
  • CDI: fly to the needle. Dot about 2 degrees, full scale 10. TO/FROM depends on position, not heading.

Sample questions

5 of the 26 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. NDBs operate on frequencies of about:

    1. 108 to 118 MHz
    2. 190 to 1,750 kHz
    3. 962 to 1,213 MHz
    4. 329 to 335 MHz
    Show answer

    Answer: B. LF and MF.

  2. Q2. Heading 060(M), relative bearing 030. The QDR is:

    1. 090
    2. 270
    3. 030
    4. 210
    Show answer

    Answer: B. QDM 090, so QDR is 270.

  3. Q3. ADF quadrantal error is greatest on relative bearings of:

    1. 000, 090, 180, 270
    2. It is constant
    3. 030 and 330
    4. 045, 135, 225, 315
    Show answer

    Answer: D. Caused by the airframe bending the signal.

  4. Q4. A VOR gives bearing by:

    1. Timing pulses
    2. Doppler shift only
    3. A rotating loop
    4. Comparing the phase of a reference and a variable signal
    Show answer

    Answer: D. The phase difference equals the radial.

  5. Q5. To find which radial you are on, turn the OBS until the needle centres with:

    1. A FROM flag
    2. A TO flag
    3. An OFF flag
    4. Any flag
    Show answer

    Answer: A. With FROM, the course shown is your radial.
