Why mass and centre of gravity matter, the effects of a forward or aft CG, datum, arm and moment, finding the CG and its position in % MAC, moving and adding loads, the mass terms from basic empty mass to landing mass, and how to find the most traffic load you can carry.

Why it matters

  • The centre of gravity (CG) is the point through which the whole weight of the aircraft acts. The aircraft balances there and turns about it in flight.
  • The manufacturer sets a forward and an aft CG limit, and maximum masses. Flying outside them can make the aircraft uncontrollable or overstress it.
CG near the forward limitCG near the aft limit
StabilityMore stable (longitudinally)Less stable. Beyond the aft limit it can become unstable
Control forcesHeavier: more effort to rotate and flareLighter: easy to over-control
Stall speedHigher (the tail pushes down more, so the wing carries more)Lower
Drag and fuelMore trim drag, more fuel, less rangeLess trim drag, better range
DangerMay not be able to raise the nose to rotate or flareMay not recover from a stall or spin

More mass means longer take-off and landing runs, a higher stall speed, poorer climb, less range and endurance, and more stress on the structure.

Datum, arm and moment

TermMeaning
DatumA reference point chosen by the manufacturer on the fore and aft axis, from which all arms are measured
Arm (balance arm)The distance of a mass from the datum. Aft of the datum is plus, forward is minus
MomentMass x arm
MACThe mean aerodynamic chord: a reference chord of the wing. CG is often given as a percentage of MAC from its leading edge (LEMAC)

CG (arm) = total moment / total mass

CG % MAC = (CG - LEMAC) / MAC length x 100

Shift of CG = mass moved x distance moved / total mass

Worked example: Finding and moving the CG

  1. Aircraft 1,000 kg at arm 2.0 m (moment 2,000). Pilot 80 kg at 1.5 m (120). Fuel 100 kg at 2.4 m (240). Total 1,180 kg, moment 2,360. CG = 2,360 / 1,180 = 2.0 m aft of datum.
  2. A 5,000 kg aircraft. A 100 kg box is moved 3 m aft. CG moves 100 x 3 / 5,000 = 0.06 m aft.
  3. LEMAC at 10 m, MAC 4 m, CG at 11 m. CG = (11 - 10) / 4 x 100 = 25% MAC.
  4. To bring the CG forward by 0.1 m on a 6,000 kg aircraft by moving cargo 5 m forward: mass = 6,000 x 0.1 / 5 = 120 kg.

Adding or removing a mass: work out the new total mass and new total moment, then divide. Or use: CG change = mass added x (its arm - old CG) / new total mass.

Mass terms

TermIs
Basic empty mass (BEM)The aircraft with its fixed equipment, unusable fuel, full engine oil and other fixed fluids
Dry operating mass (DOM)BEM + crew, their baggage, catering and items for the flight. No usable fuel, no traffic load
Traffic load (payload)Passengers, baggage and cargo, including non-revenue load
Zero fuel mass (ZFM)DOM + traffic load
Take-off mass (TOM)ZFM + take-off fuel, at the start of the take-off run
Ramp (taxi) massTOM + taxi fuel
Landing mass (LM)TOM - trip fuel
  • Each mass has a maximum structural limit: MRM (ramp), MTOM, MLM, MZFM. MZFM exists because fuel in the wings relieves wing bending, while load in the fuselage does not.
  • Take-off and landing masses may also be limited by performance at that aerodrome (runway length, climb, obstacles). The regulated mass is the lowest of the structural and performance limits.
  • The final figures are on the load sheet, signed by the PIC before departure. Floor loading limits (kg per square metre) must also be met for cargo.

Max TOM allowed = lowest of: MTOM (or performance TOM), MLM + trip fuel, MZFM + take-off fuel

Max traffic load = max TOM allowed - DOM - take-off fuel

Worked example: Most traffic load

  1. DOM 40,000 kg. Take-off fuel 12,000 kg, trip fuel 8,000 kg. MTOM 70,000, MLM 60,000, MZFM 55,000.
  2. Limit 1: MTOM = 70,000. Limit 2: MLM + trip = 60,000 + 8,000 = 68,000. Limit 3: MZFM + take-off fuel = 55,000 + 12,000 = 67,000.
  3. Lowest is 67,000 kg (zero fuel mass limited).
  4. Max traffic load = 67,000 - 40,000 - 12,000 = 15,000 kg.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Forward CG: more stable, heavier controls, higher stall speed, more drag, less range.
  • Aft CG: less stable, lighter controls, lower stall speed, better range, stall recovery may be poor.
  • Aft of datum +, forward -. Moment = mass x arm. CG = total moment / total mass.
  • %MAC = (CG - LEMAC) / MAC x 100.
  • Shift = mass moved x distance / total mass.
  • BEM: unusable fuel and full oil. DOM = BEM + crew and items. ZFM = DOM + traffic load. TOM = ZFM + take-off fuel. LM = TOM - trip fuel.
  • MZFM protects the wing roots.
  • Max TOM = lowest of MTOM, MLM + trip, MZFM + take-off fuel. Traffic load = that - DOM - take-off fuel.

Quick revision

The 5 revision slides for this module, as a list.

Forward and aft CG

  • Forward: stable, heavy, higher stall
  • Aft: less stable, light, better range
  • Limits set by maker
  • Outside: may be uncontrollable

Moments

  • Aft +, forward -
  • Moment = mass x arm
  • CG = moment / mass
  • %MAC from LEMAC

Moving load

  • Shift = m x d / M
  • Add mass: new moment / new mass
  • Mass to move = M x shift / d
  • Check limits after

Mass terms

  • BEM, then DOM (+ crew)
  • ZFM = DOM + traffic
  • TOM = ZFM + fuel
  • LM = TOM - trip

Max traffic load

  • Lowest of three limits
  • MTOM
  • MLM + trip fuel
  • MZFM + take-off fuel

Sample questions

5 of the 24 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The centre of gravity is the point through which:

    1. Lift acts
    2. The total weight of the aircraft acts
    3. Thrust acts
    4. Drag acts
    Show answer

    Answer: B. The aircraft balances there.

  2. Q2. The danger of a CG beyond the forward limit is:

    1. Not being able to raise the nose for rotation or flare
    2. Unrecoverable spin
    3. Too light controls
    4. Too much range
    Show answer

    Answer: A. The elevator may run out of authority.

  3. Q3. A 5,500 lb aircraft has its CG at 22 in. A 150 lb passenger moves 70 in aft. The new CG is about:

    1. 22.0 in
    2. 20.1 in
    3. 25.5 in
    4. 23.9 in
    Show answer

    Answer: D. Shift = 150 x 70 / 5,500 = 1.9 in. 22 + 1.9 = 23.9.

  4. Q4. Zero fuel mass is:

    1. DOM + traffic load
    2. BEM + fuel
    3. TOM - taxi fuel
    4. DOM + fuel
    Show answer

    Answer: A. Everything except usable fuel.

  5. Q5. DOM 40,000, take-off fuel 12,000, trip fuel 8,000, MTOM 70,000, MLM 60,000, MZFM 55,000 kg. The maximum traffic load is:

    1. 18,000 kg
    2. 16,000 kg
    3. 15,000 kg
    4. 30,000 kg
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Lowest limit is MZFM + fuel = 67,000. 67,000 - 40,000 - 12,000 = 15,000.
