The pitot-static system and the instruments it feeds: the airspeed indicator, altimeter, vertical speed indicator and Machmeter, their errors and what blockages do to them. Then temperature probes, density altitude and the air data computer.

The pitot-static system

  • The pitot tube faces into the airflow and senses total (pitot) pressure = static + dynamic.
  • Static vents on the side of the fuselage sense static pressure, the pressure of the still air around the aircraft.
  • Dynamic pressure = pitot - static = 1/2 x density x V squared. This is what the ASI measures.
  • The ASI and Machmeter use pitot and static. The altimeter and VSI use static only.
  • Pitot heads are heated against icing. Static vents are often placed on both sides to cancel errors in sideslip. A drain hole lets water out of the pitot.
  • An alternate static source inside the cabin can be selected if the vents block.

Airspeed indicator

  • Inside is a capsule fed with pitot pressure in a case fed with static. The capsule expands with dynamic pressure and moves the needle.
  • The ASI is calibrated to ISA sea level density, so it reads true airspeed only at sea level in ISA.

The altimeter

  • A pressure altimeter: a stack of sealed, evacuated capsules in a case fed with static pressure. As static pressure falls with height, the capsules expand and drive the needles.
  • A sensitive altimeter has more than one capsule and a subscale to set the pressure datum (QNH, QFE or 1013).
  • It is calibrated to the ISA: 1013.25 hPa and +15 degrees C at sea level, temperature falling 1.98 degrees C per 1,000 ft. Near the ground, 1 hPa is about 30 ft.
  • A bimetallic strip in the linkage corrects for the temperature of the instrument itself, not the outside air.
  • Turning the subscale up (higher pressure) makes the altimeter read higher. About 30 ft per hPa.

Worked example: Density altitude

  1. Aerodrome pressure altitude 2,000 ft, temperature +31 degrees C.
  2. ISA at 2,000 ft = 15 - 4 = +11 degrees C. ISA deviation = +20.
  3. Density altitude = 2,000 + 20 x 120 = 4,400 ft. The aircraft performs as if it were at 4,400 ft.

Vertical speed indicator

  • A capsule fed directly with static pressure sits in a case also fed with static, but through a metered leak (a tiny hole). In a climb or descent, the case pressure lags behind the capsule pressure, and the difference shows as rate of climb or descent.
  • The VSI has a lag of a few seconds. An instantaneous VSI (IVSI) adds small dashpots (accelerometer pistons) that react at once to a change of vertical speed.
  • The metered leak is made to work over a range of temperature and height, so the reading stays correct.

The Machmeter

  • Mach number = TAS / local speed of sound (LSS).
  • LSS depends only on temperature: LSS (kt) = 38.95 x square root of absolute temperature (K). At ISA sea level (288 K) it is about 661 kt.
  • The Machmeter has an airspeed capsule (pitot vs static) and an altitude capsule (sealed, in static). It measures the ratio (pitot - static) / static, which gives Mach number.
  • It has no temperature error, because the formula cancels it out. It does have instrument, position and blockage errors like the ASI.
Above the tropopause the temperature is constant, so at constant Mach the TAS stays constant too.

Memory trick

A blocked static traps the old pressure. The altimeter stops, the VSI shows zero, and the ASI goes wrong the opposite way to the height change in a descent.

Temperature and the air data computer

  • Static air temperature (SAT, or OAT) is the real temperature of the air around the aircraft.
  • A probe in fast airflow reads higher, because the air is slowed and heated (ram rise). The full rise gives total air temperature (TAT).
  • TAT = SAT + ram rise. The ram rise grows with the square of TAS, and is small at low speed.
  • The air data computer (ADC) takes pitot, static and TAT and works out altitude, vertical speed, CAS, TAS, Mach, SAT and TAT. It feeds the flight displays, the autopilot, the transponder (Mode C) and the flight management system.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • ASI and Machmeter: pitot and static. Altimeter and VSI: static only.
  • Dynamic pressure = pitot - static = 1/2 rho V squared.
  • ASI errors: instrument and position (CAS), compressibility (EAS, over-reads), density (TAS).
  • White arc Vso to Vfe. Green Vs1 to Vno. Yellow Vno to Vne. Red Vne.
  • ISA: 1013.25 hPa, +15 C, 1.98 C per 1,000 ft. 1 hPa about 30 ft.
  • High to low, or hot to cold: altimeter over-reads, look out below.
  • Density altitude = PA + 120 ft per degree above ISA.
  • VSI: metered leak. IVSI: dashpots.
  • Mach = TAS / LSS. LSS = 38.95 x square root of K, depends on temperature only.
  • Climb at constant CAS: TAS and Mach rise. At constant Mach (in the troposphere): TAS falls.
  • Static blocked: altimeter frozen, VSI zero, ASI over-reads in descent.
  • Pitot and drain blocked: ASI over-reads in climb. Cabin alternate static: all over-read.
  • TAT = SAT + ram rise.

    1. Altimeter and VSI
    2. ASI and Machmeter
    3. VSI only
    4. All four

    Answer: B. The altimeter and VSI use static pressure only.

  2. Q2. The upper end of the green arc on an ASI is:

    1. Vne
    2. Vno
    3. Vfe
    4. Va
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Max structural cruising speed.

  3. Q3. Pressure altitude 2,000 ft, temperature +31 C. The density altitude is about:

    1. 2,000 ft
    2. 3,200 ft
    3. 4,400 ft
    4. 6,000 ft
    Show answer

    Answer: C. ISA at 2,000 ft is +11. Deviation +20. 2,000 + 20 x 120 = 4,400 ft.

  4. Q4. An aircraft flies at Mach 0.8 where the LSS is 600 kt. Its TAS is:

    1. 480 kt
    2. 750 kt
    3. 600 kt
    4. 520 kt
    Show answer

    Answer: A. TAS = Mach x LSS = 0.8 x 600.

  5. Q5. The pitot tube blocks but the drain hole stays open. The ASI will:

    1. Fall to zero
    2. Over-read
    3. Freeze
    4. Read correctly
    Show answer

    Answer: A. The pitot pressure drains to static.
