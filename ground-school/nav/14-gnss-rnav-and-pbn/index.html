Satellite navigation: how GPS finds position, its three segments, errors and the integrity check RAIM, the other GNSS systems, augmentation including India's GAGAN. Then area navigation, waypoints and performance based navigation with RNAV and RNP specifications.

How GNSS works

  • GNSS (global navigation satellite system) is the general name. GPS (USA) is the best known. Others: GLONASS (Russia), Galileo (Europe), BeiDou (China). India has its regional NavIC (IRNSS).
  • Each satellite sends its position and a very exact time. The receiver measures how long each signal took, which gives a range (called a pseudo-range because the receiver clock is not perfect).
  • With 3 satellites you can fix position in 2D if height is known. 4 satellites give 3D position and the time: the fourth one solves the receiver's clock error.
  • For an integrity check (RAIM), 5 satellites are needed to detect a bad one, and 6 to exclude it and keep going.
  • GPS works in the WGS 84 datum. Its civil signal is on L1, 1,575.42 MHz.
GPS segmentWhat it is
SpaceAbout 24 or more satellites in 6 orbital planes, about 20,200 km up, inclined 55 degrees, each going round in about 12 hours
ControlGround stations that track the satellites and upload corrected orbits and clock data
UserThe receivers in aircraft, ships, phones

GNSS errors and integrity

ErrorNote
Ionospheric delayThe biggest natural error. The signal slows passing through the ionosphere. Two frequencies, or augmentation, can correct it
Tropospheric delaySmaller, from the lower air
Satellite clock and orbit (ephemeris)Small, corrected by the control segment
MultipathSignals bouncing off the airframe or buildings
Receiver noiseSmall
Geometry (DOP)Satellites bunched together give a poor fix. Spread out gives a good one. Measured by dilution of precision
  • RAIM (receiver autonomous integrity monitoring): the receiver checks its satellites against each other and warns if one is giving bad data. A RAIM prediction is done before flight for GNSS approaches.
  • GNSS signals are weak and can be jammed or spoofed. Crews must watch for position jumps and keep other aids ready.

Augmentation

TypeHowExample
ABAS (aircraft based)Checks inside the aircraft: RAIM, or mixing with IRSRAIM
SBAS (satellite based)Ground stations measure errors and send corrections and integrity data through geostationary satellites. Allows LPV approaches with vertical guidanceGAGAN (India), WAAS (USA), EGNOS (Europe)
GBAS (ground based)A ground station at the airport sends corrections by VHF. Allows precision approaches (GLS)GLS

GAGAN (GPS Aided GEO Augmented Navigation) was built by the AAI and ISRO. It is certified for en-route use and for APV approaches (LPV) over India.

RNAV and PBN

  • Area navigation (RNAV) lets an aircraft fly any path between waypoints, instead of only from beacon to beacon. Position comes from GNSS, DME/DME, VOR/DME or IRS.
  • Waypoints can be fly-by (the aircraft turns before reaching it, to cut the corner) or fly-over (it must pass overhead before turning).
  • Performance based navigation (PBN) describes routes and procedures by the accuracy, integrity and functions needed, not by the equipment.
  • An RNAV specification states accuracy only. An RNP specification adds on-board performance monitoring and alerting: the aircraft warns the crew if it cannot meet the accuracy.
  • The number is the accuracy in nm that must be met 95% of the time: RNAV 1 means within 1 nm.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • GNSS: GPS, GLONASS, Galileo, BeiDou. India: NavIC.
  • 4 satellites for 3D + time. RAIM: 5 to detect, 6 to exclude.
  • GPS: 24+ satellites, 6 planes, 20,200 km, 55 degrees, 12 h orbit. WGS 84. L1 1,575.42 MHz.
  • Biggest error: ionosphere. Poor geometry: high DOP.
  • ABAS: RAIM. SBAS: GAGAN, WAAS, EGNOS, gives LPV. GBAS: GLS.
  • GAGAN: AAI and ISRO.
  • RNAV: any path between waypoints. Fly-by cuts the corner, fly-over goes overhead.
  • RNP = RNAV + on-board monitoring and alerting. Number = nm for 95%.
  • LNAV to MDA. LNAV/VNAV and LPV to DA.

Sample questions

5 of the 24 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. India's own regional satellite navigation system is:

    1. GAGAN
    2. NavIC
    3. GLONASS
    4. BeiDou
    Show answer

    Answer: B. NavIC (IRNSS). GAGAN is an augmentation system for GPS.

  2. Q2. A GNSS range is called a pseudo-range because:

    1. It is measured in feet
    2. The receiver clock is not exact
    3. It is a guess
    4. It comes from radar
    Show answer

    Answer: B. The clock error is solved with an extra satellite.

  3. Q3. Satellites bunched close together in the sky give:

    1. A high DOP and a poorer fix
    2. A better fix
    3. No effect
    4. Faster acquisition
    Show answer

    Answer: A. Spread out satellites give good geometry.

  4. Q4. An LNAV approach is flown to:

    1. A DA
    2. A DH of 100 ft
    3. An MDA
    4. No minimum
    Show answer

    Answer: C. It has lateral guidance only.

  5. Q5. RNAV 1 means the aircraft must stay within:

    1. 1 nm of the path 95% of the time
    2. 1 km always
    3. 0.1 nm
    4. 1 degree
    Show answer

    Answer: A. The number is nm for 95% of the time.
