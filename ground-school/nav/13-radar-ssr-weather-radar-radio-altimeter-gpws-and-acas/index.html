How radar measures range and bearing, primary and secondary radar on the ground, transponder modes and codes, airborne weather radar and its colours, the radio altimeter, ground proximity warnings and the collision avoidance system.

Radar principles

  • Primary radar sends a pulse and listens for the echo reflected off the target. It needs nothing in the aircraft.
  • Range comes from the time the echo takes. Bearing comes from the direction the antenna is pointing.
  • Radar time: 12.36 microseconds for each nm of range (there and back).
  • Pulse repetition frequency (PRF): pulses per second. A lower PRF gives a longer maximum range (more time to listen). Max range (nm) is about 81,000 / PRF.
  • Pulse length sets the minimum range and how close two targets can be and still show as two (range resolution).
  • Beam width sets how close two targets can be side by side (bearing resolution). A narrow beam needs a large antenna or a short wavelength.
  • A continuous wave radar uses the Doppler shift instead of pulses, to find speed.

Range (nm) = time (microseconds) / 12.36. Max range (nm) = 81,000 / PRF (about)

  • Secondary surveillance radar (SSR) asks the aircraft's transponder a question on 1,030 MHz. The transponder answers on 1,090 MHz.
  • SSR needs less power than primary radar, has no ground clutter or weather echoes, and gives identity and level.
  • Mode A: a 4 digit code (digits 0 to 7, so 4,096 codes). Mode C: pressure altitude in 100 ft steps, from the encoding altimeter (always referred to 1013 hPa). Mode S: a unique 24-bit address for each aircraft, selective calls and data link. It also feeds ACAS.
  • IDENT makes your return stand out on the controller's screen for a short time. Press it only when asked.
  • Problems: garbling (replies from two close aircraft overlap) and FRUIT (replies meant for other radars). Mode S cuts both.

Airborne weather radar

  • Works on about 9 GHz (X band, wavelength about 3 cm), which reflects well off large water drops.
  • It sees wet weather: rain and wet hail. Dry hail, snow and clear air turbulence show weakly or not at all.
  • Colours show return strength: black (none), green (light), yellow (amber) (moderate), red (heavy), magenta (very heavy or turbulence on some sets).
  • The steeper the colour change from green to red (a tight gradient), the worse the turbulence. Avoid red and magenta cells by at least 20 nm at height.
  • Modes: WX (weather), WX + T (turbulence on some sets, using Doppler), MAP (ground mapping with a wider beam).
  • Tilt points the beam up or down. Too much down tilt shows ground returns. A storm behind a heavy cell may be hidden by attenuation (the radar shadow).
  • Safety: do not run the radar on the ground near people or fuelling, as the beam can harm them.

Radio altimeter

  • Measures height above the ground directly below, by timing a signal bounced off the surface.
  • Works on about 4.2 to 4.4 GHz with frequency modulated continuous wave (FMCW): the difference between the sent and received frequencies gives the height.
  • Range up to about 2,500 ft. Accurate to within a few feet, which makes it essential for CAT II and III approaches and autoland.
  • It feeds the GPWS, the decision height alert, and autoland flare.

  • Basic GPWS uses the radio altimeter, so it cannot see a cliff ahead. Enhanced GPWS (EGPWS) or TAWS adds a terrain database and GPS position to look ahead, and shows terrain on the ND.
  • On a PULL UP warning: at once, full power, pitch up to the stick shaker or the best climb, wings level, and keep climbing until the warning stops. Do not wait to check.

ACAS (TCAS)

  • ACAS interrogates other aircraft's transponders, works out their range, bearing and level, and predicts a collision. It needs no ground station.
  • It sees only aircraft with a working transponder. With Mode C or S, it can give advice in the vertical. With Mode A only, it gives traffic advisories without level.
  • A traffic advisory (TA) about 40 seconds before the closest point: TRAFFIC TRAFFIC. Look, do not manoeuvre.
  • A resolution advisory (RA) about 25 seconds before: CLIMB or DESCEND or MONITOR VERTICAL SPEED. Follow it at once, even against ATC, then tell ATC.
  • Two aircraft with ACAS II and Mode S coordinate their RAs so they do not both turn the same way.
  • ACAS II gives vertical RAs only.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Radar: 12.36 microseconds per nm. Lower PRF, longer range. Pulse length: min range. Beam width: bearing resolution.
  • SSR: ground 1,030 MHz, aircraft 1,090 MHz. Mode A code (4,096), Mode C altitude (100 ft, 1013), Mode S address and data.
  • Weather radar 9 GHz, sees wet drops. Green, yellow, red, magenta. Avoid red by 20 nm. Tight gradient: turbulence.
  • Radio altimeter 4.2 to 4.4 GHz, FMCW, up to 2,500 ft.
  • GPWS modes: 1 sink rate, 2 closure, 3 sink after take-off, 4 too low gear/flap, 5 glideslope, 7 windshear.
  • EGPWS/TAWS: terrain database, looks ahead.
  • TCAS: TA about 40 s, RA about 25 s. Follow the RA. Amber circle TA, red square RA.
