True, magnetic and compass north, variation and deviation and how to convert between them, the Earth's magnetic field and dip, aircraft magnetism and the compass swing, and the direct reading compass with its acceleration and turning errors.

Three norths

NorthPoints toDifference
True northThe geographic North Pole, along the meridian
Magnetic northWhere a freely hung magnet points: towards the north magnetic poleVariation: the angle from true to magnetic north
Compass northWhere the aircraft's compass points, pulled aside by the aircraft's own magnetismDeviation: the angle from magnetic to compass north
  • Variation and deviation are east (+) if the north they lead to lies east of the one before, and west (-) if it lies west.
  • Directions are measured clockwise from north, from 000 to 360 degrees. (T) is true, (M) magnetic, (C) compass.
  • Variation depends on where you are and changes slowly with time (the annual change), because the magnetic poles move.
  • Deviation depends on the aircraft and on its heading. It is listed on a deviation card in the cockpit.

Converting between true, magnetic and compass

True = Magnetic + Variation East (or - West)

Magnetic = Compass + Deviation East (or - West)

  • Going from true towards compass, you subtract east and add west.
  • Going from compass towards true, you add east and subtract west.

Memory trick

Variation east, magnetic least. Variation west, magnetic best. The same works for deviation: deviation east, compass least. Deviation west, compass best.

Worked example: T, V, M, D, C

  1. True heading 100, variation 5W: magnetic = 100 + 5 = 105(M) (west is best).
  2. Magnetic heading 105, deviation 2E: compass = 105 - 2 = 103(C) (east is least).
  3. Compass heading 270, deviation 3W, variation 1E: magnetic = 270 - 3 = 267, true = 267 + 1 = 268(T).
  4. Delhi has a small east variation. A runway on a true heading of 270 there points about 269(M).

The Earth's magnetic field

  • The Earth acts like a giant bar magnet. Its magnetic poles are not at the geographic poles and they move a little each year.
  • The field at any place has a horizontal part (H) and a vertical part (Z). Only H gives the compass its direction.
  • Dip (inclination) is the angle the field makes with the horizontal. Zero at the magnetic equator, 90 degrees at the magnetic poles.
  • H is strongest at the magnetic equator and zero at the magnetic poles. So a compass becomes sluggish and useless close to the magnetic poles (roughly within 1,000 nm).
  • Z is zero at the magnetic equator and strongest at the poles. Z is what causes the compass errors below.

Aircraft magnetism and the compass swing

  • Steel parts and electrical currents make the aircraft a magnet of its own. This causes deviation.
  • Hard iron magnetism is permanent, from the aircraft's build. Soft iron magnetism is induced by the Earth's field and changes with heading and latitude.
  • Deviation changes with heading, so it is measured on several headings in a compass swing, and the remaining errors are written on the deviation card.
  • Compass swing results are split into coefficients: A (the same on all headings, often a misaligned lubber line), B (biggest on east and west) and C (biggest on north and south).
A compass swing is needed
When the compass is new or replacedAfter a lightning strike or heavy landing
After major repairs or new electrical or steel parts near the compassWhen the aircraft moves to a very different latitude to be based there
After the aircraft has stood on one heading for a long timeAt the intervals set in the maintenance programme

A swing is done at a compass base, a quiet area of the aerodrome away from steel, power lines and radio, where magnetic directions have been surveyed.

The direct reading compass

  • The standby compass in almost every aircraft. Its magnets sit in a bowl of liquid with a card or scale read against a lubber line.
  • Horizontality: the magnet should stay level. The centre of gravity is hung below the pivot (pendulous), so its weight fights the pull of dip. This works well except near the magnetic poles.
  • Sensitivity: it must find magnetic north quickly. Several small magnets give strength without weight. The liquid also takes weight off the pivot, cutting friction.
  • Aperiodicity: after a disturbance it must settle without swinging back and forth. Damping by the liquid and by damping wires does this.
  • The liquid (such as a silicone fluid or alcohol) must be clear, have a low freezing point, low viscosity, low expansion and must not corrode the parts.

Acceleration and turning errors

Because the magnet's weight hangs below the pivot while dip pulls the north end down (in the northern hemisphere), any acceleration or turn tilts the magnet, and the vertical part of the field (Z) then swings it off north. These errors are bigger at higher latitudes, where dip is greater, and are zero at the magnetic equator.

Acceleration errorTurning error
Biggest onEast and west headingsNorth and south headings
Zero onNorth and south headingsEast and west headings
Northern hemisphere ruleANDS: Accelerate, shows a turn North. Decelerate, shows a turn SouthUNOS: Undershoot North, Overshoot South. When turning onto north, roll out before the compass reads north. Turning onto south, roll out after
Southern hemisphereThe opposite: accelerate shows a turn southThe opposite: undershoot south, overshoot north
  • Through north in the northern hemisphere, the compass card is sluggish (it lags behind the turn). Through south, it is lively (it runs ahead of the turn).
  • Liquid swirl in a turn drags the magnet along with the turn. It adds to the error when the compass is sluggish and reduces it when the compass is lively.
  • The answer is to read the compass only in straight, level and steady flight, and to use the heading indicator for turns.

Memory trick

ANDS: Accelerate North, Decelerate South. UNOS: Undershoot North, Overshoot South. Both for the northern hemisphere, and reversed in the south.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Variation: true to magnetic. Deviation: magnetic to compass. East is +, west is - (going towards true).
  • Variation east, magnetic least. West, magnetic best. Same for deviation and compass.
  • Isogonal: equal variation. Agonic: zero variation. Isoclinal: equal dip. Aclinic: zero dip (magnetic equator).
  • Dip 0 at the magnetic equator, 90 at the magnetic poles. H strongest at the magnetic equator, zero at the poles.
  • Deviation changes with heading. Hard iron is permanent, soft iron is induced.
  • Coefficient A: same on all headings (lubber line). B: east and west. C: north and south.
  • Ideal compass: horizontality, sensitivity, aperiodicity.
  • Acceleration error: biggest on E and W, zero on N and S. ANDS in the north.
  • Turning error: biggest on N and S, zero on E and W. UNOS in the north.
  • All dip errors are zero at the magnetic equator and reverse in the southern hemisphere.

Sample questions

5 of the 27 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. Variation is the angle between:

    1. True north and compass north
    2. True north and magnetic north
    3. Magnetic north and compass north
    4. Grid north and true north
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Variation is true to magnetic. Deviation is magnetic to compass.

  2. Q2. True track 045, variation 10E. The magnetic track is:

    1. 055
    2. 035
    3. 045
    4. 025
    Show answer

    Answer: B. East is least: 045 - 10 = 035.

  3. Q3. A direct reading compass becomes unreliable near the magnetic poles because:

    1. Variation is zero there
    2. The liquid freezes
    3. Deviation is greatest there
    4. The horizontal component H is too weak
    Show answer

    Answer: D. Only H gives direction, and it fades towards the poles.

  4. Q4. Compass acceleration error is greatest on headings of:

    1. North and south
    2. Northeast and southwest
    3. East and west
    4. It is the same on all headings
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Zero on north and south, biggest on east and west.

  5. Q5. In the northern hemisphere, turning onto south using the compass, you should roll out:

    1. Before it reads south
    2. It does not matter
    3. Exactly at south
    4. After it reads south
    Show answer

    Answer: D. UNOS: overshoot south. The compass runs ahead through south.
