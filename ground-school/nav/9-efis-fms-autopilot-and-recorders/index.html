The glass cockpit: the primary flight display and navigation display with their modes and colour code, engine and warning displays, the flight management system and its database, the autopilot, flight director and autothrottle with autoland, and the flight data and cockpit voice recorders.

EFIS: PFD and ND

  • An Electronic Flight Instrument System (EFIS) shows flight information on screens. Each pilot has a PFD and an ND, driven by symbol generators (display computers). A third symbol generator can take over if one fails.
  • The primary flight display (PFD) replaces the attitude indicator, ASI, altimeter and VSI, and shows flight director bars and autopilot modes (the flight mode annunciator).
  • The navigation display (ND) replaces the HSI and adds the route, weather radar, terrain and traffic.
  • The screens dim and brighten automatically with a light sensor.

  • EICAS (Boeing style) or ECAM (Airbus style) shows engine readings and system pages, and lists warning and caution messages, with the most urgent at the top.
  • Warnings (red) come with a master warning light and aural alert. Cautions (amber) with a master caution.

Flight management system

  • The FMS combines navigation and performance management. The crew talk to it through the CDU (or MCDU): a screen with a keyboard and line select keys.
  • Navigation: it works out the best position from IRS, GNSS, DME and VOR, and flies the route in LNAV and VNAV through the autopilot.
  • Performance: it works out speeds, climb, cruise and descent profiles, fuel and the top of descent, using the cost index chosen by the airline.
  • The navigation database holds airports, navaids, waypoints, airways, SIDs, STARs and approaches. It is updated every 28 days (the AIRAC cycle). Two databases are loaded, the current one and the next.
  • The crew must check the database is current and the route against the flight plan before flight.

Autopilot, flight director and autothrottle

  • An autopilot flies the aircraft through servos on the controls. The inner loop keeps the aircraft stable (attitude). The outer loop follows the chosen mode (heading, altitude, route).
  • A single axis autopilot works in roll only. A two axis autopilot works in roll and pitch. A yaw damper works on the rudder to stop Dutch roll.
  • A flight director works out the same commands as the autopilot but shows them as bars on the PFD for the pilot to follow by hand.
  • The autothrottle (autothrust) sets thrust to hold a speed or a thrust setting.
  • Lateral modes include HDG, LNAV (NAV), VOR LOC. Vertical modes include ALT HOLD, V/S, FLCH (level change), VNAV, GS (glide slope).
  • The mode control panel (MCP) or flight control unit (FCU) is where the crew select modes and targets. Engaged modes show on the flight mode annunciator.
  • Disconnecting the autopilot gives an aural warning. Pilots must always be able to override or disconnect it quickly.
Autoland termMeaning
Fail passiveAfter a failure, the autopilot disconnects without a big upset. The pilot lands by hand. Used for CAT II and some CAT IIIA
Fail operationalAfter one failure, the system keeps landing automatically (needs two or three channels). Used for CAT IIIB

Flight recorders

Flight data recorder (FDR)Cockpit voice recorder (CVR)
RecordsFlight parameters: time, altitude, airspeed, heading, acceleration, attitude, control positions, engine power and moreCrew voices, radio calls, cockpit sounds and alerts
Keeps at leastThe last 25 hoursThe last 2 hours (25 hours on aircraft first certified from 2022)
StartsAutomatically before take-off rollFrom the start of the checklist before engine start
  • Both are painted bright orange (often called black boxes), fitted in the tail where they are most likely to survive, and are built to resist impact, fire and water.
  • Each has an underwater locator beacon that sends pulses at 37.5 kHz, for at least 30 days (90 days on newer units), once in water.
  • The CVR has an erase function that works only on the ground with the parking brake set.
  • Aeroplanes over 5,700 kg carry two recorders, or two combined units.
  • After an accident or serious incident, the recorders are switched off to keep the data.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • PFD: attitude, speed, altitude, VS, FD, mode annunciator. ND: heading, route, radar, terrain, traffic.
  • ND modes: ROSE, ARC, MAP (track up), PLAN (north up, no radar).
  • Colours: magenta fly to, green engaged, cyan armed, amber caution, red warning, white present data.
  • EICAS or ECAM: engines and warnings.
  • FMS database updated every 28 days (AIRAC). Crew check it is current.
  • Inner loop: stability. Outer loop: modes. Yaw damper stops Dutch roll.
  • Flight director: commands as bars for hand flying.
  • Fail passive: disconnects, pilot lands. Fail operational: keeps landing.
  • FDR 25 h, CVR 2 h (25 h on new aircraft). Beacon 37.5 kHz, 30 days (90 on newer).

Sample questions

5 of the 24 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. On an EFIS, the display that replaces the attitude indicator, ASI and altimeter is the:

    1. ND
    2. PFD
    3. EICAS
    4. CDU
    Show answer

    Answer: B. The primary flight display.

  2. Q2. On the flight mode annunciator, an engaged mode is usually shown in:

    1. Cyan
    2. Green
    3. Amber
    4. Magenta
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Armed modes are cyan, engaged modes green.

  3. Q3. The crew enter data into the FMS through the:

    1. CDU or MCDU
    2. MCP
    3. ND
    4. EICAS
    Show answer

    Answer: A. The control display unit.

  4. Q4. A single axis autopilot controls the aircraft in:

    1. Pitch
    2. All three
    3. Yaw
    4. Roll
    Show answer

    Answer: D. Roll only. A two axis adds pitch.

  5. Q5. A flight data recorder must keep at least the last:

    1. 30 minutes
    2. 2 hours
    3. 10 hours
    4. 25 hours
    Show answer

    Answer: D. 25 hours.
