How the Earth's orbit and tilt make the seasons, the kinds of day and year, local mean time, UTC and standard times like IST, converting between arc and time, the International Date Line, and sunrise, sunset and twilight.

The Earth and the sun

  • The Earth goes round the sun on an ellipse with the sun at one focus (Kepler's first law). Seen from above the north pole, it goes anticlockwise.
  • It moves fastest when nearest the sun (perihelion, about 4 January) and slowest when farthest (aphelion, about 3 July). A line from the sun to the Earth sweeps out equal areas in equal times (Kepler's second law).
  • The Earth's axis is tilted 23.5 degrees from the line square to its orbit. This tilt is the obliquity of the ecliptic, and it causes the seasons.
  • The sun's declination is its angle north or south of the equator. It moves between 23.5N and 23.5S over the year. It is like a latitude for the sun.

  • Day length changes fastest around the equinoxes, when declination changes fastest, and slowest around the solstices.

The difference between apparent (sundial) time and mean (clock) time is the equation of time. It is up to about 16 minutes in early November and about 14 minutes the other way in mid February.

  • Local mean time (LMT) is the time by the mean sun on the local meridian. It is 1200 LMT when the mean sun crosses that meridian. Every place on the same meridian has the same LMT.
  • UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is the LMT at Greenwich. All aviation uses UTC, written with a Z (1430Z).
  • Places east of Greenwich see the sun first, so their LMT is ahead of UTC. Places west are behind.

LMT = UTC + longitude East in time (or - longitude West)

Memory trick

Longitude east, Greenwich time least. Longitude west, Greenwich time best. The same shape as variation.

Worked example: Converting

  1. Longitude 075E in time: 75 / 15 = 5 h. So LMT is UTC + 5 h. 1200 LMT there = 0700 UTC.
  2. Longitude 07730E in time: 77 x 4 = 308 min, plus 30' x 4 s = 2 min. Total 5 h 10 min.
  3. Longitude 045W: 3 h. 0900 UTC = 0600 LMT.
  4. Sunrise at a place at 080E is at 0600 LMT. 80 x 4 = 320 min = 5 h 20 min. So sunrise is 0040 UTC.

Standard time and the date line

  • Using LMT would mean every town has its own clock. Countries use a standard time (zone time) for the whole country or region.
  • Indian Standard Time (IST) is UTC + 5 h 30 min, based on the meridian of 82.5E (near Mirzapur).
  • Standard times are listed in the Air Almanac and in the AIP (GEN 2.1).
  • The International Date Line roughly follows the 180 degree meridian, bending round some islands. The date is one day ahead on its west side.
  • Crossing it westbound (from America towards Asia): add a day. Eastbound: take away a day.

Worked example: Standard time

  1. Departure from Delhi at 0930 IST is 0400 UTC (take away 5:30).
  2. A flight leaves Mumbai at 2200 IST (1630 UTC) and lands in Dubai after 3 h 15 min: 1945 UTC, which is 2345 local in Dubai (UTC + 4).

Sunrise, sunset and twilight

  • Sunrise and sunset are when the upper edge of the sun is on the horizon, seen at sea level. Because of refraction, the sun is then really about 50 minutes of arc below the horizon.
  • Sunrise and sunset tables in the Air Almanac are in LMT, by latitude and date. Convert to UTC with the longitude.
  • Twilight is the time the sky is still lit when the sun is below the horizon.

  • Twilight is shortest at the equator (civil twilight about 21 minutes), where the sun goes down steeply, and longer at higher latitudes.
  • At height, sunrise is earlier and sunset later than on the ground, and civil twilight is shorter.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Perihelion about 4 January (fastest), aphelion about 3 July (slowest).
  • Obliquity 23.5 degrees. Equinoxes 21 March, 23 September. Solstices 21 June (23.5N), 21 December (23.5S).
  • Sidereal day 23 h 56 min. Mean solar day 24 h. Equation of time up to 16 min (November).
  • 15 degrees = 1 h, 1 degree = 4 min, 15' = 1 min, 1' = 4 s.
  • LMT = UTC + long E (- long W). East is least, west is best.
  • IST = UTC + 5:30, meridian 82.5E.
  • Date line westbound: add a day. Eastbound: take away a day.
  • Twilight: civil 6, nautical 12, astronomical 18 degrees. Shortest at the equator.
  • Sunrise tables are in LMT.

    2. Early July

    Answer: A. About 4 January. Aphelion is about 3 July.

  2. Q2. The sun is overhead at the equator at:

    1. The solstices
    2. The equinoxes
    3. Perihelion
    4. Aphelion

    Answer: B. About 21 March and 23 September.

  3. Q3. 15 degrees of longitude is equal to:

    1. 15 minutes of time
    2. 1 hour
    3. 4 minutes
    4. 1 minute
    Show answer

    Answer: B. 360 degrees in 24 hours.

    1. The real sun is overhead at Greenwich
    2. The sun is at its highest declination
    3. It is noon in IST
    4. The mean sun crosses that place's meridian

    Answer: D. Local noon by the mean sun.

    1. UTC
    2. Sidereal time
    3. Standard time
