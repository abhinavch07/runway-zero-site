Words and speeds

TermMeaning
Heading (HDG)The direction the aircraft's nose points
Track (TR)The path over the ground. Required track is the one planned. Track made good (TMG) is the one actually flown
DriftThe angle between heading and track, caused by wind. Starboard (right) drift: track is right of heading. Port (left): left of heading
Track error (TE)The angle between the required track and the track made good
Wind velocity (W/V)The direction the wind blows from, and its speed: 270/30 is from the west at 30 kt. Forecasts and METARs give true direction. ATC and ATIS give magnetic
TASTrue airspeed: speed through the air
GSGround speed: speed over the ground
Dead reckoning (DR)Working out position from heading, TAS, wind and time, without a fix
AirspeedWhat it is
IASWhat the ASI shows
CASIAS corrected for instrument and position error
EASCAS corrected for compressibility (matters above about 300 kt)
TASEAS corrected for air density: grows with altitude and temperature
  • Rule of thumb: TAS is about 2% more than CAS for every 1,000 ft of altitude. At FL 100, TAS is about 20% more than CAS.
  • TAS is found exactly on the navigation computer (CRP or E6B) from CAS, pressure altitude and outside air temperature.

Memory trick

ICE-T: IAS, CAS, EAS, TAS, in that order of corrections.

The wind triangle

  • Three vectors make the triangle: heading/TAS (air vector, one arrow), W/V (three arrows), and track/GS (two arrows). Air vector plus wind vector equals ground vector.
  • If you know four of the six values, you can find the other two. The most common sum: from track, TAS and W/V, find heading and GS.
  • Wind from the left gives right (starboard) drift. To hold track, steer into the wind: heading is left of track.
  • Drift is greatest when the wind is at 90 degrees to the track, and zero with a direct head or tail wind.

Max drift (degrees) = wind speed x 60 / TAS

Drift = max drift x sin (wind angle). Headwind = wind speed x cos (wind angle)

Worked example: Mental wind triangle

  1. Track 090(T), TAS 120 kt, W/V 060/30. Wind angle = 30 degrees, from the left of the nose.
  2. Max drift = 30 x 60 / 120 = 15 degrees. Drift = 15 x 1/2 = 7.5 degrees right. So steer about 082 (into wind).
  3. Headwind = 30 x cos 30 = about 26 kt. GS = 120 - 26 = 94 kt.

Runway wind components

  • Runway numbers are magnetic. Use the ATIS or tower wind (magnetic) directly. A forecast or METAR wind is true, so convert it first.
  • Headwind = W x cos (angle between wind and runway). Crosswind = W x sin (same angle).
  • If the angle is more than 90 degrees, the cos is negative: you have a tailwind.

Worked example: Runway 27, wind 310/30 (magnetic)

  1. Angle = 310 - 270 = 40 degrees, from the right.
  2. Headwind = 30 x cos 40 (0.77) = 23 kt. Crosswind = 30 x sin 40 (0.64) = 19 kt from the right.

Time, speed, distance and fuel

Distance = Speed x Time. Time (min) = Distance x 60 / GS

Worked example: Sums

  1. GS 220 kt, 57 nm to go: 57 x 60 / 220 = 15.5 min.
  2. Fuel flow 2,300 kg/h, 1 h 37 min to go: 2,300 x 97 / 60 = 3,718 kg.
  3. 2,250 litres of Jet A-1 at SG 0.8 = 1,800 kg = about 3,970 lb.
  4. Endurance: 380 imperial gallons usable at 130 gallons per hour = 2 h 55 min.

The 1 in 60 rule

For small angles, 1 degree of track error puts you about 1 nm off track for every 60 nm flown. This makes quick heading corrections easy.

Track error = distance off x 60 / distance gone

Closing angle = distance off x 60 / distance to go

  • To fly straight to the destination, alter heading by track error + closing angle, towards the planned track.
  • To regain track at the halfway point of the remaining distance and then turn back, alter by twice the track error.

Worked example: Off track

  1. After 40 nm you are 4 nm left of track, with 80 nm to go.
  2. Track error = 4 x 60 / 40 = 6 degrees. Closing angle = 4 x 60 / 80 = 3 degrees.
  3. Turn 9 degrees right to fly direct to the destination.

Glide path height (ft) = slope (degrees) x 100 x range (nm)

Rate of descent (ft/min) = GS (kt) x slope (degrees) x 100 / 60

  • On a 3 degree slope: height is about 300 ft per nm, and the rate of descent is about 5 x GS. At 120 kt GS, about 600 ft/min.
  • If GS falls (more headwind), the rate of descent must fall too, to stay on the slope.
  • A gradient in percent: ROD (ft/min) = gradient (%) x GS (kt), roughly.

Relative speed

  • Two aircraft flying towards each other close at the sum of their ground speeds.
  • Two aircraft on the same track, one behind, close at the difference of their ground speeds.
  • Time to meet or catch up = gap / closing speed.

Worked example: Closing sums

  1. A at 300 kt and B at 200 kt fly towards each other, 250 nm apart. Closing speed 500 kt. They meet in 250 / 500 = 30 min.
  2. A at 480 kt is 40 nm behind B at 400 kt on the same track. Closing 80 kt. A catches B in 40 / 80 = 30 min.

Pressure pattern flying

  • Buys Ballot's law (northern hemisphere): with your back to the wind, low pressure is on your left. In the southern hemisphere it is on your right.
  • So in the north, if you fly with low pressure on your left, the wind is behind you: a tailwind.
  • Flying in the north from high towards low pressure, the wind comes from your left, so you drift right (starboard).
  • Flying from high to low pressure on the same altimeter setting, the altimeter over-reads: you are lower than you think. "High to low, look out below."
  • Pressure pattern flying uses the wind around highs and lows to save time on long flights: a single heading may be flown, with the drift worked out from the change in pressure height (Bellamy's method) rather than the wind itself.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Drift: angle between heading and track. Wind from the left, drift right. Steer into wind.
  • Forecast wind true. ATC and ATIS wind magnetic.
  • IAS, CAS, EAS, TAS. TAS about 2% per 1,000 ft more than CAS.
  • Max drift = W x 60 / TAS. Drift = max drift x sin angle. Headwind = W x cos angle.
  • sin 30 = 1/2, sin 45 about 0.7, sin 60 about 0.87.
  • 1 imp gal = 4.546 L, 1 US gal = 3.785 L, 1 kg = 2.205 lb. kg = L x SG.
  • TE = off x 60 / gone. CA = off x 60 / to go. Direct: TE + CA.
  • Glide path: slope x 100 x nm. 3 degrees: 300 ft/nm, ROD about 5 x GS.
  • Closing: towards = sum, same way = difference.
  • North: low on the left with the wind behind you. High to low: drift right, altimeter over-reads.

Sample questions

5 of the 30 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. Drift is the angle between:

    1. True and magnetic north
    2. Heading and track
    3. Track and the wind direction
    4. TAS and GS
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Drift is how far the wind pushes the track off the heading.

  2. Q2. TAS 120 kt, wind 30 kt at 30 degrees off the nose. The drift is about:

    1. 15 degrees
    2. 7.5 degrees
    3. 13 degrees
    4. 3 degrees
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Max drift 15 x sin 30 (1/2) = 7.5 degrees.

  3. Q3. Runway 09, wind 270/10. The aircraft has:

    1. 10 kt headwind
    2. No wind effect
    3. 10 kt crosswind
    4. 10 kt tailwind
    Show answer

    Answer: D. The wind is from directly behind on runway 09.

  4. Q4. You are 4 nm off track with 80 nm to go. The closing angle is:

    1. 3 degrees
    2. 6 degrees
    3. 9 degrees
    4. 1.5 degrees
    Show answer

    Answer: A. 4 x 60 / 80 = 3 degrees.

  5. Q5. Aircraft A at 480 kt is 40 nm behind B at 400 kt on the same track. A catches B after:

    1. 5 min
    2. 60 min
    3. 30 min
    4. 15 min
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Closing speed 80 kt. 40 / 80 = 0.5 h.
