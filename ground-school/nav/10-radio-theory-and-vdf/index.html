What radio waves are, frequency and wavelength, the frequency bands and what each is used for, how waves travel (ground, sky and space waves), modulation, antennas, and VHF direction finding with its Q codes and accuracy classes.

Radio waves

  • A radio wave is an electromagnetic wave. It travels at the speed of light: about 300,000,000 m/s (162,000 nm per second).
  • Frequency is the number of cycles per second, in hertz (Hz). Wavelength is the length of one cycle.
  • The wave has an electric and a magnetic field at right angles. Its polarisation is the direction of the electric field: vertical or horizontal.
  • Phase is how far through its cycle a wave is, in degrees. Comparing the phase of two signals is how a VOR works.

Wavelength (m) = 300,000,000 / frequency (Hz) = 300 / frequency (MHz)

Memory trick

Each band is ten times the one below, and the wavelength is ten times shorter. VLF, LF, MF, HF, VHF, UHF, SHF.

How radio waves travel

WaveHowUsed by
Surface (ground) waveFollows the curve of the Earth by diffraction. Goes further at lower frequency and over sea (less loss)LF and MF: NDBs
Sky waveRefracted back to Earth by the ionosphereHF long range radio. A nuisance for NDBs at night
Space (direct) waveLine of sight, a straight pathVHF and above: VOR, ILS, DME, radar
  • The ionosphere is a set of layers of charged air, about 60 to 400 km up, made by the sun. The D layer (lowest) fades at night, so sky waves come back further and stronger at night.
  • Skip distance: from the transmitter to where the first sky wave comes down. Dead space: the gap between the end of the ground wave and that point, where nothing is received.
  • HF needs higher frequencies by day and lower by night, because the ionosphere changes.

Line of sight range (nm) = 1.23 x (square root of transmitter height in ft + square root of receiver height in ft)

Worked example: VHF range

  1. Aircraft at 10,000 ft, station at sea level: 1.23 x square root of 10,000 = 1.23 x 100 = 123 nm.
  2. Aircraft at 2,500 ft, station on a 400 ft hill: 1.23 x (50 + 20) = 86 nm.

Modulation and antennas

  • A plain carrier wave carries no information. Modulation adds the information to it.
  • Amplitude modulation (AM): the strength of the carrier changes. Used for VHF voice in aviation.
  • Frequency modulation (FM): the frequency changes. Less noise, used in the VOR's reference signal and the radio altimeter.
  • Pulse modulation: short bursts. Used by radar, DME and SSR.
  • A simple antenna (the dipole) works best when it is half a wavelength long. That is why VHF antennas are short and LF antennas are long wires or loops.
  • A loop antenna is directional: it gives a sharp null when its plane is at right angles to the signal. ADFs use this, plus a sense antenna to tell which way.

VHF direction finding (VDF)

  • A ground station finds the direction of the aircraft's VHF transmission. The aircraft needs only a VHF radio.
  • The pilot asks for a bearing and transmits so the station can measure it. Because it is VHF, range is line of sight.
  • Accuracy is poor over the station, and suffers from site errors and propagation errors.

Memory trick

QDM: M for magnetic, to the station. QDR: R for radial, from the station. QDM and QDR differ by 180. So do QUJ and QTE.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Speed of radio waves 300,000,000 m/s. Wavelength (m) = 300 / MHz.
  • LF and MF: NDB. HF: long range voice. VHF: VOR, LOC, radio, markers. UHF: GP, DME, SSR, GNSS. SHF: weather radar, radio altimeter.
  • Ground wave: LF and MF, further over sea. Sky wave: HF, stronger at night (D layer fades). Space wave: VHF and up, line of sight.
  • Line of sight range = 1.23 x (root H1 + root H2).
  • Voice: AM. Radar, DME, SSR: pulse.
  • Dipole: half a wavelength. Loop: directional null, plus sense antenna.
  • QDM mag to, QDR mag from (radial), QUJ true to, QTE true from.
  • VDF class A 2, B 5, C 10 degrees, D worse.

  1. Q1. Radio waves travel at about:

    1. 300,000 m/s
    2. 300,000,000 m/s
    3. 3,000 m/s
    4. The speed of sound
    Show answer

    Answer: B. The speed of light.

  2. Q2. NDBs operate in the:

    1. VHF band
    2. LF and MF bands
    3. UHF band
    4. SHF band
    Show answer

    Answer: B. About 190 to 1,750 kHz.

  3. Q3. At night, sky waves are received at longer ranges because:

    1. The D layer fades
    2. The sun heats the ionosphere
    3. The E layer disappears
    4. Temperature falls
    Show answer

    Answer: A. The D layer absorbs signals by day and fades at night.

  4. Q4. Aviation VHF voice uses:

    1. FM
    2. No modulation
    3. Pulse modulation
    4. AM
    Show answer

    Answer: D. Amplitude modulation.

  5. Q5. QDR is the:

    1. Magnetic bearing from the station (radial)
    2. Magnetic heading to the station
    3. True bearing from the station
    4. Distance from the station
    Show answer

    Answer: A. R for radial.
