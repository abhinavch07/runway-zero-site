Take-off speeds and distances, what affects take-off and landing, climb speeds and segments, best range and endurance speeds, then flight planning: the navigation log, top of climb and descent, fuel planning, and the point of no return and critical point.

Take-off speeds

SpeedMeaning
VMCGMinimum control speed on the ground with the critical engine failed, using rudder only (no nosewheel steering)
VMCAMinimum control speed in the air with the critical engine failed, with up to 5 degrees of bank towards the live engine
VEFThe speed used in the performance figures for the critical engine failing. Not less than VMCG
V1Decision speed. An engine failure before V1: stop. At or after V1: continue the take-off
VRRotation speed. Not less than V1, nor 1.05 VMCA
VMUMinimum unstick speed: the slowest at which the aircraft can lift off and keep going
VLOFLift-off speed: when the main wheels leave the ground
V2Take-off safety speed: to be reached by the screen height (35 ft dry) with one engine failed. At least 1.2 VS (or 1.13 VSR) and 1.1 VMCA
VMBEMaximum brake energy speed. V1 must not be above it
  • The order is: VMCG, V1, VR, VLOF, V2. V1 is limited: not less than VMCG and VEF, not more than VR and VMBE.
  • TODR: distance to reach 35 ft. ASDR: distance to accelerate to V1 and then stop. A balanced field is when ASDR equals TODR (ASDA equals TODA).
  • Clearway can be counted in TODA (no more than half the TORA). Stopway can be counted in ASDA.

What affects take-off and landing

FactorTake-off and landing distance
More massLonger
Higher aerodrome, higher temperature (higher density altitude)Longer: less engine power and higher TAS for the same IAS
HeadwindShorter. Planning uses only 50% of a reported headwind
TailwindLonger. Planning uses 150% of a reported tailwind
Uphill slopeLonger take-off, shorter landing
Wet, grass or contaminated runwayLonger, especially landing
More flap (take-off)Shorter take-off run, but poorer climb
  • For a jet on a dry runway, the landing distance required is the actual landing distance divided by 0.6 (so the LDA must be 1.67 times the actual distance). A wet runway adds 15%.
  • The second segment of the take-off climb (gear up, take-off flap, take-off thrust, V2) is usually the most limiting. A twin must climb at 2.4% gross gradient there with one engine failed.

Climb, cruise and descent

SpeedGivesUse
VXBest angle of climb: most height for distanceClearing obstacles after take-off
VYBest rate of climb: most height for timeNormal climb
  • VX is always lower than VY. As height increases they come closer and are equal at the absolute ceiling, where the aircraft can no longer climb.
  • A headwind improves the climb angle over the ground but not the rate of climb.
  • Piston aircraft: best endurance at the minimum power speed, best range at the minimum drag (best L/D) speed.
  • Jet aircraft: best endurance at the minimum drag speed, best range at a higher speed (about 1.32 x the minimum drag speed).
  • In a headwind, fly faster than the still-air best range speed. In a tailwind, a little slower.
  • Jets get more range higher up, which is why they cruise in the flight levels.

Descent distance (nm) = height to lose (ft) / descent gradient. Rule of thumb: 3 nm per 1,000 ft

Flight planning and the nav log

  • A navigation log lists each leg: track, distance, safe altitude, planned level, TAS, wind, heading, GS, time and fuel.
  • Choose the level for the semicircular rule, the minimum safe altitude, the winds and the aircraft's best level.
  • Top of climb (TOC) and top of descent (TOD) are worked out from the climb and descent rates and ground speeds.
  • In flight, compare actual times and fuel with the plan at each check point. Revise ETAs and the fuel remaining.
  • Fuel parts: taxi, trip, contingency (5% of trip, min 5 min hold), alternate, final reserve (30 min jet, 45 min piston, at 1,500 ft), additional and extra (see Air Regulations Module 13).

Worked example: Top of descent

  1. Cruise FL 350 to an airport at sea level. Height to lose about 35,000 ft.
  2. At 3 nm per 1,000 ft: start down about 105 nm out.
  3. If the GS in the descent is 300 kt and you want 1,500 ft/min: 35,000 / 1,500 = 23 min, at 5 nm per min = 117 nm.

Point of no return and critical point

Critical point (CP), also called equal time point (ETP)Point of no return (PNR)
MeaningFrom here, it takes the same time to go on to destination as to go backThe furthest point you can fly out and still return to base with the safe fuel
Depends onDistance and ground speeds. Not on fuelFuel endurance and ground speeds
Effect of windMoves into wind: a headwind on the way out moves it closer to the destinationAny wind brings it closer to base (less PNR distance)

Distance to CP = D x H / (O + H)

Time to PNR = E x H / (O + H). Distance to PNR = time to PNR x O

D is the total distance, E the safe endurance (fuel time), O the ground speed out (onwards) and H the ground speed home (back). For an engine failure CP, use the one engine failed TAS to work out O and H.

Worked example: CP and PNR

  1. Distance 1,200 nm. TAS 400 kt, headwind out 50 kt: O = 350, H = 450.
  2. CP from start = 1,200 x 450 / (350 + 450) = 675 nm, beyond halfway because of the headwind out. Time = 675 / 350 = 1 h 56 min.
  3. Safe endurance 5 h. Time to PNR = 5 x 450 / 800 = 2.81 h (2 h 49 min). Distance = 2.81 x 350 = 984 nm.

Memory trick

The CP moves into wind, and a wind of any direction pulls the PNR in towards base.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Order: VMCG, V1, VR, VLOF, V2. V1 between VMCG and VR, not above VMBE.
  • Fail before V1: stop. At or after V1: go.
  • V2 at 35 ft, at least 1.2 VS and 1.1 VMCA. VMCA: up to 5 degrees bank.
  • Balanced field: ASDA = TODA. Clearway in TODA, stopway in ASDA.
  • Planning: 50% headwind, 150% tailwind. Hot, high, heavy, wet, uphill: longer take-off.
  • Jet landing dry: actual distance / 0.6. Wet: +15%.
  • VX angle (obstacles), VY rate. VX < VY, equal at the absolute ceiling.
  • Piston: range at min drag, endurance at min power. Jet: endurance at min drag, range at about 1.32 x that.
  • Descent: about 3 nm per 1,000 ft.
  • CP = D x H / (O + H). Moves into wind. No fuel needed.
  • PNR time = E x H / (O + H). Any wind reduces it.

Sample questions

5 of the 27 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The correct order of take-off speeds is:

    1. V1, VMCG, VR, V2
    2. VMCG, V1, VR, VLOF, V2
    3. VR, V1, V2, VLOF
    4. V2, V1, VR, VMCG
    Show answer

    Answer: B. VMCG is lowest. V2 is reached at the screen height.

  2. Q2. VMCA is worked out with up to how much bank towards the live engine?

    1. 0 degrees
    2. 5 degrees
    3. 15 degrees
    4. 30 degrees
    Show answer

    Answer: B. 5 degrees.

  3. Q3. For take-off planning, a reported tailwind of 4 kt is counted as:

    1. 2 kt
    2. 4 kt
    3. 6 kt
    4. 8 kt
    Show answer

    Answer: C. 150% of a tailwind.

  4. Q4. A headwind during the climb:

    1. Increases the rate of climb
    2. Reduces the rate of climb
    3. Increases the climb angle over the ground
    4. Has no effect
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Rate of climb is through the air and does not change.

  5. Q5. The critical point (equal time point) depends on:

    1. Distance and the ground speeds on and back
    2. Fuel on board
    3. Altitude only
    4. The alternate
    Show answer

    Answer: A. Fuel is not used for the CP.
