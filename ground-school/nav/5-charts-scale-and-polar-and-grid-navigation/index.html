What makes a chart good for navigation, chart scale and how to use it, the Mercator, Lambert and polar stereographic projections and how great circles and rhumb lines look on each, and an introduction to grid navigation near the poles.

What a navigation chart needs

  • A flat chart can never show the curved Earth perfectly. Each projection keeps some properties and gives up others.
  • Orthomorphic (conformal): angles are correct at every point, and scale is the same in all directions at a point. Meridians and parallels cross at right angles. All navigation charts must be orthomorphic, so a measured track is a true direction.
  • Also wanted: constant scale, great circles as straight lines (for planning long routes), rhumb lines as straight lines (for constant tracks), and an easy graticule to plot on. No single chart gives all of these.
TermMeaning
Chart convergencyThe angle between two meridians on the chart. Compare with Earth convergency (Module 2)
Constant of the cone (n)For a conic chart: chart convergency = d.long x n. For Lambert, n = sin (parallel of origin)
Perspective chartMade by projecting from a light source. Most navigation charts are mathematical (non-perspective) instead

Scale

Scale = chart length / Earth distance (same units)

  • Written as a representative fraction, such as 1:500,000: 1 cm on the chart is 500,000 cm (5 km) on the ground.
  • A larger scale (smaller second number) shows more detail over a smaller area. 1:250,000 is larger scale than 1:1,000,000.
  • Scale may also be given as a statement (1 cm = 5 km) or as a scale line on the chart.
  • Useful: 1 nm = 185,200 cm. So at 1:1,000,000, 1 cm is about 5.4 nm.

Worked example: Scale sums

  1. Chart 1:500,000. A track measures 18.52 cm. Earth distance = 18.52 x 500,000 = 9,260,000 cm = 50 nm.
  2. A distance of 60 nm is 11.1 cm long on a chart. Scale = 11.1 / (60 x 185,200) = 1:1,000,000 (about).
  3. On a 1:1,000,000 chart, how long is 100 km? 10,000,000 cm / 1,000,000 = 10 cm.

  • On a Mercator, the scale is within 1% of correct only up to about 8 degrees from the equator. The poles cannot be shown.
  • A Transverse Mercator turns the cylinder to touch a chosen meridian, giving a narrow north-south band of good scale. It is used for some topographical maps.
  • On a Lambert chart, the parallel of origin is about halfway between the standard parallels. Scale error is very small over the whole chart, which is why it is popular.

Polar and grid navigation

  • Near the poles, meridians converge so fast that a straight-line track crosses each one at a different true angle, and the compass is useless because H is weak.
  • Grid navigation lays a set of parallel grid lines over the chart, all lined up with one chosen meridian (the datum meridian). Grid north is the direction of those lines.
  • A straight track then has one constant grid direction, even though its true direction keeps changing.
  • Headings are kept with a gyro (directional gyro or inertial system) set to grid, not with the magnetic compass.
TermMeaning
Grid northThe direction of the grid lines, parallel to the datum meridian
Convergence (grid)The angle between grid north and true north at a place
GrivationThe angle between grid north and magnetic north. It is convergence and variation together
IsogrivA line joining places of equal grivation

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Navigation charts must be orthomorphic: correct angles, scale same in all directions at a point.
  • Scale = chart length / Earth distance. Larger scale = more detail, smaller area.
  • 1 nm = 185,200 cm. At 1:1,000,000, 1 cm is about 5.4 nm.
  • Mercator: cylinder, equator. Scale x sec lat. RL straight. GC concave to equator. Convergency zero.
  • Lambert: cone, two standard parallels. Convergency d.long x sin parallel of origin. GC nearly straight. ICAO 1:1,000,000.
  • Polar stereographic: plane at the pole. Convergency = d.long. GC nearly straight. Scale correct at the pole.
  • Grid: lines parallel to a datum meridian. Grivation = angle from grid north to magnetic north.

Quick revision

The 5 revision slides for this module, as a list.

Chart properties

  • Orthomorphic: correct angles
  • Scale same in all directions at a point
  • GC straight for planning
  • RL straight for constant tracks

Scale

  • Chart length / Earth distance
  • 1:500,000: 1 cm = 5 km
  • 1 nm = 185,200 cm
  • 1:1,000,000: 1 cm about 5.4 nm

Three projections

  • Mercator: RL straight, low latitudes
  • Lambert: GC nearly straight, mid latitudes
  • Polar stereographic: near the poles
  • All orthomorphic

Chart convergency

  • Mercator zero
  • Lambert d.long x sin origin
  • Polar d.long
  • Earth: d.long x sin mean lat

Grid navigation

  • Grid lines parallel to a datum meridian
  • Straight track, one grid direction
  • Gyro, not compass
  • Grivation: grid to magnetic

Sample questions

5 of the 25 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. A chart on which angles are correct and scale is the same in all directions at any point is:

    1. Equal area
    2. Orthomorphic (conformal)
    3. Perspective
    4. Gnomonic
    Show answer

    Answer: B. All navigation charts must be orthomorphic.

  2. Q2. On a Mercator chart, scale is correct:

    1. At the poles
    2. At the equator
    3. At the standard parallels
    4. Everywhere
    Show answer

    Answer: B. The cylinder touches the equator. Scale grows with sec latitude away from it.

  3. Q3. A Lambert conformal conic chart has scale correct on:

    1. The equator
    2. The pole
    3. The two standard parallels
    4. The central meridian
    Show answer

    Answer: C. The cone cuts the Earth along two standard parallels.

  4. Q4. The parallel of origin on a Lambert chart lies:

    1. At the equator
    2. At the pole
    3. Outside the standard parallels
    4. About halfway between the standard parallels
    Show answer

    Answer: D. It is where the cone's constant is taken.

  5. Q5. In grid navigation, grid north is:

    1. Parallel to a chosen datum meridian
    2. The direction of the North Pole
    3. Magnetic north
    4. Compass north
    Show answer

    Answer: A. All grid lines are parallel to the datum meridian.
