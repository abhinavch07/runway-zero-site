Basic principles

  • Hydraulics lets a small force at one place move a large or distant load, smoothly and accurately. Used for landing gear, flaps, flight controls, brakes, steering, spoilers, thrust reversers, doors.
  • Pascal's law: pressure applied to a liquid in a closed container is passed equally in all directions.
  • Liquids are almost incompressible, so movement is passed at once and the jack holds its position.

Pressure = Force / Area, so Force = Pressure x Area

Worked example: Force from a jack

  1. System pressure 3,000 psi, jack piston area 4 sq in
  2. Force = 3,000 x 4 = 12,000 lb
  3. Double the area and the force doubles. The jack moves slower for the same flow.

A jack piston has a smaller area on the rod side, so for the same pressure it pushes harder on extension than it pulls on retraction.

Hydraulic fluids and seals

A good fluid is incompressible, has a high boiling point and low freezing point, keeps a fairly steady viscosity over the temperature range, lubricates, resists fire, does not foam, and does not attack the seals.

Fluids must never be mixed, and the wrong fluid ruins the seals. Skydrol is fire resistant but harms skin, eyes and paint.

Parts of a hydraulic system

PartJob
ReservoirStores fluid, with space for expansion and for fluid returning from jacks. Often pressurised (by bleed air or a bootstrap piston) so the pump gets a steady flow without cavitation or foaming at altitude. Has a stand pipe that keeps a reserve for the emergency pump
PumpsEngine driven (EDP), electric (ACMP), air driven, or hand pumps. A hand pump is usually double acting (delivers on both strokes)
FiltersOn the suction, pressure and return lines. A bypass opens if a filter clogs, and an indicator (pop-up button or light) shows it
Relief valveLimits the maximum pressure. A thermal relief valve lets out the pressure rise from heating in a trapped section
AccumulatorA gas (nitrogen) precharge behind a piston or bladder stores fluid under pressure. Damps pressure surges, gives a reserve for emergencies (such as brakes), and stops the pump cutting in and out too often
Selector valveDirects fluid to one side of the jack and connects the other side to return
Non-return (check) valveLets fluid flow one way only
RestrictorSlows the flow, for example to make the gear or flaps go down slowly
Sequence valveMakes one action finish before the next starts (doors, then gear)
Shuttle valveLets a second (emergency) supply feed a unit, and shuts off the normal line
Priority valveWhen pressure is low, shuts off less important services so the important ones (flight controls, brakes) keep the pressure
Hydraulic fuseShuts the line if there is a big leak (too much flow), saving the rest of the fluid
Jack (actuator)Turns pressure into straight line movement. A hydraulic motor gives turning movement
  • An accumulator's gas precharge is checked with the system pressure off (fluid side empty). The gauge then reads the precharge.
  • A hydraulic lock is fluid trapped on both sides of a jack so it cannot move. Used deliberately to hold the gear or a surface in place. A fault can cause one by accident.
  • Hammering: a valve closing suddenly stops the moving fluid and makes a pressure surge that can be heard and felt. The accumulator damps it.

Pumps and system pressure

Pump typeHow it worksNeeds
Constant volume (fixed delivery)Delivers the same flow whenever it turns (gear pump)An automatic cut-out valve (ACOV) or relief valve to send fluid back to the reservoir when no service is working, so the pump runs off load
Constant pressure (variable volume)A swash plate changes the piston stroke to keep the pressure constant and give only the flow neededNo cut-out needed. Used on modern aircraft at about 3,000 psi (some at 5,000 psi)
  • Open centre systems: fluid circulates back to the reservoir at low pressure when nothing is selected. Closed systems keep full pressure all the time.
  • Case drain: some fluid leaks past the pump pistons to cool and lubricate it, and goes back through a case drain filter. A dirty case drain filter is an early sign of pump wear.
  • Large aircraft have two or three separate systems. Back-ups: an electric pump, a power transfer unit (PTU) that uses one system's pressure to pump another's fluid with no fluid passing between them, a ram air turbine (RAT) dropping into the airflow, and a hand pump for doors.
  • Warnings: low pressure, low quantity, high fluid temperature, filter blockage, pump overheat.

Memory trick

Constant volume pump: needs a cut-out. Constant pressure pump: makes its own pressure, gives only the flow asked for.

Pneumatic systems

  • Most transport aircraft take bleed air from the engine compressors (high volume, low pressure). Other sources: the APU and a ground air cart.
  • Bleed air is used for air conditioning and pressurisation, wing and engine anti-ice, engine starting (air turbine starters), air driven hydraulic pumps, cargo heating and some thrust reversers.
  • Air is taken from a low pressure stage when possible and from a high pressure stage at low engine power, with valves switching between them. A precooler uses fan air to cool very hot bleed air.
  • Duct leak or overheat detection loops along the ducts warn of hot air escaping, which could damage structure or wiring. The affected bleed is then shut off.
  • Light aircraft use small engine driven vacuum (suction) or pressure pumps to drive gyro instruments and de-icing boots.
  • Some aircraft use high pressure stored air or nitrogen bottles for emergency gear lowering or brakes.
  • Compared with hydraulics, air is compressible, so pneumatic jacks are springy and less precise, but leaks do not cause a fire risk or mess.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Pascal: pressure in a closed liquid acts equally in all directions. Force = pressure x area.
  • Mineral fluid: red, synthetic rubber seals. Skydrol: purple, butyl seals. Never mix.
  • Reservoir: pressurised to stop cavitation, space for expansion, stand pipe reserve.
  • Accumulator: nitrogen precharge. Damps surges, stores pressure, emergency reserve. Check precharge with fluid pressure off.
  • Relief valve: max pressure. Thermal relief: heat expansion.
  • Shuttle valve: emergency supply. Priority valve: keeps important services. Fuse: stops a big leak.
  • Constant volume pump needs an ACOV. Constant pressure pump: modern, about 3,000 psi.
  • PTU: one system powers another, no fluid mixes. RAT: emergency power.
  • Hydraulic lock: fluid trapped, jack cannot move. Hammering: pressure surge.
  • Bleed air: air conditioning, pressurisation, anti-ice, engine start.

Sample questions

5 of the 26 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. Pascal's law states that pressure applied to a fluid in a closed container:

    1. Acts only downwards
    2. Is passed equally in all directions
    3. Falls with distance
    4. Depends on the shape of the container
    Show answer

    Answer: B. This is why a small pump pressure can move jacks anywhere in the system.

  2. Q2. Hydraulic reservoirs are often pressurised to:

    1. Raise the system pressure
    2. Stop pump cavitation and foaming at altitude
    3. Cool the fluid
    4. Replace the accumulator
    Show answer

    Answer: B. A positive head of pressure keeps fluid flowing into the pump inlet.

  3. Q3. A thermal relief valve protects against:

    1. Low fluid temperature
    2. Fire
    3. Pressure rise from heating of fluid trapped in a line
    4. Pump failure
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Trapped fluid expands when heated. The valve lets a little out.

  4. Q4. A constant pressure (variable volume) pump:

    1. Keeps the pressure constant and gives only the flow needed
    2. Delivers the same flow all the time
    3. Needs an ACOV
    4. Is only hand operated
    Show answer

    Answer: A. A swash plate changes the stroke of the pistons with demand. This is the pump used on modern aircraft.

  5. Q5. A hand pump in a hydraulic system is usually:

    1. Double acting, delivering fluid on both strokes
    2. Single acting
    3. Electrically driven
    4. Only used in flight
    Show answer

    Answer: A. A double acting pump delivers on both the push and the pull.
