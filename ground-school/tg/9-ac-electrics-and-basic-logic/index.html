AC basics

  • Alternating current (AC) keeps reversing direction. One complete wave is a cycle. Cycles per second is the frequency in hertz (Hz).
  • Large aircraft use 115 V, 400 Hz, three phase AC (200 V between phases). Higher voltage means less current for the same power, so thinner, lighter wires. 400 Hz lets motors and transformers be much smaller and lighter than at 50 Hz.
  • Peak value: the top of the wave. RMS (root mean square) value: the steady DC value that gives the same heating. RMS = 0.707 x peak. AC meters read RMS, so 115 V AC is RMS.
  • Three phase: three outputs 120° apart, from three sets of windings. It gives smoother power and suits large motors.
  • Phase sequence: the order in which the phases peak. Getting it wrong makes three phase motors run backwards.

RMS = 0.707 x peak. Peak = 1.414 x RMS

Memory aid CIVIL: in a Capacitor, I leads V. V leads I in an inductor (L).
EffectWhat it does in an AC circuit
ResistanceOpposes current. Voltage and current stay in phase
Inductance (coils)Opposes changes in current. Current lags the voltage. Its opposition (inductive reactance) rises with frequency
CapacitanceCurrent leads the voltage. Its opposition (capacitive reactance) falls with frequency
Impedance (Z)Total opposition to AC: resistance and reactance together, in ohms
  • Apparent power = volts x amps, in kVA. True (real) power, the power that does work, is in kW. The difference is reactive power (kVAR), which flows back and forth.
  • Power factor = true power / apparent power = kW / kVA. For a pure resistance it is 1. Aircraft generators are rated in kVA.

AC generators and constant frequency

Frequency (Hz) = rpm x number of pole pairs / 60

Worked example: Generator with 4 poles at 12,000 rpm

  1. 4 poles = 2 pole pairs
  2. f = 12,000 x 2 / 60 = 400 Hz
  • Frequency depends on speed. Engine speed varies, so a constant speed drive (CSD) between the engine gearbox and the generator turns the generator at a constant rpm to give 400 Hz. It works hydraulically with its own oil.
  • An integrated drive generator (IDG) is a CSD and generator in one case.
  • A CSD (or IDG) can be disconnected in flight by the crew if it overheats or loses oil pressure. It usually cannot be reconnected in flight, only on the ground.
  • Some newer aircraft use variable frequency generators driven straight from the engine, with equipment designed to accept the changing frequency.
  • A brushless generator has no brushes or slip rings to wear or spark: a permanent magnet generator feeds the exciter, whose output is rectified by diodes on the rotor to feed the main field. The main output is taken from the stator.
  • Voltage is controlled by changing the field current (the voltage regulator in the generator control unit, GCU).

Generator control and paralleling

  • The GCU regulates voltage and protects against over and under voltage, over and under frequency and faults by opening the generator control relay (field) and generator breaker.
  • Split bus system: each generator feeds its own bus. Generators are not paralleled. A bus tie breaker joins buses if a generator fails. Common on modern twins.
  • Parallel system: all generators feed a common tie bus and share the load. Before connecting, a generator's voltage, frequency and phase sequence must match, and it must be in phase.
  • Load sharing in a parallel system: real load (kW) is shared by adjusting the CSD speed (frequency). Reactive load (kVAR) is shared by adjusting the field excitation (voltage).
  • Ground power units plug in through an external power socket. The system checks voltage, frequency and phase before connecting it.

  • A transformer's voltages are in the ratio of the turns on its windings. More turns on the secondary: step up. Fewer: step down. Power stays about the same, so stepping up the voltage steps down the current.
  • A transformer cannot work on steady DC, because induction needs a changing field.

Secondary volts / primary volts = secondary turns / primary turns

  • Emergency AC sources: the APU generator, a ram air turbine (RAT) generator, and the battery through a static inverter for essential loads.

Basic logic

Computers work with binary signals: 1 (on, high voltage) or 0 (off). Logic gates combine signals. They are used in warning and control systems, for example: warn if the gear is NOT down AND the throttles are closed.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Aircraft AC: 115 V, 400 Hz, three phase (200 V between phases). 400 Hz: lighter motors and transformers.
  • RMS = 0.707 x peak. Meters read RMS.
  • Inductor: current lags. Capacitor: current leads (CIVIL).
  • Generators rated in kVA. Power factor = kW / kVA.
  • f = rpm x pole pairs / 60.
  • CSD keeps generator speed and frequency constant. IDG = CSD + generator.
  • CSD disconnected in flight: cannot be reconnected in flight.
  • Brushless: PMG, exciter, rotating diodes, main generator.
  • Paralleling: match voltage, frequency, phase sequence, and be in phase.
  • Real load shared by CSD speed. Reactive load by excitation.
  • Transformer: AC only, turns ratio. TRU: AC to DC. Inverter: DC to AC.
  • AND: all 1. OR: any 1. NOT: inverts. NAND: 0 only when all 1. NOR: 1 only when all 0. XOR: inputs differ.

Sample questions

5 of the 26 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The normal AC supply of a large transport aircraft is:

    1. 28 V, 50 Hz, single phase
    2. 115 V, 400 Hz, three phase
    3. 230 V, 50 Hz, single phase
    4. 14 V DC
    Show answer

    Answer: B. 115 V phase to neutral, 200 V between phases, at 400 Hz.

  2. Q2. As frequency rises, inductive reactance:

    1. Falls
    2. Rises
    3. Stays the same
    4. Becomes zero
    Show answer

    Answer: B. A coil opposes faster changes more strongly. Capacitive reactance does the opposite.

  3. Q3. An integrated drive generator (IDG) is:

    1. A generator with a battery
    2. A TRU
    3. A CSD and a generator in one unit
    4. An APU
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Combining them saves weight and space.

  4. Q4. In a parallel AC system, reactive load (kVAR) sharing is controlled by adjusting the:

    1. CSD speed
    2. Bus tie breaker
    3. Generator field excitation
    4. Frequency meter
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Excitation sets the generated voltage, which controls reactive current.

  5. Q5. A transformer has 100 turns on the primary and 20 on the secondary. With 115 V on the primary, the secondary gives:

    1. 575 V
    2. 5 V
    3. 115 V
    4. 23 V
    Show answer

    Answer: D. Volts are in the turns ratio: 115 x 20 / 100 = 23 V.
