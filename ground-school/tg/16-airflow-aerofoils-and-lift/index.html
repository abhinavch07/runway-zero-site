Air and airflow

  • Air has mass, is compressible (at high speed), flows easily and has low viscosity.
  • Density (ρ) is mass per unit volume. It falls with higher altitude, higher temperature and higher humidity (moist air is lighter). Lower density means less lift and thrust for the same TAS, so longer take-offs.
  • ISA sea level: 15°C, 1013.25 hPa, density 1.225 kg/m³.
  • Streamline (laminar) flow: smooth layers that do not mix. Turbulent flow: eddies and mixing.
  • Continuity: in a steady flow through a tube, mass flow is the same everywhere. Where the tube narrows, the air speeds up.

Continuity (low speed): A1 x V1 = A2 x V2

  • Bernoulli's theorem: in a steady streamline flow, static pressure + dynamic pressure = total pressure, which stays constant. Where the air speeds up, its static pressure falls.
  • Dynamic pressure (q) = ½ρV². It is the pressure from the air's movement, and what the ASI measures (pitot minus static).
  • A venturi (narrow throat) shows both laws: speed up, pressure down. Used in carburettors and to drive old gyro instruments.

Static pressure + ½ρV² = total (pitot) pressure = constant

Remember ICE-T. At low speed and low level, IAS, CAS and EAS are about the same.

Lift

  • An aerofoil at a positive AoA speeds up the air over its upper surface, so the pressure there falls below the outside pressure. The pressure below is slightly higher. Most lift comes from the suction on top.
  • The airflow is also turned downwards (downwash) behind the wing, and upwash comes in front of it. Pushing air down gives an equal force up.
  • Lift acts at 90° to the relative airflow. Drag acts parallel to it. Their total is the total reaction.

Lift = CL x ½ρV² x S

  • CL (lift coefficient) depends on the shape of the aerofoil and the angle of attack. ½ρV² is the dynamic pressure, so the IAS. S is wing area.
  • Lift is proportional to V²: double the speed, four times the lift (same AoA). Three times the speed: nine times.
  • At a constant IAS (dynamic pressure), the same AoA gives the same lift at any altitude. The TAS is higher at altitude, but the density is lower.
  • In level flight, lift = weight. To fly slower, the pilot must raise the AoA (more CL). To fly faster, lower it.
  • Heavier aircraft need more lift, so a higher speed for the same AoA (and a higher stall speed).

For the same lift: V2 = V1 x √(CL1 / CL2). At the same CL, V2 = V1 x √(W2 / W1)

The lift curve and centre of pressure

  • CL rises in a straight line with AoA over the normal range, up to CLmax at the critical (stalling) angle, about 15 to 16° for a typical wing. Beyond it the flow separates and lift falls (Module 18).
  • A symmetrical aerofoil gives no lift at 0° AoA. A cambered aerofoil gives lift at 0° and has its zero lift AoA slightly negative (about -2 to -4°). More camber means more CL at a given AoA and a higher CLmax.
  • The centre of pressure (CP) is the point on the chord where the total lift acts. On a cambered aerofoil, as the AoA increases the CP moves forward, up to the stall, then moves back suddenly at the stall.
  • The aerodynamic centre (AC) is the point, about 25% of the chord back from the leading edge (subsonic), about which the pitching moment does not change with AoA. That makes it a better reference than the moving CP.
  • A cambered aerofoil has a nose down pitching moment about its aerodynamic centre. The tailplane balances it.

Lift/drag (L/D) ratio: how much lift a wing gives for each unit of drag. It is highest at one AoA, about 4° on a typical wing. Flying there gives the least drag and the best glide (Module 17).

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Density falls with altitude, temperature and humidity.
  • Continuity: narrower, faster. Bernoulli: faster, lower static pressure. Static + dynamic = constant.
  • Dynamic pressure q = ½ρV². The ASI measures it.
  • IAS, CAS (instrument and position error), EAS (compressibility), TAS (density).
  • AoA: chord to relative airflow. Incidence: chord to longitudinal axis, fixed.
  • Aspect ratio = span² / area. High AR: less induced drag.
  • Washout: tip at lower incidence, root stalls first.
  • L = CL ½ρV² S. Double the speed: four times the lift.
  • Lift at 90° to the relative airflow. Drag parallel to it.
  • Fly slower in level flight: raise the AoA.
  • CLmax at the critical angle, about 15 to 16°.
  • Symmetrical: no lift at 0°. Cambered: lift at 0°, zero lift at a small negative AoA.
  • CP moves forward as AoA increases (until the stall). AC at about 25% chord.
  • Best L/D at about 4° AoA.

Sample questions

5 of the 24 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. Air density decreases when:

    1. Temperature falls
    2. Humidity increases
    3. Pressure increases
    4. Altitude decreases
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Water vapour is lighter than dry air, so humid air is less dense. Density also falls with height and heat.

  2. Q2. EAS is CAS corrected for:

    1. Instrument error
    2. Position error
    3. Compressibility
    4. Density
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Compressibility matters at high speeds and altitudes. TAS is EAS corrected for density.

  3. Q3. Washout on a wing means the:

    1. Wing tip has a lower angle of incidence than the root
    2. Wing tip has a higher angle of incidence than the root
    3. Wing has no twist
    4. Wing is swept back
    Show answer

    Answer: A. The twist makes the root reach the stalling angle first, keeping the ailerons working.

  4. Q4. In the lift formula L = CL ½ρV² S, CL depends mainly on:

    1. Air density
    2. Speed
    3. Aerofoil shape and angle of attack
    4. Wing area
    Show answer

    Answer: C. The other terms are the dynamic pressure and the area.

  5. Q5. The aerodynamic centre of a subsonic aerofoil is about:

    1. 25% of the chord from the leading edge
    2. At the leading edge
    3. 50% of the chord
    4. At the trailing edge
    Show answer

    Answer: A. About it the pitching moment does not change with AoA.
