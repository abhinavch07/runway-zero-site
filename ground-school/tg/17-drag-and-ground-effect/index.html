Parasite drag (form, skin friction, interference) and how it grows with speed, induced drag from wing tip vortices and what affects it, winglets, the total drag curve, minimum drag speed and best lift/drag ratio, speed stability, minimum power speed, the effect of weight, wake turbulence, and ground effect.

Parasite drag

Drag is the force that opposes the aircraft's motion through the air, acting parallel to the relative airflow. Total drag = parasite drag + induced drag.

Form drag plus skin friction drag together are called profile drag.
Parasite drag typeCauseReduced by
Form (pressure) dragThe pressure difference from front to back of a body, made worse by flow separation behind itStreamlining (fairings, spinners)
Skin friction dragThe air's viscosity in the boundary layer rubbing on the surface. More with a turbulent boundary layer and a rough surfaceSmooth, clean surfaces, flush rivets, keeping the flow laminar
Interference dragAirflows meeting at junctions, such as wing and fuselage, strut and wingFillets and fairings at the joins
  • Parasite drag rises with the square of the speed (V²). Double the speed, four times the parasite drag.
  • Lowering the gear or flaps, or opening cowl flaps, adds a lot of parasite drag.

Induced drag

  • Induced drag is the price of making lift. The high pressure below the wing flows round the tips to the low pressure above, so the air flows outwards below the wing and inwards above it. Behind the wing these flows roll up into wing tip vortices.
  • The vortices add downwash behind the wing. That tilts the relative airflow at the wing down a little, so the effective angle of attack is less and the lift is tilted back. The backward part of the lift is induced drag.
  • Induced drag is greatest at high angles of attack: at low speed, high weight, and in turns (more lift needed). It varies with 1/V²: double the speed, a quarter of the induced drag.
  • It is less with a high aspect ratio (long thin wings, as on gliders), an elliptical lift spread along the span, and winglets, wing fences or tip tanks, which weaken the tip vortices.
  • Flaps add a lot of drag. At a given speed they let the wing fly at a lower angle of attack.

The total drag curve

  • VMD (minimum drag speed): the speed at which total drag is least. Here induced drag = parasite drag.
  • At VMD the lift/drag ratio is best. Flying at VMD gives the best glide range (power off), and for a jet, the best endurance.
  • VMP (minimum power speed) is slower than VMD. It gives the least power required, so the best endurance for a propeller aircraft and the least rate of descent in a glide.
  • Speed stability: above VMD, if the speed drops, drag falls, so the aircraft tends to return to its speed (stable). Below VMD (the back of the drag curve), a speed drop increases drag, so the aircraft slows further unless the pilot adds power (speed unstable). This matters on the final approach of jets.
  • More weight moves the drag curve up and to the right: more drag and a higher VMD. The best L/D ratio stays the same (at the same AoA), but at a higher speed.
  • With flaps or gear down, parasite drag rises, so VMD becomes lower and the minimum drag is higher.
  • Altitude does not change VMD as an EAS (IAS), but its TAS is higher, which is why jets fly high.

Worked example: Speed and drag

  1. At 150 kt, parasite drag = 1,000 N and induced drag = 1,000 N (this is VMD)
  2. At 300 kt: parasite x 4 = 4,000 N, induced / 4 = 250 N
  3. Total drag goes from 2,000 N to 4,250 N

Wake turbulence

  • Wing tip vortices from a large aircraft are a hazard to aircraft behind it. They are strongest behind an aircraft that is heavy, clean (flaps up) and slow, so at a high angle of attack, as on take-off and landing.
  • Vortices sink at about 300 to 500 ft per minute and level off about 500 to 900 ft below the flight path. They drift with the wind.
  • Near the ground they spread outwards at about 5 kt. A light crosswind of 3 to 5 kt can hold the upwind vortex over the runway. They last longest in calm air.
  • Avoid by staying above the larger aircraft's flight path, landing beyond its touchdown point, and taking off before its rotation point. ATC applies wake turbulence separation (Air Regulations Module 10).

Ground effect

  • When the wing is within about one wingspan of the ground (strongly within half a span), the ground restricts the downwash and the tip vortices.
  • Effects: induced drag falls, the effective angle of attack rises, so lift increases at a given AoA. The stalling angle of attack is a little lower.
  • On landing, the aircraft tends to float in the flare, especially if too fast.
  • On take-off, an aircraft may lift off below the proper speed, then sink or fail to climb when it climbs out of ground effect, as induced drag rises again. Do not climb steeply until the speed is safe.
  • Less downwash at the tail changes the trim: entering ground effect tends to give a nose down change, leaving it a nose up change. Static pressure near the ground also changes, so the ASI and altimeter may read wrongly.
  • Low wing aircraft feel ground effect more than high wing aircraft.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Total drag = parasite + induced. Profile = form + skin friction.
  • Parasite drag goes with V². Induced drag with 1/V².
  • Interference drag: junctions. Cure: fillets.
  • Induced drag: tip vortices, downwash. Outwards below, inwards above.
  • Induced drag up with weight, low speed, high AoA, turns. Down with high aspect ratio, elliptical, winglets.
  • VMD: induced = parasite, best L/D, best glide range.
  • VMP below VMD: least power, best prop endurance, least sink rate.
  • Below VMD: speed unstable (back of the drag curve).
  • More weight: VMD higher, drag higher, same best L/D ratio.
  • Wake: worst behind heavy, clean, slow. Sink 300 to 500 fpm. Light crosswind holds upwind vortex on runway.
  • Ground effect (within one span): less induced drag, more lift, lower stalling angle, float.

  • Less induced drag, more lift
  • Float on landing

Sample questions

5 of the 25 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. Profile drag is made up of:

    1. Induced drag and form drag
    2. Form drag and skin friction drag
    3. Interference drag and induced drag
    4. Wave drag only
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Profile drag = form (pressure) drag + skin friction drag.

  2. Q2. On a lifting wing, the spanwise flow is:

    1. Outwards on the top surface and inwards underneath
    2. Inwards on the top surface and outwards underneath
    3. Inwards on both surfaces
    4. There is no spanwise flow
    Show answer

    Answer: B. Air below moves out towards the tip, air above moves in towards the root.

  3. Q3. At the minimum drag speed (VMD):

    1. Induced drag equals parasite drag
    2. Parasite drag is zero
    3. Induced drag is greatest
    4. The aircraft is stalled
    Show answer

    Answer: A. The two curves cross where total drag is least.

  4. Q4. If the aircraft's weight increases, VMD:

    1. Increases
    2. Decreases
    3. Stays the same
    4. Becomes zero
    Show answer

    Answer: A. More weight needs more lift, so the drag curve moves up and to the right.

  5. Q5. In ground effect:

    1. Induced drag increases
    2. The aircraft cannot fly
    3. Parasite drag doubles
    4. Induced drag decreases and lift increases at a given AoA
    Show answer

    Answer: D. The ground limits the downwash and the tip vortices.
