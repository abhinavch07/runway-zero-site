Speed of sound and Mach number

  • The speed of sound (a) depends only on the air temperature: the colder the air, the slower sound travels. Pressure does not change it.
  • At ISA sea level (15°C) it is about 661 kt. It falls with height in the troposphere and stays at about 573 kt in the lower stratosphere (-56.5°C).

Speed of sound (kt) = 38.95 x √(T in kelvin). Mach number = TAS / speed of sound

Worked example: TAS 480 kt at -56.5°C

  1. T = 273 - 56.5 = 216.5 K. √216.5 = 14.71
  2. a = 38.95 x 14.71 = 573 kt
  3. M = 480 / 573 = 0.84
RangeMach number
SubsonicBelow about 0.75
TransonicAbout 0.75 to 1.2: some airflow is subsonic, some supersonic
SupersonicAbout 1.2 to 5
HypersonicAbove 5
  • Below about M 0.4 air behaves as if it does not compress. Above that, compressibility matters: pressure waves from the aircraft cannot move ahead fast enough to warn the air in front.
  • Climbing at a constant IAS, the TAS rises and the speed of sound falls, so the Mach number increases. Climbing at a constant Mach, the IAS falls. Jets climb at a set IAS, then change to a set Mach at the crossover altitude.
  • In a descent at constant Mach, the IAS increases, so a crew must watch for VMO.

Critical Mach number and shock waves

  • Air speeds up over the wing's upper surface, so it reaches Mach 1 locally while the aircraft is still subsonic.
  • Critical Mach number (Mcrit): the free stream Mach number at which the airflow first reaches M 1.0 somewhere on the aircraft (usually the upper surface of the wing).
  • Above Mcrit, a pocket of supersonic flow forms, ending in a normal shock wave. Across a normal shock the airflow slows to subsonic, and its pressure, temperature and density rise suddenly. Energy is lost: wave drag.
  • The sudden pressure rise behind the shock makes the boundary layer separate: shock induced separation. This gives high speed (Mach) buffet, loss of lift (shock stall), and a big rise in drag.
  • Drag divergence Mach number: a little above Mcrit, where the drag starts to rise very steeply.
  • As Mach rises, the shock moves back along the wing and a shock forms on the lower surface too. At M 1 and above, a bow wave stands ahead of the aircraft.
EffectWhat happens
Mach tuck (tuck under)The shock moves back, so the CP moves aft, and the wing gives less downwash on the tail. The nose drops, which raises the speed further. Corrected automatically by Mach trim
Control problemsShocks on the control surfaces reduce their effect, and can cause aileron buzz. Powered controls and spoilers help
BuffetSeparated flow shakes the aircraft: a warning to slow down
Instrument errorsShocks near the static ports cause position errors

Raising the critical Mach number

MethodWhy it works
Thin aerofoils (low thickness / chord ratio)The air speeds up less over the top, so it reaches M 1 later
SweepbackOnly the part of the airflow at right angles to the leading edge sets the pressure pattern. That part is M x cos(sweep), so the wing behaves as if it were flying slower
Supercritical aerofoilFlat top, curved rear underside. The supersonic region is weaker and the shock is weaker and further back, so drag rises later. Also allows a thicker wing
Area ruleThe total cross section of the aircraft (fuselage plus wings) should change smoothly from nose to tail. Fuselages are 'waisted' where the wing joins
Vortex generatorsRe-energise the boundary layer behind the shock, reducing separation

Worked example: Effect of sweep

  1. Flight Mach 0.80, wing swept 30°
  2. Normal component = 0.80 x cos 30° = 0.80 x 0.866 = 0.69
  3. The wing's pressure pattern is like flying at M 0.69.
  • Sweepback disadvantages: lower CLmax (higher stall speed, needs powerful flaps and slats), tip stall and pitch up, more Dutch roll tendency, spanwise flow, and a heavier structure.

Speed limits and the coffin corner

  • VMO is the maximum operating speed as an IAS (structural and flutter limit, matters lower down). MMO is the maximum operating Mach number (compressibility limit, matters higher up). An overspeed warning (clacker) sounds when either is passed.
  • Low speed buffet is the onset of the stall. High speed buffet is Mach buffet. As altitude increases, the stall speed as a Mach number rises, and the Mach buffet speed falls, so the two close together.
  • Where they meet is the aerodynamic ceiling, often called the coffin corner. There, slowing down stalls the aircraft and speeding up gives Mach buffet.
  • Buffet margin: the cruise level is chosen so the aircraft can pull at least 1.3 g (about 40° bank) before buffet. More weight or a turn (higher load factor) lowers the buffet boundary altitude.
  • Kinetic (aerodynamic) heating: air brought to rest on the aircraft warms up. The thermometer reads total air temperature (TAT), which is higher than the true outside air (SAT or static air temperature). The rise is about (TAS / 100)² in °C. At very high speeds it limits the materials.

Remember for the exam

Remember for the exam

  • Speed of sound depends only on temperature. a = 38.95 √T(K). ISA SL 661 kt.
  • Mach = TAS / a.
  • Transonic: about 0.75 to 1.2.
  • Constant IAS climb: Mach rises. Constant Mach climb: IAS falls.
  • Mcrit: free stream Mach when the flow first reaches M 1 somewhere.
  • Across a normal shock: speed falls to subsonic, pressure, temperature, density rise.
  • Shock induced separation: Mach buffet, shock stall, drag rise.
  • Mach tuck: CP moves aft, nose drops. Cure: Mach trim.
  • Raise Mcrit: thin wings, sweep (M x cos sweep), supercritical aerofoils, area rule.
  • Sweep downsides: lower CLmax, tip stall, pitch up, Dutch roll.
  • VMO: IAS limit. MMO: Mach limit.
  • Coffin corner: low and high speed buffet meet. 1.3 g buffet margin.
  • TAT is higher than SAT (kinetic heating).

Sample questions

5 of the 24 practice questions for this module. Tap "Show answer" to check yourself.

  1. Q1. The speed of sound in air depends only on the air's:

    1. Pressure
    2. Density
    3. Temperature
    4. Humidity
    Show answer

    Answer: C. a = 38.95 √T. Pressure and density cancel out.

  2. Q2. An aircraft climbs at a constant Mach number. Its IAS:

    1. Increases
    2. Decreases
    3. Stays the same
    4. First increases then decreases
    Show answer

    Answer: B. At a fixed Mach, the TAS changes little but density falls, so IAS falls.

  3. Q3. Mach tuck is:

    1. A nose up pitch at high Mach
    2. A wing drop at the stall
    3. A nose down pitch as the shock wave and centre of pressure move aft
    4. A yaw from an engine failure
    Show answer

    Answer: C. Less downwash on the tail adds to it. Mach trim corrects it.

  4. Q4. The area rule says that:

    1. The aircraft's total cross sectional area should change smoothly from nose to tail
    2. Wing area should be as large as possible
    3. The tail should be larger than the wing
    4. Sweep should be 45°
    Show answer

    Answer: A. Smooth changes in area reduce wave drag near Mach 1.

  5. Q5. The coffin corner is where:

    1. The low speed and high speed buffet boundaries meet
    2. Fuel runs out
    3. VMO and VREF are equal
    4. The engines flame out
    Show answer

    Answer: A. It is the aerodynamic ceiling. Slower stalls, faster gives Mach buffet.
