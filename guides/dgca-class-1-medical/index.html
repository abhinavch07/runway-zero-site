DGCA Class 1 medical: where, how and how long it lasts

Every commercial pilot in India must hold a DGCA Class 1 medical. Here is how it fits with the Class 2, where to do it, what to expect and how long it lasts.

Class 2 and Class 1

MedicalNeeded forValid for
Class 2Student Pilot Licence, PPL, radio licence. You need it to start flying.2 years up to age 50, then 1 year
Class 1CPL and ATPL12 months up to age 60, then 6 months. Only 6 months after 40 for single-crew commercial flying

A first medical runs from the date of the exam. A renewal done in the 45 days before expiry runs on from the old expiry date, so no days are lost.

The order to do it in

  1. Register on eGCA.

    Medicals are booked and recorded on your eGCA profile.

  2. Class 2 with a DGCA empanelled Class 2 examiner.

    The DGCA website lists the examiners. Contact one near you for an appointment.

  3. Class 1 initial.

    Once your Class 2 assessment is issued, book the Class 1 initial at an approved centre (below). Do it before paying big flying school fees.

  4. Renew every year.

    Renewals are done by DGCA empanelled Class 1 examiners or the approved centres. If your last Class 1 is more than two years old, you need a fresh initial (re-initial) instead.

Where to do the Class 1 initial

  • Indian Air Force boarding centres: Institute of Aerospace Medicine (IAM), Bengaluru, and Air Force Central Medical Establishment (AFCME), New Delhi.
  • DGCA empanelled civil hospitals. In October 2025 the DGCA approved 10 new Aeromedical Evaluation Centres that can do all DGCA medicals, Class 1 included: Apollo hospitals in New Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai and Indore, Nanavati and VM Medical Care Centre in Mumbai, Ruby Hall Clinic in Pune, and Max and Medanta in the Delhi area.

Check before you pay

The list of centres and examiners changes. Check the current DGCA list (linked on our home page) and your eGCA account before booking.

What is checked

The Class 1 is a full check, often spread over more than one day. Expect a doctor's examination, eyes (including colour vision), ears and hearing, heart (ECG), blood and urine tests and a chest X-ray, with more tests depending on your age and history. The exact list depends on the centre and on whether it is an initial or a renewal.

  • Carry your Class 2 assessment, ID, eGCA details and any glasses or lenses you use.
  • Tell the examiner about past illnesses and medicines. Hiding them can cost you the licence later.
  • If you have a known condition, see an aviation medical examiner for advice before you book.

If you fall ill later

After any illness or injury that stops you flying for 15 days or more, you may not fly again until a fresh medical finds you fit, and the DGCA must be told. More in our Air Regulations notes, Module 4.

Remember

In short

  • Class 2 first, to start flying. Class 1 for the CPL.
  • Class 1 initial at IAM Bengaluru, AFCME New Delhi or a DGCA empanelled hospital.
  • Class 1 lasts 12 months (6 after 60, or after 40 for single-crew commercial).
  • Re-initial if your last Class 1 is more than two years old.

Questions

Can I do the Class 1 before the Class 2?

The usual order is Class 2 first, then the Class 1 initial. Your Class 2 assessment is asked for when you go for the Class 1.

Do I need glasses-free eyesight?

Not always. Many vision problems are allowed if corrected within the limits. Ask an aviation medical examiner about your own case before you book.

How long is a Class 1 medical valid?

12 months up to age 60, then 6 months. It is 6 months after age 40 for single-crew commercial flying with passengers or for instructing.
