The path to a Commercial Pilot Licence (CPL) in India, in the order most cadets do it: what you need to start, the medicals, the DGCA exams, the flying, and roughly what it costs and how long it takes.

Who can become a pilot

For a CPL from the DGCA you need:

NeedFor a CPL
AgeAt least 18 when the licence is issued. You can start training earlier: the Student Pilot Licence is from 16.
EducationClass 12 with Physics and Mathematics, from a recognised board.
MedicalA Class 1 medical assessment from the DGCA. Training starts on a Class 2.
EnglishAt least ICAO Level 4 English for radio telephony.
Upper ageThere is no upper age to register for the DGCA exams.

If you did not take Physics and Maths in Class 12, many students pass the two subjects later through an open board such as NIOS. Check that the board is recognised before you rely on it.

The steps, in order

  1. Finish Class 12 with Physics and Maths.

    Keep your Class 10 and 12 mark sheets and pass certificates ready. You will upload them more than once.

  2. Register on eGCA and get a Class 2 medical.

    eGCA is the DGCA's online system for licences and medicals. A DGCA empanelled Class 2 examiner does the Class 2 medical. You need it to start flying.

  3. Do your Class 1 medical early.

    It is the medical a commercial pilot must hold. Do it before you pay large flying school fees, so you know you are fit. See the Class 1 medical guide.

  4. Get your DGCA computer number.

    Apply on the Pariksha portal. It is your ID for every DGCA exam, for life. See the DGCA exams guide.

  5. Pass the DGCA theory papers.

    Air Regulations, Air Navigation, Meteorology, Technical General and Technical Specific, plus the RTR(A) radio exam. Many cadets clear most papers before or during flying training. Our free Ground School covers the syllabus.

  6. Join a DGCA approved flying school (FTO).

    You fly at least 200 hours, with solo, cross-country, night and instrument time (see the table below), then pass the skill tests.

  7. Apply for the CPL on eGCA.

    With the exams, hours, skill tests, Class 1 medical, English proficiency and radio licence in place.

  8. After the CPL.

    To fly for an airline you usually add a multi-engine instrument rating and a type rating on an airliner such as the A320 or B737, or join an airline cadet programme that includes them.

Flying hours for the CPL

FlyingAt least
Total flight time200 hours
As pilot in command (PIC)100 hours, with 15 in the 6 months before you apply
Cross-country as PIC20 hours, including one flight of 300 nm with full stop landings at two different aerodromes
Instrument time10 hours, no more than 5 on an approved simulator
Night5 hours, with 10 take-offs and 10 landings as PIC in the 6 months before you apply

These are the minimums in the 2026 rules (First Schedule, Section J). More detail is in our Air Regulations notes, Module 4.

How much it costs

Costs depend a lot on the flying school, the aircraft, fuel prices and how quickly you finish. As of November 2026, typical figures quoted for training in India are:

PartTypical range
CPL in India, all inabout ₹45 lakh to ₹70 lakh
Flying (the 200 hours)the biggest part, usually well over half the total
DGCA exam fee₹2,500 per paper for the regular online exam, not refundable
Type rating after the CPLabout ₹15 lakh to ₹25 lakh more

Check before you pay

These are rough ranges, not quotes. Ask each school for a written breakdown: hourly rate, what happens if weather or aircraft delays add hours, simulator, exam and accommodation costs.

How long it takes

Most cadets take about 18 months to 2 years from starting to holding a CPL. Weather, aircraft availability, medical delays and failed papers are what usually stretch it. Clearing the theory papers early saves the most time.

Remember

In short

  • Class 12 with Physics and Maths, age 18 for the licence.
  • Class 2 medical to start, Class 1 medical for the CPL. Do the Class 1 early.
  • Computer number on Pariksha, then the DGCA papers and RTR(A).
  • 200 hours of flying at a DGCA approved school.
  • Roughly ₹45 to 70 lakh in India, about 18 months to 2 years.

Questions

Can I become a pilot without Maths in Class 12?

Not directly. The CPL needs Class 12 with Physics and Mathematics. Many students pass these two subjects later through a recognised open board, then apply.

Do I need the Class 1 medical before I start flying?

You start on a Class 2. But the CPL needs a Class 1, so it is wise to pass the Class 1 before paying for flying, in case something is found.

Is there an age limit?

The CPL is from 18. There is no upper age to register for the DGCA exams, but airlines have their own hiring rules.

Can I study for the DGCA exams before joining a flying school?

Yes. Many cadets clear most papers first. You can use our free notes, practice questions and mock tests in Ground School.
